\section{PRELIMINARIES \& PROBLEM FORMULATION} \label{background: section}
Image features throughout this paper are expressed in a \emph{virtual camera frame} $\mathcal{V}$, obtained from the camera frame $\mathcal{C}$ by rotating out the UAV roll $\phi$ and pitch $\theta$ so that its image plane is always parallel to the inertial horizontal plane (Fig.~\ref{frames planes: figure}). This abstraction \cite{fink2017, xie2016_2} decouples the translational image-feature dynamics from the rotational motion of the underactuated quadrotor; the pinhole projection on $\mathcal{V}$ and the closed-form virtual-feature map are given in Section~S1 of the supplement.

\subsection{Dynamics of Quadrotor-Target System} \label{quadrotor dynamics: section}
Let $\mathcal{B}=\{O_{\text{b}},X_{\text{b}},Y_{\text{b}},Z_{\text{b}}\}$ denote the body-fixed frame of UAV in forward-right-down configuration, with linear and angular velocities $\,^\mathcal{B}\boldsymbol{v}_\text{b}$ and $\,^\mathcal{B}\boldsymbol{\omega}_\text{b}$. From the Newton--Euler formulation, the UAV flight dynamics read
\begin{equation}
\label{quadrotor dynamics: equation}
    \begin{bmatrix} ^\mathcal{B} \dot{\boldsymbol{v}}_{\text{b}} \\ ^\mathcal{B} \dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{\text{b}} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} B_v & O_{3 \times 3} \\ O_{3 \times 3} & B_{\omega} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} ^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{F}_u \\ ^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{\tau}_u \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{c}_v \\ \boldsymbol{c}_{\omega} \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} ^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{F_{\text{d}}} \\ ^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{\tau_{\text{d}}} \end{bmatrix}
 \end{equation}
 where
 \begin{align*}
     B_v &= \frac{1}{m} I_{3 \times 3}, & \boldsymbol{c}_v &= \frac{1}{m} \,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{F_{\text{g}}} - \,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{b}} \times \,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{v}_{\text{b}} \\ B_{\omega} &= J^{-1}, & \boldsymbol{c}_{\omega} &= - J^{-1}\left(\,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{b}} \times J \,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{b}} \right)
 \end{align*}
Here $m$ is the body mass, and $J$ is the body inertia matrix. The control force $\,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{F}_u$ is defined as $\,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{F}_u = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & \,^\mathcal{B} T_u\end{bmatrix}^\top $ with $\,^\mathcal{B} T_u$ being the total rotor thrust. $\,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{F_{\text{g}}}$ is the gravitational force and $\,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{\tau}_u$ is the control torque. The unknown bounded disturbances $\,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{F_{\text{d}}}$ and $\,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{\tau_{\text{d}}}$ aggregate wind gusts, the aerodynamic ground effect \cite{sanchez-cuevas2017} that dominates near touchdown, and residual unmodelled rotor dynamics.

A downward-facing monocular camera is mounted so that its optical axis is aligned with $Z_\text{b}$. Following the optic-flow formulation of \cite{herisse2012}, the target image dynamics are
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\boldsymbol{h}} = B_h \,^\mathcal{V} \boldsymbol{F}_u + \boldsymbol{c}_h + \boldsymbol{d}_h,
\end{equation}
where 
\begin{align*}
    B_h &= - \frac{1}{m \,^\mathcal{V} z_{\text{t}}} I_{3 \times 3}, \\
    \boldsymbol{c}_h &=  - \,^\mathcal{V}\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{\text{t/b}} \times \boldsymbol{s} - \,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{t/b}} \times \left(\,^\mathcal{V} \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{t/b}} \times \boldsymbol{s} \right) - 2 \left(\,^\mathcal{V} \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{t/b}} \times \boldsymbol{h} \right) \nonumber \\
    &~~~ - \left[\left( \boldsymbol{h} - \,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{t/b}} \times \boldsymbol{s} \right) \cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_3 \right] \boldsymbol{h} - \dot{\boldsymbol{h}}_\text{d} \\
    \boldsymbol{d}_h &= \frac{\,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{t}}}{\,^\mathcal{V}z_{\text{t}}} - \left(\frac{\,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{F_{\text{g}}} + \,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{F_{\text{d}}}}{m \,^\mathcal{V}z_{\text{t}}} - \,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{b}} \times \frac{\,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{v}_{\text{b}}}{\,^\mathcal{V}z_{\text{t}}} \right).
\end{align*}
Here $\boldsymbol{h} = \,^\mathcal{V} \boldsymbol{v}_{\text{t/b}}/\,^\mathcal{V} z_{\text{t}}$ is the optic flow representing the visual velocity of the target relative to the UAV, and $\,^\mathcal{V} \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{t/b}}$ is the angular rate of the target relative to the UAV, both expressed in the virtual frame $\mathcal{V}$.
 

\subsection{Image-Kinematic Reformulation and Problem Statement} \label{problem statement: section}
The proposed controller replaces the scale-dependent inertial state $(\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{r}_\text{b},\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{v}_\text{b})$ of the cascaded pose-based baseline (Section~S1, supplement) with the scale-independent image features $(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{h})$. Under the pinhole projection, the normalized target position is
\begin{equation} \label{normalized_pos: equation}
    \boldsymbol{s} = \begin{bmatrix}\,^\mathcal{V}\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}\\ 1\end{bmatrix} = \frac{\,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{r}_\text{t}}{\,^\mathcal{V}z_\text{t}},
\end{equation}
where the target image point $\,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\hat{r}}$ represents the projection of the target point $\,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{r}_{\text{t}}$ in the virtual image plane and it is defined as $\,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\hat{r}} = \left(\sum^{N}_{k=1} \,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\hat{r}}_k \right)/N$ with the $k^{th}$ feature point represented as $\boldsymbol{\hat{r}}_k = [\,^\mathcal{V}\hat{x}_k, \,^\mathcal{V}\hat{y}_k]^{\top}$.
Differentiating \eqref{normalized_pos: equation} and defining the position error $\boldsymbol{s}_\text{e}=\boldsymbol{s}-\boldsymbol{s}_\text{d}$ with $\dot{\boldsymbol{s}}_\text{d}=\boldsymbol{0}$ yields the image position kinematics
\begin{equation} \label{s_e_dot: equation}
    \dot{\boldsymbol{s}}_\text{e} = \boldsymbol{h} - \,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\omega}_\text{t/b}\times\boldsymbol{s} - \big[(\boldsymbol{h}-\,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\omega}_\text{t/b}\times\boldsymbol{s})\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_3\big]\boldsymbol{s},
\end{equation}
from which the desired optic flow is solved as
\begin{equation} \label{h_des: equation}
    \boldsymbol{h}_\text{d} = \dot{\boldsymbol{s}}_\text{e} + \,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\omega}_\text{t/b}\times\boldsymbol{s} + \big[h_\text{rd} - (\,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\omega}_\text{t/b}\times\boldsymbol{s})\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_3\big]\boldsymbol{s},
\end{equation}
where $h_\text{rd}>0$ is a constant desired radial (descent) optic flow. The optic-flow error $\boldsymbol{h}_\text{e}=\boldsymbol{h}-\boldsymbol{h}_\text{d}$ then satisfies
\begin{equation} \label{h_e_dot_1: equation}
    \dot{\boldsymbol{h}}_\text{e} = \beta\,\boldsymbol{u}_h + \boldsymbol{c}_h + \boldsymbol{d}_h,
\end{equation}
with $\boldsymbol{u}_h=-\,^\mathcal{I}R_\mathcal{V}^\top\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$, $\beta=1/\,^\mathcal{V}z_\text{t}$; $\boldsymbol{c}_h$ is known and available for feedback linearization, while $\beta$ and $\boldsymbol{d}_h$ are unknown.

Orientation alignment is handled without a magnetometer by extracting the target image orientation from second-order centred moments:
\begin{equation} \label{target orientation: equation}
    \alpha = \tfrac12\tan^{-1}\!\big(2\mu_{11}/(\mu_{20}-\mu_{02})\big),
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{ij}$ are the centered moments given as $\mu_{ij} = \sum^{N}_{k=1} (\,^\mathcal{V}\hat{x}_k - \,^\mathcal{V}\hat{x})^i (\,^\mathcal{V}\hat{y}_k - \,^\mathcal{V}\hat{y})^j$.
Differentiating \eqref{target orientation: equation} and defining the orientation error $\alpha_\text{e}=\alpha-\alpha_\text{d}$ with $\dot{\alpha}_\text{d}=0$ yields the image orientation kinematics
\begin{equation} \label{alpha_e_dot: equation}
    \dot{\alpha}_\text{e} = - \dot{\psi}_\text{d} + d_{\alpha},
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_e$ unwrapped to $(-\pi/2,\pi/2]$.
Here $\dot{\psi}_\text{d}$ is the desired yaw rate; $d_{\alpha} = f(\,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{t}}, \,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{b},xy})$ is unknown.

\textit{Assumption 1} (uncertainty bounds): The disturbances $\boldsymbol{d}_h$, $d_{\alpha}$ are uniformly bounded with unknown componentwise bound $\boldsymbol{d}_{\max}$, and $0<\beta_{\min}\le\beta\le\beta_{\max}$ with unknown $\beta_{\min},\beta_{\max}$.

\textit{Property 1} (cone-clamped lateral acceleration): By construction of the funnel-margin cone clamp in Section~\ref{fov cone: section}, the commanded lateral acceleration satisfies the state-dependent bound $|\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}|\le|\,^\mathcal{I}a_{\text{d},z}|\tan\theta_\text{cone}(t)$, with $\theta_\text{cone}(t)\le\theta_\text{cap}=60^\circ$.

The control-design problem is therefore to synthesise $\boldsymbol{h}_\text{d}$, $\boldsymbol{u}_h$ and $\dot{\psi}_\text{d}$ so that the target feature points remain visible throughout the descent and $\boldsymbol{h}_\text{e}$, $\alpha_\text{e}$ converge to small ultimate bounds under Assumption~1 and Property~1.

\section{MDF-ASMC CONTROL DESIGN} \label{control strategy: section}
The proposed MDF-ASMC is a \emph{dual-funnel} design (Fig.~\ref{block diagram: figure}). The outer loop is developed in three sub-blocks --- Section~\ref{normalized pid: section} (normalized virtual feature PID producing $\boldsymbol{h}_\text{d}$), Section~\ref{ppc optic flow: section} (optic-flow funnel and leakage-type adaptive SMC), Section~\ref{fov cone: section} (target image funnel and funnel-margin cone clamp) --- followed by the inner loop (Section~\ref{inner loop: section}) and the layered Lyapunov certificate (Section~\ref{stability: section}).

\subsection{Outer Loop: Dual Funnel and Adaptive SMC} \label{outer loop: section}

\subsubsection{Normalized Virtual Image Feature Control Law} \label{normalized pid: section}
The desired optic flow $\boldsymbol{h}_\text{d}$ feeding the optic-flow funnel is synthesised from a scale-independent PID on the normalized virtual image feature error. With $\mathcal{R}=[r_h,r_w]^\top$ the camera-sensor resolution (in pixels) and $f$ the focal length (in pixels), let $\boldsymbol{\varphi}_\text{max}=\mathcal{R}/(2f)\in\mathbb{R}^2_{>0}$ denote the camera half-FoV expressed in tangent (feature) units, and define the \emph{virtual image feature error} $\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}_\text{e}$ and its \emph{normalized virtual image feature error} $\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}_\text{e}$ by
\begin{equation} \label{normalized error: equation}
    \bar{\boldsymbol{r}}_\text{e} = \hat{\boldsymbol{r}}_\text{e}\oslash\boldsymbol{\varphi}_\text{max},\qquad\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}_\text{e}=\,^\mathcal{V}\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}-\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}_\text{d},
\end{equation}
where $\oslash$ is componentwise division and $|\bar{r}_{\text{e}_k}|=1$ marks the FoV boundary. The \emph{normalized virtual feature PID} generates the reference error-derivative
\begin{equation} \label{s_e_dot_d: equation}
    \dot{\boldsymbol{s}}_\text{e} = -K_{rp}\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}_\text{e} - K_{ri}\!\int\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}_\text{e}\,d\tau - K_{rd}\frac{\delta\bar{\boldsymbol{r}}_\text{e}}{\delta t},
\end{equation}
with diagonal gains $K_{rp},K_{ri},K_{rd}\succ 0$. The third (descent) component of $\dot{\boldsymbol{s}}_\text{e}$ vanishes identically by~\eqref{s_e_dot: equation}, so the PID acts only on the two lateral axes; the descent reference enters $\boldsymbol{h}_\text{d}$ separately through $h_\text{rd}$ in~\eqref{h_d final: equation}. Substituting into \eqref{h_des: equation} yields
\begin{equation} \label{h_d final: equation}
    \boldsymbol{h}_\text{d} = \dot{\boldsymbol{s}}_\text{e} + \,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\omega}_\text{t/b}\times\boldsymbol{s} + \big[h_\text{rd} - (\,^\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\omega}_\text{t/b}\times\boldsymbol{s})\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_3\big]\boldsymbol{s}.
\end{equation}
Normalization by $\boldsymbol{\varphi}_\text{max}$ makes the PID gains invariant under a change of camera resolution and lifts the unit-free constraint $|\bar{r}_{\text{e}_k}|\le 1$ into a common tuning target.

\subsubsection{Optic Flow Control Law} \label{ppc optic flow: section}
The optic-flow funnel constrains $h_{e_k}\in(-p_{2_k}(t),p_{2_k}(t))$, $k\in\{1,2,3\}$, with performance function $\boldsymbol{p}_2(t) = e^{-\Xi_2 t}(\boldsymbol{p}_{2_0}-\boldsymbol{p}_{2_\infty})+\boldsymbol{p}_{2_\infty}$, where $\boldsymbol{p}_{2_0},\boldsymbol{p}_{2_\infty},\Xi_2=\text{diag}(\xi_{2_k})\succ 0$ set the maximum overshoot, steady-state residual, and minimum convergence rate. A hyperbolic-tangent transformation maps the constrained error onto the unconstrained coordinate $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_2$:
\begin{align}
    \label{optical flow error transformation: equation}
    \boldsymbol{h}_e &= \mathcal{S}_2(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_2)\boldsymbol{p}_2(t),\quad S_2(\zeta_{2_k}) = \frac{e^{\zeta_{2_k}}-1}{e^{\zeta_{2_k}}+1},
\end{align}
with inverse $\zeta_{2_k}=\ln\!\big((p_{2_k}+h_{e_k})/(p_{2_k}-h_{e_k})\big)$. Differentiating and substituting \eqref{h_e_dot_1: equation} yields the unconstrained perturbed dynamics
\begin{equation} \label{unconstrained system: equation}
    \dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_2 = \mathcal{G}_2\!\left[\beta\boldsymbol{u}_h + \boldsymbol{c}_h + \boldsymbol{d}_h - \mathcal{S}_2(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_2)\dot{\boldsymbol{p}}_2(t)\right],
\end{equation}
with Jacobian $\mathcal{G}_2=\text{diag}(g_{2_k})$, $g_{2_k}=(e^{\zeta_{2_k}}+1)^2/(2e^{\zeta_{2_k}}p_{2_k})$.

A funnel-saturation guard clamps $|S_2(\zeta_{2_k})|\le 1-\epsilon_S$ to keep $\mathcal{G}_2$ bounded as $|S_2|\to 1$; implementation details, initial-condition feasibility, and sliding-surface anti-windup are in Section~S2.

A leakage-type adaptive first-order sliding mode law then drives $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_2$ in \eqref{unconstrained system: equation} to a uniform ultimate bound without any \emph{a priori} knowledge of $\beta$ or $\boldsymbol{d}_h$, extending~\cite{singhal2025} to the 3D image-kinematic setting.

Define the PI sliding surface $\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{\zeta}_2+\mathcal{X}\!\int_{t_0}^{t}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_2\,d\tau$ with $\mathcal{X}=\text{diag}(\chi_k)\succ 0$. Combining $\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}=\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_2+\mathcal{X}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_2$ with \eqref{unconstrained system: equation} yields
\begin{equation} \label{sigma derivative: equation 2}
    \dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}} = \beta\mathcal{G}_2\!\left[\boldsymbol{u}_h+\boldsymbol{c}_h-\mathcal{S}_2(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_2)\dot{\boldsymbol{p}}_2(t)+\mathcal{G}_2^{-1}\mathcal{X}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_2\right] + \beta_{\min}\mathcal{G}_2\boldsymbol{\theta}\boldsymbol{\overline{d}},
\end{equation}
where regressor $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ and lumped disturbance $\boldsymbol{\overline{d}}$ collect the uncertain terms with $\|\boldsymbol{\overline{d}}\|\le\tilde{d}$ (definitions in Section~S2). The proposed control law cancels the known part and switches on the sliding surface with an online-identified gain:
\begin{align} \label{adaptive control law: equation}
    \boldsymbol{u}_h &= \mathcal{G}_2^{-1}[\boldsymbol{u}_\text{sw}+\boldsymbol{u}_\text{eq}],\nonumber\\
    \boldsymbol{u}_\text{sw} &= -\Gamma\boldsymbol{\sigma} - \|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|\,\text{sat}(\mathcal{E}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\sigma})\mathcal{G}_2\boldsymbol{\kappa}(t),\\
    \boldsymbol{u}_\text{eq} &= \mathcal{G}_2\!\left[-\boldsymbol{c}_h+\mathcal{S}_2(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_2)\dot{\boldsymbol{p}}_2(t)-\mathcal{G}_2^{-1}\mathcal{X}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_2\right],\nonumber
\end{align}
where $\Gamma=\text{diag}(\gamma_k)\succ 0$, $\mathcal{E}=\text{diag}(\varepsilon_k)$ with $0<\varepsilon_k\ll 1$ sets the boundary-layer thickness, and $\text{sat}(\cdot)$ is the componentwise saturation. The adaptive gain evolves via the leakage-type law
\begin{equation} \label{adaptive law: equation}
    \dot{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}(t) = \|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|\mathcal{N}\mathcal{G}_2|\boldsymbol{\sigma}| - \mathcal{N}\mathcal{P}\boldsymbol{\kappa}(t),\quad\boldsymbol{\kappa}(0)\in\mathbb{R}_+^3,
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{N}=\text{diag}(\eta_k)\succ 0$, $\mathcal{P}=\text{diag}(\rho_k)\succ 0$.

\subsubsection{Target Image Funnel and Funnel-Margin Cone Clamp} \label{fov cone: section}
The target image funnel and its funnel-margin cone clamp jointly realise the state-dependent attitude cone of Property~1 by projecting the commanded lateral acceleration onto a cone whose half-angle is tied to the instantaneous funnel margin. Let $\boldsymbol{\hat{r}}_i\in\mathbb{R}^2$, $i\in\{1,\ldots,4\}$, denote the pixel coordinates of the four target feature points on the image plane. The target image funnel is a rectangular envelope centred on the image centre and collapsing exponentially toward its terminal half-widths, defined by
\begin{equation} \label{rho fov: equation}
    \boldsymbol{p}_1(t) = e^{-\xi_1 t}\big(\boldsymbol{p}_{1_0}-\boldsymbol{p}_{1_\infty}\big) + \boldsymbol{p}_{1_\infty},
\end{equation}
with $\boldsymbol{p}_{1_0},\boldsymbol{p}_{1_\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^2_{>0}$ the initial and terminal half-widths (in pixel units), $\xi_1>0$ the decay rate, and a strict inset $\boldsymbol{p}_{1_0}\prec\mathcal{R}/2$ (15~px per side) absorbing the perspective spread from initial body tilt. Define the \emph{funnel margin} $d_\text{min}^\text{fov}(t)$ as the worst-case axis-wise slack between the four feature points and $\boldsymbol{p}_1(t)$:

\begin{equation} \label{d min fov: equation}
    d_\text{min}^\text{fov}(t) = \max\!\left(0,\ \min_{i,k}\!\big(p_{1_k}(t)-|\hat{r}_{i,k}|\big)\right),\ k\in\{1,2\},
\end{equation}
which stays strictly positive while every feature point lies inside $\boldsymbol{p}_1(t)$ and is clipped to zero the moment any feature point touches the boundary. The cone half-angle onto which $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$ is projected is
\begin{equation} \label{cone angle: equation}
    \theta_\text{cone}(t) = \min\!\Big(\theta_\text{curr}(t) + \tan^{-1}\!\big(d_\text{min}^\text{fov}(t)/f\big),\ \theta_\text{cap}\Big),
\end{equation}
where $\theta_\text{curr}(t)=\arccos(R_{33}(t))$ is the tilt of the body-$z$ axis from the inertial vertical and $\theta_\text{cap}=60^\circ$ is the airframe tilt ceiling. As any feature point approaches the funnel boundary, $d_\text{min}^\text{fov}\to 0$ and $\theta_\text{cone}\to\theta_\text{curr}$, so the outer loop cannot command any additional tilt that would push that feature point outside $\boldsymbol{p}_1(t)$. The projection itself is
\begin{equation} \label{cone clamp: equation}
    \,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}\leftarrow \min\!\left(1,\ \frac{|\,^\mathcal{I}a_{\text{d},z}|\tan\theta_\text{cone}(t)}{\|\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}\|+\epsilon_\text{c}}\right)\!\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy},
\end{equation}
with $\epsilon_\text{c}>0$ a numerical floor disjoint from the ASMC boundary-layer widths $\varepsilon_k$ of \eqref{adaptive control law: equation}. By construction the target feature points remain inside $\boldsymbol{p}_1(t)\subseteq\boldsymbol{p}_1(0)\preceq\mathcal{R}/2$, so the visibility constraint is enforced kinematically for all $t\ge 0$. The projection is active for $<1\%$ of control steps in the simulations and its bounded additive contribution is absorbed into $\boldsymbol{d}_h$ in \eqref{h_e_dot_1: equation}.

\subsection{Inner Loop: Virtual-Compass Yaw ASMC and SO(3) Tracker} \label{inner loop: section}

\subsubsection{Yaw ASMC with Virtual-Compass Integrator}
In order to drive $\alpha_e$ in \eqref{alpha_e_dot: equation} to a uniform ultimate bound without any \emph{a priori} knowledge of $d_{\alpha}$, a leakage-type ASMC law, with sliding surface $\sigma_{\alpha}=\alpha_e+\chi_{\alpha}\!\int\alpha_e\,d\tau$, is formulated to produce the desired yaw-rate $\dot{\psi}_\text{d}$ signal:
\begin{align} \label{yaw control law: equation}
    \dot{\psi}_\text{d} &= \gamma_{\alpha}\sigma_{\alpha} + \text{sat}(\sigma_{\alpha}/\varepsilon_{\alpha})\kappa_{\alpha}(t) + \chi_{\alpha} \alpha_e, \nonumber\\
    \dot{\kappa}_{\alpha}(t) &= \eta_{\alpha}|\sigma_{\alpha}| - \eta_{\alpha}\rho_{\alpha}\kappa_{\alpha}(t),\quad\kappa_{\alpha}(0)>0,
\end{align}
where gains $\gamma_{\alpha}, \chi_{\alpha}, \eta_{\alpha}, \rho_{\alpha} > 0$ and $0<\varepsilon_{\alpha}\ll 1$ sets the boundary-layer thickness.

The $\dot{\psi}_\text{d}$ signal is integrated online as the \emph{time-derivative of a desired heading}:
\begin{equation} \label{psi d integrator: equation}
    \psi_\text{d}(t_{k+1}) = \Pi_{[-\pi,\pi]}\!\big[\psi_\text{d}(t_k) + \dot{\psi}_\text{d}(t_k)\,\Delta t\big].
\end{equation}
This \emph{virtual-compass generator} makes $\psi_\text{d}$ evolve purely from the image-based yaw error, enabling magnetometer-free operation. The yaw control loop decouples from the translational dynamics, and its Lyapunov certificate is given by Theorem~2 in Section~\ref{stability: section}.

\subsubsection{Geometric SO(3) Attitude Tracker} \label{so3 inner loop: section}
The outer law \eqref{adaptive control law: equation} returns a desired inertial acceleration $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$, which MDF-ASMC realises through the geometric almost-global attitude tracker of \cite{lee2010}. The desired thrust magnitude $\,^\mathcal{B}T_u$, desired rotation $R_\text{d}$, and tracking errors $(\boldsymbol{e}_R,\boldsymbol{e}_\Omega)$ are constructed from $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$ and $\psi_\text{d}$ as detailed in Section~S2; the commanded body torque is
\begin{equation} \label{so3 torque: equation}
    \,^\mathcal{B}\boldsymbol{\tau}_u = -k_R\boldsymbol{e}_R - k_\Omega\boldsymbol{e}_\Omega + \,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{b}}\times J\,^\mathcal{B} \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\text{b}},
\end{equation}
with $k_R,k_\Omega\succ 0$, which inherits the almost-global exponential stability of \cite[Thm.~3.1]{lee2010}.

\subsection{Lyapunov Stability Analysis} \label{stability: section}
The stability analysis follows a layered Lyapunov argument for cascaded nonlinear systems \cite{khalil2002}, building on the standard definition of global uniform ultimate boundedness (GUUB). The key statements are reported below; the full proof, the preparatory Lemma on GUUB, and the line-by-line Lyapunov derivative manipulation are relegated to Section~S2 of the supplement.

\textit{Theorem 1 (Adaptive Optic-Flow Funnel Invariance).}
Consider the perturbed nonlinear system \eqref{h_e_dot_1: equation} with initial optic-flow error $-\boldsymbol{p}_{2_0}<\boldsymbol{h}_e(0)<\boldsymbol{p}_{2_0}$. Then, under \textit{Assumption~1} and \textit{Property~1}, the unconstrained system \eqref{unconstrained system: equation} driven by the proposed control law \eqref{adaptive control law: equation}--\eqref{adaptive law: equation} exponentially drives $\boldsymbol{\zeta}_2$ into a UUB
\begin{equation} \label{UUB bound: equation}
    \vartheta = \sqrt{\frac{3\,\beta_{\min}\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{P})}{\varphi_1-\varphi_2}}\,\tilde{d},
\end{equation}
where $\varphi_1 \triangleq \beta_{\min}\frac{\min\{2\lambda_{\min}(\Gamma),\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{P})\}}{\max\{1,\beta_{\min}\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{N}^{-1})\}}>0$ and $0<\varphi_2<\varphi_1$.

\textit{Corollary 1 (Closed-loop target visibility).} Under the conditions of Theorem~1, the normalized virtual image feature PID \eqref{s_e_dot_d: equation} renders $\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}_\text{e}$ ultimately bounded by an affine function of $\vartheta$, and the cone-clamp projection \eqref{cone clamp: equation} enforces the visibility constraint kinematically for all $t\ge 0$. The ISS cascade and the geometric visibility argument are in Section~S2.

\textit{Theorem 2 (Adaptive Yaw ASMC Ultimate Boundedness).}
Consider the image orientation kinematics \eqref{alpha_e_dot: equation} with initial condition $\alpha_e(0)\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2]$. Then, under \textit{Assumption~1}, the yaw ASMC law \eqref{yaw control law: equation} exponentially drives $\sigma_\alpha$ into the UUB
\begin{equation} \label{yaw UUB bound: equation}
    \vartheta_\alpha = \sqrt{\frac{\rho_\alpha}{\varphi_1^\alpha-\varphi_2^\alpha}}\,\tilde{d}_\alpha,
\end{equation}
where $\varphi_1^\alpha \triangleq \frac{\min\{2\gamma_\alpha,\,\rho_\alpha\}}{\max\{1,\,\eta_\alpha^{-1}\}}>0$, $0<\varphi_2^\alpha<\varphi_1^\alpha$, and $\tilde{d}_\alpha$ is the upper bound on $|d_\alpha|$ from Assumption~1. Consequently, $\alpha_e$ is uniformly ultimately bounded by an affine function of $\vartheta_\alpha$, and the virtual-compass integrator \eqref{psi d integrator: equation} remains well-defined for all $t\ge 0$. The proof is provided in Section~S2.

Together, Theorems~1--2 and Corollary~1 deliver a single layered certificate for soft precise landing, target visibility, and magnetometer-free heading alignment.
