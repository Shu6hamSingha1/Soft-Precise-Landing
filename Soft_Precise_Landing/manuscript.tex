\begin{abstract}This paper addresses autonomous soft-precise landing of a quadrotor on a moving target from monocular vision alone, with no metric depth or heading measurement. Existing visual-servoing laws built for tracking do not guarantee the soft-precise touchdown, motivating \emph{Monocular Dual-Funnel Adaptive Sliding-Mode Control} (MDF-ASMC). An optic-flow funnel generates the commanded acceleration through a leakage-type adaptive sliding-mode law. A target image funnel defines a state-dependent attitude cone that kinematically clips the commanded acceleration, enforcing the visibility and actuator-saturation constraints. By deriving the desired optic flow and the yaw reference from scale-independent image parameters, MDF-ASMC eliminates the controller's dependence on metric-depth or heading measurements. A layered stability certificate delivers adaptive optic-flow funnel invariance, magnetometer-free heading alignment, and kinematic target visibility. Simulations across five initial conditions and five trajectories (including two ship-deck cases) under depth-dependent pixel noise, wind with turbulence, parametric uncertainty, ground effect, delay, and saturation yield a $25/25$ landing rate with mean landing time $\le 18.2~\text{s}$ and worst-case horizontal error of $5.7~\text{cm}$. In a comparative benchmark against four recent baselines, MDF-ASMC is the only controller to achieve worst-case $\le 2.1~\text{cm}$ touchdown on every trajectory in the comparison study, well inside the $0.08~\text{m}$ precise threshold.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
U{\scshape nmanned} Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are increasingly tasked with autonomous landings on mobile platforms such as ships and ground vehicles. An imprecise landing risks missing the platform entirely or grazing its edge, while a hard landing risks airframe and payload damage on contact. Hence, the terminal landing phase demands both \emph{precise} positioning and a \emph{soft} touchdown, despite encountering the worst wind, ground effect, and measurement noise of the descent. For a guaranteed soft-precise landing, the functional requirements are (a) the smooth lateral approach of the UAV to a close vicinity of the target before touchdown, and (b) the simultaneous convergence of the UAV's altitude and velocity to those of the target at touchdown.

Existing solutions for autonomous landing fall into position-based and image-based visual servoing (PBVS and IBVS) frameworks. PBVS approaches rely on explicit 3-D pose reconstruction from visual measurements \cite{lin2022, zhang2026}, and do not guarantee the precise touchdown under sensing degradation such as calibration drift, scale drift, or marker occlusion. IBVS methods instead drive image features directly \cite{chaumette2006}, with extensions to aerial platforms \cite{jabbari2014, lee2012, fink2017, xie2016_2, xie2020, cho2022, chen2025} through the virtual-camera abstraction and adaptive variants that compensate for depth uncertainty, disturbances, and input saturation. These IBVS frameworks achieve the functional requirement of the precise touchdown through asymptotic image-feature convergence. However, they depend on explicit metric depth or implicit scale information, and assume noise-free image features and perfect intrinsic calibration.

None of these frameworks achieve the functional requirement of the soft touchdown. \emph{Optic flow} inherently regulates both relative altitude and relative velocity to the target, making it a natural sensing modality for the soft touchdown \cite{herisse2012, izzo2011, singhal2025}. However, existing optic-flow schemes either restrict to purely vertical descent on stationary targets \cite{izzo2011, singhal2025} or rely on an external heading reference and assume an unrestricted field of view (FoV) during the descent \cite{herisse2012}.

An orthogonal line of work directly enforces \emph{visibility constraints} so that the target remains visible during the terminal descent. Barrier-function constructions \cite{salehi2021} impose hard inequality constraints but render the control law unbounded near the FoV boundary. Input-saturated visual servoing \cite{xie2016} bounds the vehicle's roll and pitch to keep features inside the FoV, but the fixed bound cannot tighten as features approach the FoV edge. Beyond these visibility-side limitations, neither method addresses the soft touchdown problem.

Beyond the above frameworks, recent work introduces vision--IMU sensor fusion, adaptive control, and learning-based control. State-estimation pipelines \cite{bouazza2025} reconstruct relative pose through vision--IMU fusion but require an inter-platform communication channel and known target geometry. Adaptive control schemes \cite{paris2020, chen2025, zhang2026} presuppose an \emph{a priori} characterization of the disturbance class or switching gain. Learning-based control schemes \cite{kamath2026} depend on heavy perception pipelines and pre-identify an aerodynamic model offline. None of these approaches is available at flight time on an unknown target with unknown depth scaling.

To the best of the authors' knowledge, the literature exhibits three research gaps. \emph{(i)} The soft-precise touchdown on a moving target using only monocular vision remains an open problem. \emph{(ii)} Online identification of the full uncertainty on the optic-flow dynamics (depth scaling, disturbances, and noisy image features) is unresolved. \emph{(iii)} A closed-loop stability guarantee that simultaneously certifies the soft touchdown, the precise touchdown, and target visibility is missing. These gaps, summarized qualitatively in Table~\ref{tab:comparison}, motivate the Monocular Dual-Funnel Adaptive Sliding-Mode Control (MDF-ASMC) framework developed in this paper.

The main contributions of this paper are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] An optic-flow funnel is stabilized by a leakage-type adaptive sliding-mode control (ASMC) whose switching gain is identified online without an \emph{a priori} uncertainty bound. A target image funnel sizes a state-dependent attitude cone that clips the commanded acceleration, enforcing the visibility and actuator-saturation constraints (Section~\ref{control design: section}-\ref{outer loop: section}, closing gaps~(i)--(ii)).
    \item[(b)] Optic flow and the virtual image orientation make MDF-ASMC the only scale-free framework in Table~\ref{tab:comparison}, eliminating the metric-depth and heading-measurement dependencies retained by existing image-based landing laws (Section~\ref{background: section}-\ref{image parameters: section}, supporting gap~(i)).
    \item[(c)] A layered Lyapunov argument (Section~\ref{control design: section}-\ref{stability: section}, closing gap~(iii)) renders the optic-flow error (Theorem~1) and the image-orientation error (Theorem~2) globally uniformly ultimately bounded (GUUB). Optic-flow invariance further propagates to a closed-loop target-visibility guarantee (Corollary~1).
    \item[(d)] A 33-axis deep sweep over the locked MDF-ASMC parameters and a $\pm 40\%$ speed-envelope study quantify the design margin of the locked gain set, while a comparative benchmark against four recent baselines~\cite{lin2022,zhang2026,chen2025,cho2022} under a shared disturbance model exposes their structural limitations (Section~\ref{experimentation: section}).
\end{enumerate}

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. Section~\ref{background: section} presents the image-kinematic model of the optic-flow-guided soft-precise landing problem. Section~\ref{control design: section} develops the dual-funnel prescribed-performance design, the leakage-type adaptive sliding mode law, and the layered Lyapunov stability analysis. Section~\ref{experimentation: section} reports the numerical validation, including the robustness study across initial conditions (ICs) and target speeds and the comparative benchmark against four state-of-the-art landing controllers. Finally, Section~\ref{conclusion: section} presents the conclusions of this study.

\section{CONCLUSION}
\label{conclusion: section}
MDF-ASMC delivers both the soft-precise touchdown and target visibility in closed-loop simulation, with no metric depth or heading measurement. A leakage-type adaptive sliding-mode law stabilizes the optic-flow funnel online without any \emph{a priori} uncertainty bound. An exponentially-decaying target image funnel sizes a state-dependent attitude cone that kinematically clips the commanded acceleration in closed form. A layered stability certificate delivers optic-flow funnel invariance, magnetometer-free heading alignment, and kinematic target visibility under actuator saturation, ground effect, pixel noise, and computational delay.

Across twenty-five deterministic runs covering five ICs and five target trajectories (including two ship-deck cases), the closed-loop simulation lands in every run with worst-case horizontal error $5.7~\text{cm}$ and remains soft and precise across a $\pm 40\%$ target-speed envelope. In a comparative benchmark against four recent baselines, MDF-ASMC is the only controller to clear the visibility, soft-touchdown, and landing-time thresholds on every trajectory in the comparison study.

Future work includes PX4 hardware-in-the-loop validation on the X500 platform, an analytic bound on the cone-projection residual to tighten Theorem~1's UUB bound (which currently absorbs the cone-projection residual into $\boldsymbol{d}_h$) from implicit absorption to an explicit residual, and a target-rate-adaptive funnel schedule to widen the admissible speed band beyond the $\pm 40\%$ envelope edge.
