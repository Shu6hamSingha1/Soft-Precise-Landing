\begin{abstract}This paper addresses autonomous soft precise landing of a quadrotor on a moving target from monocular vision alone, with no metric depth or magnetic compass measurement. A visual-servoing law built for tracking does not guarantee a vanishing relative velocity at touchdown. We propose \emph{Monocular Dual-Funnel Adaptive Sliding-Mode Control} (MDF-ASMC), a dual-funnel architecture: an optic-flow funnel generates the commanded acceleration through a leakage-type adaptive sliding-mode law, and a target image funnel defines a state-dependent attitude cone that clips the commanded acceleration, enforcing the visibility and actuator-saturation constraints. The desired optic flow comes from a normalized virtual image feature PID, and the yaw reference is extracted from the target image orientation, so the controller operates without altitude or compass measurements. A layered Lyapunov certificate delivers adaptive optic-flow funnel invariance (Theorem~1), magnetometer-free heading alignment (Theorem~2), and closed-loop target visibility (Corollary~1). Simulations across five initial conditions and five trajectories (including two ship-deck cases) under depth-dependent pixel noise, wind with turbulence, parametric uncertainty, ground effect, delay, and saturation yield a $25/25$ landing rate with mean landing time $\le 18.2~\text{s}$ and worst-case horizontal error of $5.7~\text{cm}$; MDF-ASMC is the only controller in a comparative benchmark against four recent baselines to achieve worst-case $\le 2.1~\text{cm}$ touchdown on every moving trajectory --- well inside the $0.08~\text{m}$ precise threshold.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
U{\scshape nmanned} Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are increasingly tasked with autonomous landings on mobile platforms such as ships, ground vehicles, and robots, where imprecise or hard landings risk damage to the UAV, the payload, and the target infrastructure. The terminal landing phase is particularly demanding because it couples tight positioning requirements with a bounded UAV--target relative velocity while operating in the regime where wind, ground effect, and measurement noise are most pronounced. Throughout, a touchdown is termed \emph{precise} when the horizontal offset to the target is at most $0.08~\text{m}$ and \emph{soft} when the UAV--target relative speed is at most $0.20~\text{m/s}$, with the terminal altitude measured as the above-target gap $z_\text{f}=0.20~\text{m}$.

Existing solutions fall into position-based and image-based visual servoing (PBVS and IBVS) frameworks. PBVS approaches rely on explicit 3-D pose reconstruction from LiDAR, stereo cameras, or GPS \cite{salehi2021, cho2022} and degrade in GPS-denied environments and under calibration or scale drift. IBVS methods instead drive image features directly \cite{chaumette2006} and have been extended to aerial platforms \cite{jabbari2014, lee2012, fink2017, xie2016_2, xie2020} through the virtual-camera abstraction and adaptive variants that compensate for depth uncertainty, disturbances, and input saturation. Yet these works still rely on implicit or explicit scale information, assume ideal sensing, and rarely enforce the \emph{transient} specification required for a soft touchdown.

Regulating \emph{optic flow} rather than metric position circumvents scale ambiguity entirely \cite{herisse2012, izzo2011}: since optic flow is the ratio of velocity to distance, forcing it to zero drives the descent velocity to zero as altitude vanishes. Existing optic-flow schemes, however, typically address purely vertical descent on stationary targets; our own prior work \cite{singhal2025} was restricted to a single vertical output on a stationary surface, without the three-dimensional image-kinematic coupling that arises during lateral chase of a maneuvering target.

An orthogonal line of work enforces \emph{visibility constraints} so that the target does not leave the camera field of view (FoV) during the terminal descent. Barrier-function constructions \cite{salehi2021} impose hard inequality constraints but render the control law unbounded near the FoV boundary; prescribed-performance control (PPC) \cite{lin2022} instead forces the image error inside a smooth time-varying funnel without any unbounded gain. PPC, however, has so far been applied to the image-plane error \emph{alone}, and therefore answers the visibility question but not the soft-touchdown question: a single-funnel PPC law has no mechanism to prescribe the rate at which the UAV--target relative velocity vanishes. Recent adaptive and learning-based schemes \cite{paris2020, chen2025, bouazza2025, kamath2026, zhang2026} either depend on heavy perception pipelines, presuppose an \emph{a priori} upper bound on the switching gain or disturbance, or pre-identify an aerodynamic model offline --- none of which is available at flight time on an unknown target with unknown depth scaling.

The literature therefore exhibits three gaps. \emph{(i)} No existing image-based landing controller simultaneously constrains the image-plane error and the optic-flow error, so none converts a generic image-based servoing law into a provable soft-landing law without an external altitude estimate. \emph{(ii)} Existing adaptive visual-servoing laws identify only a subset of the uncertain terms: they require an \emph{a priori} bound on the switching gain or disturbance, or a pre-identified aerodynamic model, so none identifies the full sliding-mode gain online on an image-kinematic plant whose depth scaling $\beta=1/z_\text{t}$ is itself unknown. \emph{(iii)} No prior work has established a layered Lyapunov certificate linking the optic-flow error back to the visibility error in closed loop, as required to jointly certify soft and precise touchdown. These gaps --- summarised qualitatively in Table~\ref{tab:comparison} --- motivate the MDF-ASMC framework developed in this paper.

The main contributions of this paper are summarised as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] \textit{Dual-funnel architecture with leakage-type adaptive SMC.} An optic-flow funnel, driven by a normalized virtual feature PID, is stabilised by a leakage-type adaptive sliding-mode law whose switching gain is identified online without an \emph{a priori} uncertainty bound; a funnel-saturation guard removes the numerical pole of classical PPC near the funnel edge. A target image funnel sizes a state-dependent attitude cone whose projection clips the commanded acceleration, enforcing the visibility and actuator-saturation constraints (Sections~\ref{normalized pid: section}--\ref{fov cone: section}).
    \item[(b)] \textit{Scale-free and compass-free image-kinematic reformulation.} The translational loop uses optic flow in place of metric depth, and the rotational loop extracts its yaw reference from the image-plane orientation $\alpha$ of the target feature, eliminating both the range sensor and the magnetic compass retained by existing image-based landing laws (Section~\ref{problem statement: section}).
    \item[(c)] \textit{Layered Lyapunov certificate for soft precise landing.} A layered Lyapunov argument (Section~\ref{stability: section}) renders the optic-flow error globally uniformly ultimately bounded (Theorem~1), drives the image-orientation error to a uniform ultimate bound under the yaw ASMC (Theorem~2), and propagates optic-flow invariance to a closed-loop target-visibility guarantee (Corollary~1).
    \item[(d)] \textit{Systematic robustness characterization and comparative benchmarking.} A 33-axis deep sweep over the locked MDF-ASMC parameters and a $\pm 40\%$ speed-envelope study quantify the design margin of the locked gain set, while a comparative benchmark against four recent baselines~\cite{lin2022,zhang2026,chen2025,cho2022} under a shared disturbance model isolates the structural barriers that prevent existing image-based landing laws from simultaneously meeting both the precise and soft touchdown specifications on moving targets (Sections~\ref{multi init: subsec}--\ref{comparison: subsec}).
\end{enumerate}
Contribution~(a) closes gaps~(i)--(ii) by fusing the image-plane and optic-flow constraints in a single adaptive cascade that requires no \emph{a priori} uncertainty bound, (c) closes gap~(iii) through the layered certificate, (b) removes the external sensing retained by existing image-based landing laws, and (d) characterises the resulting design margin.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. Section~\ref{background: section} presents the image-kinematic model of the optic-flow-guided soft-landing problem. Section~\ref{control strategy: section} develops the dual-funnel prescribed-performance design, the leakage-type adaptive sliding mode law, and the layered Lyapunov stability analysis. Section~\ref{experimentation: section} reports the numerical validation, including the multi-initial-condition robustness study, the target-speed envelope, and the comparative benchmark against four state-of-the-art landing controllers. Finally, Section~\ref{conclusion: section} presents the conclusions of this study.

\section{CONCLUSION}
\label{conclusion: section}
MDF-ASMC meets both the soft-touchdown and target-visibility requirements without a range sensor or magnetometer: a leakage-type adaptive sliding-mode law stabilises the optic-flow funnel online without any \emph{a priori} uncertainty bound, an exponentially-decaying target image funnel sizes a state-dependent attitude cone that geometrically clips the commanded acceleration in closed form, and a layered Lyapunov certificate delivers optic-flow funnel invariance, magnetometer-free heading alignment, and closed-loop target visibility under actuator saturation, ground effect, pixel noise, and computational delay. Across twenty-five deterministic runs covering five initial conditions and five target trajectories (including two ship-deck cases), the closed loop lands in every run with worst-case horizontal error $5.7~\text{cm}$ and remains soft and precise across a $\pm 40\%$ target-speed envelope; in a comparative benchmark against four recent baselines, MDF-ASMC is the only controller to clear the visibility, soft-touchdown, and landing-time thresholds on every moving trajectory.

Future work includes PX4 hardware-in-the-loop validation on the X500 platform, an analytic bound on the cone-projection residual to upgrade Corollary~1 from an implicit-residual to a strict guarantee, and a target-rate-adaptive funnel schedule to widen the admissible speed band beyond the $\pm 40\%$ envelope edge.
