\begin{abstract}This paper addresses the image-based soft-precise landing problem of a low-SWaP-C quadrotor on an unpredictable moving vehicle using a monocular camera and IMU, without any prior knowledge of the target state or dynamics. Most prior image-based landing controllers regulate either image features for precise lateral convergence or optical flow for soft touchdown, but not both simultaneously, as the two image parameters remain coupled through nonlinear landing kinematics that intensify near the landing surface. To overcome this limitation, this paper proposes \emph{Visual Dual-Funnel Adaptive Sliding-Mode Control}, a unified scale-free strategy that simultaneously regulates both the image parameters and guarantees that the target remains visible throughout the descent, while ensuring desired transient and steady-state performance in the presence of unknown bounded uncertainties arising from wind gusts, ground effects, and actuator nonlinearities, without any prior knowledge of their upper bounds. Detailed Lyapunov stability analysis demonstrates that the image parameter errors converge exponentially to small prescribed bounds in steady state, which is shown to be the key to achieving a guaranteed soft-precise touchdown. Numerical simulations across five initial conditions and five target trajectories under realistic disturbance settings, including multiple target speeds, demonstrate guaranteed soft-precise landing with mean landing time $\leq 18.2$~s and horizontal error within $5.7$~cm. Performance comparison studies against four state-of-the-art baselines demonstrate that the proposed strategy is the only controller to achieve soft-precise landing on every trajectory.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION} \label{intro: section}
Autonomous landing on a moving platform underpins UAV missions in logistics, search and rescue, and ship-deck operations, yet it remains one of the most demanding phases of autonomous flight~\cite{cho2022,gupta2023,angelis2026}. A successful landing demands that the UAV simultaneously closes the lateral gap to the target and reduces its relative velocity to near zero at touchdown. Any residual separation or hard impact compromises both mission success and vehicle integrity. These two requirements, precise lateral convergence and soft touchdown, must be met jointly and in real time, under the size, weight, power, and cost (SWaP-C) constraints of the UAV platform. When the full state and dynamics of both the UAV and the target are available, designing a controller to meet these requirements is tractable~\cite{kumar2024,marcon2018}.

In practice, however, the full state of the target is rarely available. When the target shares its position and velocity over a communication link, or when cooperative sensing infrastructure such as GPS receivers or LiDAR units mounted on the target platform is available, landing controllers can exploit this information to close the loop~\cite{marcon2018,isilak2025,bouazza2025,cho2022}. These approaches implicitly assume a degree of target cooperation, either through shared sensing or active data exchange, which cannot be guaranteed in practical deployment scenarios. In particular, GPS-based infrastructure is unavailable in the indoor and GPS-denied environments where autonomous landing is most needed~\cite{bautista2023,niu2022}, and communication links cannot be assumed when the target is uncooperative or adversarial. In all cases, the common requirement is access to the target's state or dynamics through cooperative means.

When such cooperation is unavailable but the target carries observable visual features, its relative pose can be recovered from onboard vision without a cooperative data link~\cite{wenzel2010}. Some approaches exploit this by formulating landing control in relative dynamics, using prescribed performance bounds or robust compensation schemes to guarantee convergence despite unknown target velocity~\cite{lin2022,zhao2021}. These approaches, however, require the target's metric relative pose to be recovered from onboard sensing, which demands accurate depth estimation. When depth estimation degrades near the ground, however, these methods become unreliable.

The reliability of landing control then hinges on the onboard sensing modality itself. Range-based sensors, such as rangefinders and ultrasonic sensors, are single-point sensors that offer limited spatial awareness with a low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and low sampling rates, particularly near the ground~\cite{zhao2012}. Radar altimeters face additional challenges, including range-Doppler coupling during descent and insufficient angular resolution for precise lateral touchdown, which require complex waveform design to mitigate~\cite{awan2024,veneruso2025}. These sensors also impose significant size, weight, and power penalties that are incompatible with low-SWaP-C platforms. Vision sensors, by contrast, are compact, lightweight, and provide rich spatial information about the target without requiring any ground infrastructure, making them the preferred sensing modality for low-SWaP-C UAVs~\cite{semerikov2025,lin2023,angelis2026}. The same GPS-denied conditions, moreover, deprive the UAV of reliable estimates of its own inertial position and velocity, so a practical landing controller must close the loop without the absolute state of either vehicle.

Vision-based landing controllers fall into two classical paradigms. Position-based visual servoing (PBVS) reconstructs the target's metric pose from image measurements and closes the loop in metric space~\cite{lin2022,zhang2026}. This reconstruction requires knowledge of the target geometry and accurate depth estimation from onboard sensing, rendering it sensitive to calibration errors near the landing surface~\cite{lin2022,zhao2021}. Image-based visual servoing (IBVS), by contrast, formulates control directly in image space without requiring full 3D pose reconstruction, reducing its dependence on metric scale information~\cite{lin2023,cho2022}. Learning-based approaches have also been explored, training a policy directly on visual features extracted from the landing surface without requiring an explicit control law~\cite{angelis2026}. Yet learning-based approaches provide no formal stability certificate that bounds the landing error or guarantees target visibility.

The image kinematics underlying IBVS are nonetheless nonlinear and coupled. Unlike simplified formulations that treat the vertical optical-flow axis as a decoupled 1D system~\cite{herisse2012} or exploit rotation-invariant features to separate translation from rotation~\cite{lin2023}, the image-based landing kinematics couple the two image parameters---image features and optical flow---through shared kinematic terms. This coupling is most pronounced near the landing surface, where an uncertainty parameter that grows as altitude decreases renders the image-based landing dynamics sensitive to ground effects, wind gusts, and actuator nonlinearities~\cite{herisse2012,ho2018}.

Existing IBVS landing controllers address these coupled nonlinear landing dynamics using one of several control strategies. Nonlinear controllers with a fixed control gain regulate optical flow to track a constant divergence trajectory~\cite{herisse2012}, but the required gain is proportional to landing altitude, so no single fixed value ensures stability under the large disturbances near the ground~\cite{ho2018}. Empirical gain-scheduling approaches address the altitude-dependent gain problem by detecting height from self-induced oscillations at the start of descent and reducing gains exponentially during approach, but the schedule is not derived from a formal stability certificate~\cite{ho2018}. Prescribed performance approaches apply a time-varying error transformation to enforce formal convergence bounds on circle-based image features in the image space~\cite{lin2023}. Feed-forward IBVS controllers augment the standard image-space control law with a target velocity feed-forward term obtained by fusing onboard vision with ship-borne GPS measurements~\cite{cho2022}.

Despite these advances, no existing IBVS approach simultaneously guarantees soft touchdown, precise lateral convergence, and target visibility within a unified control framework. Some approaches regulate optical flow to achieve soft vertical touchdown without guaranteeing precise lateral convergence~\cite{herisse2012,ho2018}. Existing IBVS controllers that address lateral convergence provide no soft touchdown guarantee and additionally rely on the UAV's own velocity feedback~\cite{lin2023,cho2022}. While some approaches include heuristic mechanisms to keep features within the camera field of view (FoV)~\cite{cho2022}, no existing IBVS approach provides a formal closed-loop guarantee of target visibility derived from the control law~\cite{lin2023,cho2022,herisse2012,ho2018}. Table~\ref{tab:comparison} implies that, to the best of the authors' knowledge, an image-based landing strategy that simultaneously provides all three guarantees remains an open research area.

To address these limitations, this paper proposes Visual Dual-Funnel Adaptive Sliding-Mode Control (VDF-ASMC), a unified scale-free image-based control framework that builds on the leakage-type ASMC of~\cite{singhal2025} and extends it to soft-precise landing on an unpredictable moving vehicle using only a monocular camera and IMU, without metric depth, heading measurement, or the UAV's own inertial position and velocity estimates. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The image-based soft-precise landing problem on an unpredictable moving vehicle is addressed by guaranteeing the stability of the highly uncertain and nonlinear image-based landing dynamics using the proposed VDF-ASMC strategy, executing smooth and continuous control action.
\item The control gain dynamically adapts to the unknown uncertainties of the coupled and nonlinear image-based landing dynamics without requiring any prior knowledge of their upper bounds, ensuring the boundedness of the image parameter errors particularly near the landing surface where disturbances and nonlinearities are most pronounced.
\item The performance constraints are integrated within the Lyapunov stable control law to guarantee global exponential convergence of the optical-flow error to a small uniform ultimate bound (UUB) around the origin, which is shown to be the key to achieving a guaranteed soft-precise touchdown, while attenuating the impact of actuator faults and actuator saturation.
\item A lateral acceleration clamp, triggered by an exponentially decaying target feature funnel, explicitly ensures that the target remains visible throughout the descent, providing a formal closed-loop guarantee independent of the outer-loop transient.
\item Numerical simulations across five initial conditions and five target trajectories validate the robustness of VDF-ASMC against unknown target dynamics and multiple target velocities. Performance comparison studies against four state-of-the-art baselines demonstrate that VDF-ASMC is the only strategy to achieve soft-precise landing on every trajectory.
\end{enumerate}
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~II presents the problem formulation and image parameter kinematics. Section~III details the control design and stability analysis. Section~IV provides the simulation and comparison results. Section~V concludes the paper.

\section{CONCLUSION}
\label{conclusion: section}
VDF-ASMC delivers both the soft-precise touchdown and target visibility in closed-loop simulation, with no metric depth or heading measurement. A leakage-type adaptive sliding-mode law stabilizes the optical-flow funnel online without any \emph{a priori} uncertainty bound. An exponentially-decaying target feature funnel sizes a state-dependent attitude cone that kinematically clips the commanded acceleration in closed form. A layered stability certificate delivers optical-flow funnel invariance, magnetometer-free heading alignment, and kinematic target visibility under actuator saturation, ground effect, pixel noise, and computational delay.

Across twenty-five deterministic runs covering five ICs (nominal $+$ four displaced) and five target trajectories (including two ship-deck cases), the closed-loop simulation lands in every run with worst-case horizontal error $5.7~\text{cm}$ and remains soft and precise across a $\pm 40\%$ target-speed envelope. In a comparative benchmark against four recent baselines, VDF-ASMC is the only controller to clear the visibility, soft-touchdown, and precise-touchdown thresholds on every trajectory in the comparison study.

Future work includes PX4 hardware-in-the-loop validation on the X500 platform, an analytic bound on the cone-projection residual to tighten Theorem~1's UUB bound (which currently absorbs the cone-projection residual into $\boldsymbol{d}_h$) from implicit absorption to an explicit residual, and a target-rate-adaptive funnel schedule to widen the admissible speed band beyond the $\pm 40\%$ envelope edge. Together, the dual-funnel architecture and its layered stability certificate establish the theoretical and empirical basis for autonomous monocular soft-precise landing on moving targets.
