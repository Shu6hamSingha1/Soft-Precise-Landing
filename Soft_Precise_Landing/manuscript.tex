\maketitle

\begin{abstract}This paper addresses autonomous soft precise landing of a quadrotor on a moving target from monocular vision alone, with no metric depth, pose, or range measurement. A visual-servoing law built for tracking does not guarantee a vanishing descent velocity at touchdown. A \emph{Dual-Funnel Adaptive Sliding-Mode Control} (DF-ASMC) framework is proposed that couples a visibility funnel on the image-feature error with an optic-flow funnel on the descent-rate error through a barrier-transformed reference generator, converting a generic image-based servoing law into a provable soft-landing law without altitude or compass measurements --- the yaw reference is extracted directly from the image-plane orientation of the target. Both funnels are stabilised by a leakage-type adaptive sliding-mode law with provable gain boundedness under persistent noise and a saturation guard that eliminates the numerical pole of classical PPC implementations. A layered Lyapunov certificate then delivers visibility, soft touchdown, and disturbance rejection from one theorem. Simulations across five initial conditions and five trajectories (including two ship-deck cases) under pixel noise, ground effect, delay, and saturation yield a $25/25$ landing rate with worst-case horizontal error below $3.1~\text{cm}$, and DF-ASMC is the only controller in a head-to-head comparison against four recent baselines to achieve sub-decimetre touchdown on every moving trajectory.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
U{\scshape nmanned} Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are increasingly tasked with autonomous landings on mobile platforms such as ships, ground vehicles, and robots, where imprecise or hard landings risk damage to the UAV, the payload, and the target infrastructure. The terminal landing phase is particularly demanding because it couples tight positioning requirements with a bounded descent velocity while operating in the regime where wind, ground effect, and measurement noise are most pronounced.

Existing solutions fall into position-based and image-based visual servoing (PBVS and IBVS) frameworks. PBVS approaches rely on explicit 3-D pose reconstruction from LiDAR, stereo cameras, or GPS \cite{salehi2021, cho2022} and degrade in GPS-denied environments and under calibration or scale drift. IBVS methods instead drive image features directly \cite{chaumette2006} and have been extended to aerial platforms \cite{jabbari2014, lee2012, fink2017, xie2016_2, xie2020} through the virtual-camera abstraction and adaptive variants that compensate for depth uncertainty, disturbances, and input saturation. Yet these works still rely on implicit or explicit scale information, assume ideal sensing, and rarely enforce the \emph{transient} specification required for a soft touchdown.

Regulating \emph{optic flow} rather than metric position circumvents scale ambiguity entirely \cite{herisse2012, izzo2011}: since optic flow is the ratio of velocity to distance, forcing it to zero drives the descent velocity to zero as altitude vanishes. Existing optic-flow schemes, however, typically address purely vertical descent on stationary targets, and our own prior work \cite{singhal2025} --- despite combining optic-flow divergence with a robust adaptive controller and Lyapunov-based guarantees --- remained restricted to a single vertical output on a stationary surface, without the three-dimensional image-kinematic coupling that arises during lateral chase of a maneuvering target.

An orthogonal line of work enforces \emph{visibility constraints} so that the target does not leave the camera field of view during the terminal descent. Barrier-function constructions \cite{salehi2021} impose hard inequality constraints but render the control law unbounded near the field-of-view boundary; prescribed-performance control (PPC) \cite{lin2022} instead forces the image error inside a smooth time-varying funnel without any unbounded gain. PPC, however, has so far been applied to the image-plane error \emph{alone}, and therefore answers the visibility question but not the soft-touchdown question: a single-funnel PPC law has no mechanism to prescribe the rate at which the vertical velocity vanishes. Recent adaptive and learning-based schemes \cite{paris2020, chen2025, bouazza2025, kamath2026, zhang2026} either depend on heavy perception pipelines, assume a known uncertainty bound, or pre-identify an aerodynamic model --- none of which is available at flight time on an unknown target.

The literature therefore exhibits three gaps. \emph{(i)} No existing image-based landing controller simultaneously constrains the image-plane error and the optic-flow error, so none converts a generic image-based servoing law into a provable soft-landing law without an external altitude estimate. \emph{(ii)} Adaptive visual-servoing laws that tolerate disturbances and saturation do so only under a known uncertainty bound or a pre-identified model. \emph{(iii)} No prior work has established a layered Lyapunov certificate linking the optic-flow error back to the visibility error in closed loop, as required to certify both soft and precise touchdown from a single theorem. These gaps --- summarised qualitatively in Table~\ref{tab:comparison} and quantified head-to-head in Table~\ref{comparison table: table} of Section~\ref{comparison: subsec} --- motivate the DF-ASMC framework developed in this paper.

The main contributions of this paper are summarised as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] \textit{Dual-funnel prescribed-performance architecture.} A visibility funnel on the image-feature error and an optic-flow funnel on the descent-rate error are coupled through a barrier-transformed reference generator, converting a generic image-based servoing law into a provable soft-landing law without any altitude measurement (Sections~\ref{ppc visibility: section}--\ref{ppc optic flow: section}).
    \item[(b)] \textit{Scale-free and compass-free image-kinematic reformulation.} The translational loop uses optic flow in place of metric depth, and the rotational loop extracts its yaw reference from the image-plane orientation $\alpha$ of the target feature ($\dot{\alpha}=\dot{\psi}$), eliminating both the range sensor and the magnetic compass that every existing image-based landing law retains (Section~\ref{problem statement: section}).
    \item[(c)] \textit{Leakage-type adaptive sliding mode law with funnel saturation guard.} The optic-flow funnel is stabilised by a sliding-mode law whose switching gain is identified online; the leakage term provably prevents gain drift under persistent measurement noise, and a saturation guard on the optic-flow barrier transformation removes the numerical pole near the funnel edge without weakening the guarantee. An attitude-cone projection handles actuator saturation in closed form (Section~\ref{adaptive controller for inner loop : section}).
    \item[(d)] \textit{Layered Lyapunov certificate for soft precise landing.} A single layered Lyapunov argument (Section~\ref{stability: section}) renders the optic-flow error GUUB, preserves both funnels for all time, and propagates the inner-loop guarantee backward to the outer visibility error, delivering visibility, soft touchdown, and disturbance rejection from one theorem --- carried through the saturation, funnel-guard, and cone-clamp nonlinearities without approximation.
    \item[(e)] \textit{Comprehensive numerical validation.} Twenty-five deterministic runs across five initial conditions and five target trajectories (including two ship-deck cases with $\pm 15^\circ/\pm 8^\circ$ roll/pitch and heave) under pixel noise, ground effect, delay, and actuator saturation yield a $25/25$ landing rate with worst-case horizontal error below $3.1~\text{cm}$. A 30-gain deep sweep, a $\pm 40\%$ speed-envelope study, and head-to-head comparison against four recent baselines~\cite{lin2022,zhang2026,chen2025,cho2022} confirm the proposed framework is the only method to achieve sub-decimetre horizontal touchdown on every moving trajectory (Sections~\ref{multi init: subsec}--\ref{comparison: subsec}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{CONCLUSION}
\label{conclusion: section}
This paper addressed the problem of guaranteeing a soft precise landing of a quadrotor on a moving target from monocular vision alone. The premise of the paper is that visibility and soft touchdown are, in closed-loop terms, \emph{two independent funnel constraints}: one on the image-plane error to keep the target in the camera field of view, and one on the optic-flow error to drive the descent velocity to zero as the altitude vanishes. Treating them as a single constraint --- the path taken by virtually all prior image-based landing work --- is what forces existing controllers to rely on an external altitude estimate, a pre-identified aerodynamic model, or both.

The methodological answer proposed here is the dual-funnel DF-ASMC framework. Its novelty is architectural on two axes. First, two prescribed-performance funnels are coupled through a barrier-transformed reference generator so that the visibility funnel synthesises a feasible optic-flow command that the optic-flow funnel then drives to zero. Second, the entire outer loop is driven from a single monocular camera stream: optic flow supplies the translational feedback in place of metric depth, and the image-plane orientation of the target supplies the yaw reference in place of a magnetic compass, removing the two external sensing dependencies --- a calibrated range sensor and a magnetometer --- that every existing image-based landing controller retains. The optic-flow funnel is stabilised by a leakage-type adaptive sliding mode law whose switching gain is identified online, requires no \emph{a priori} bound on the lumped uncertainty, and provably remains bounded under persistent measurement noise. The funnel saturation guard on the optic-flow barrier transformation eliminates the numerical pole that afflicts conventional prescribed-performance implementations, and an underactuated attitude-cone projection carries the loop through the actuator-saturation nonlinearity without breaking the Lyapunov argument. The entire construction is certified by a single layered Lyapunov analysis that propagates the inner-loop GUUB statement backward through the reference generator, thereby establishing --- in one theorem --- visibility, soft touchdown, and disturbance rejection under actuator saturation, ground effect, pixel noise, and computational delay.

Numerical validation on twenty-five deterministic runs spanning five initial conditions and five target trajectories, including two ship-deck cases with heave, roll, and pitch oscillations, confirms the predictions of the theory: the closed loop lands in every run, the funnel and cone-clamp activation rates stay below one percent even under ship-deck motion, and the achieved touchdown error is an order of magnitude tighter than the prescribed precision bound. A cross-trajectory deep sweep, a target-speed envelope study, and a head-to-head comparison against four recent baselines further quantify the robustness, the admissible target-speed band, and the performance margin of the proposed framework --- confirming in particular that DF-ASMC is the only controller in the compared set to simultaneously satisfy the visibility, soft-touchdown, and landing-time thresholds on every moving trajectory.

The principal limitation of the present study is that validation is restricted to high-fidelity numerical simulation; hardware-in-the-loop and outdoor flight experiments on the X500 platform are the natural next step. Beyond experimental validation, two methodological extensions look particularly promising. The first is to generalise the dual-funnel construction to obstacle-aware and multi-target terminal landing by adding a third constraint layer on a free-space image-plane region, which the same layered Lyapunov machinery can in principle accommodate. The second is to deploy the rate-setpoint interface of DF-ASMC directly on top of a PX4 inner loop so that the Lyapunov analysis of Section~\ref{stability: section} transfers verbatim from the simulated geometric attitude tracker to the commercial autopilot, bridging the gap between a provable soft-landing certificate and a production-grade avionics stack.
