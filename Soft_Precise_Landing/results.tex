
\section{NUMERICAL SIMULATION RESULTS}
\label{experimentation: section}
Simulations are carried out in MATLAB using a fifth-order Runge--Kutta integrator at $\Delta t=0.01~\text{s}$ on the full thirteen-state quadrotor model of Section~\ref{background: section}. Platform parameters (X500 class) are listed in Table~\ref{uav params: table} and the locked DF-ASMC gains, obtained via the cross-trajectory deep sweep, in Table~\ref{control params: table}. A run terminates when the altitude first falls below $z_\text{f}=0.20~\text{m}$. The touchdown is \emph{precise} when $\|\,^\mathcal{I}\!e_{xy}\|\le 0.05~\text{m}$ and \emph{soft} when $\|v_\text{rel}\|\le 0.20~\text{m/s}$; the looser $xy_\text{f}=0.30~\text{m}$ threshold in Table~\ref{uav params: table} is used only for the cross-controller comparison of Section~\ref{comparison: subsec}. On a single core of an Intel i7, one DF-ASMC control update executes in $\approx 0.18~\text{ms}$, well within the $10~\text{ms}$ control period of the X500 platform.

Each of the above initial conditions is evaluated against five target trajectory cases defined as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Case 1 (Static):}  $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{r}_\text{t} = [0,\,0,\,0]^\top$~m.
    \item \textit{Case 2 (Linear, ship deck):}  a north--east surge $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{v}_{\text{t},xy} = [1.0,\,1.0]^\top$~m/s (speed $1.414~\text{m/s}$) superimposed with a heave sinusoid in the style of~\cite{lin2022} $\,^\mathcal{I}z_\text{t}(t)=0.2\sin(0.5\,t)~\text{m}$ and a large sea-state attitude oscillation $(\phi_\text{t},\theta_\text{t})=(15^\circ\sin(0.9\,t),\,8^\circ\sin(0.6\,t+\pi/3))$, $\psi_\text{t}=0$.
    \item \textit{Case 3 (Sinusoidal):}  $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{r}_\text{t} = [0.5\sin(0.8\,t),\,0.3\,t,\,0]^\top$~m, i.e., fast longitudinal oscillation combined with lateral drift (no deck attitude).
    \item \textit{Case 4 (Lissajous):} $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{r}_\text{t} = [0.5\sin(-0.8\,t),\,0.8\sin(0.4\,t),\,0]^\top$~m, producing a closed two-frequency figure-of-eight trajectory with asymmetric frequencies (no deck attitude).
    \item \textit{Case 5 (Circular, ship deck):} turning radius $r=0.5~\text{m}$ and angular rate $\omega_z = 0.3~\text{rad/s}$ with yaw $\psi_\text{t}=\omega_z t$; the same heave $0.2\sin(0.5\,t)~\text{m}$ and $(15^\circ,\,8^\circ)$ roll/pitch oscillation as Case~2 are added on top of the circular surge.
\end{enumerate}
Cases~2 and~5 are ship-deck scenarios with roll/pitch amplitudes and periods bracketing a substantially harsher envelope than the heave-only ``oscillating platform'' of~\cite{lin2022}.

\subsection{Performance under Model Uncertainty and Exogenous Perturbations}
\label{uncertainty: subsec}
The following realistic perturbations are injected: (i) pixel-level AWGN on $\,^\mathcal{C}\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}$ at $50$~dB SNR; (ii) the standard rotor-in-ground-effect thrust amplification \cite{sanchez-cuevas2017} $\,^\mathcal{B}T_\infty=[1-(R/4\,^\mathcal{I}z_\text{t})^2]^{-1}\,^\mathcal{B}T_u$; (iii) a one-step transport delay ($0.01~\text{s}$) on $[\,^\mathcal{B}T_u,\,^\mathcal{B}\boldsymbol{\tau}_u^\top]^\top$ and a $30~\text{Hz}$ zero-order hold on the image features; (iv) the visibility and optic-flow funnels $\boldsymbol{p}_1(t),\boldsymbol{p}_2(t)$ of \eqref{prescribed perfromance: equation}; and (v) the thrust saturation $0\le\,^\mathcal{B}T_u\le 60~\text{N}$ and the $35^\circ$ attitude-cone clamp on $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{a}_\text{cd}$. Under the idealised (disturbance-free) setting DF-ASMC lands from all $25$ conditions with monotonic descent and feature points strictly inside $\boldsymbol{p}_1(t)$; per-trajectory figure sets are in Sections~S1--S2 of the supplement. Under the full disturbance set DF-ASMC still achieves a soft precise landing in every case; the most demanding --- Circular ship-deck --- is shown in Fig.~\ref{Circular_3D: figure}, and closed-loop internals (funnels, sliding surface $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, adaptive gains $\boldsymbol{\kappa}$, cone-constrained acceleration) for the representative sinusoidal IC$4$ case are in Section~S3 (Figs.~S21--S25).

DF-ASMC achieves $25/25$ successful landings with mean landing time in $8.4$--$9.0~\text{s}$ and worst-case horizontal error below $3.1~\text{cm}$ (and below $1.2~\text{cm}$ on Static, Linear, and Circular), even under the ship-deck $\pm 15^\circ/\pm 8^\circ$ roll/pitch oscillation on the Linear and Circular cases. The worst-case horizontal error grows with the lateral excursion of the target (Lissajous $>$ Sinusoidal $>$ Linear $>$ Circular $\approx$ Static), consistent with the feed-forward lag being proportional to the target acceleration.

The locked gain set of Table~\ref{control params: table} is the outcome of a cross-trajectory deep sweep (each of the $30$ DF-ASMC parameters perturbed over $\{\times 0.5,\times 0.75,\times 1.25,\times 1.5\}$ on all four moving trajectories and all five initial conditions; $2400$ deterministic runs). Only two knobs survive as strict Pareto winners --- $\boldsymbol{p}_{2_0}(3)$ and $K_{\zeta d}$ --- whose combined lock-in reduces the aggregate landing time by $4.8\%$ and the aggregate worst-case horizontal error by $35.0\%$ while preserving the $20/20$ land rate; the remaining $28$ parameters partition into fragile, dead, and Pareto-trade classes. The full methodology, summary table, and per-parameter classification are given in Section~S4 of the supplementary material.

\subsection{Target Speed Envelope}
\label{speed: subsec}
With IC $[0,0,-5]~\text{m}$ and all gains fixed, a symmetric multiplicative speed scale $\lambda\in\{0.6,0.8,1.0,1.2,1.4\}$ is applied to every moving trajectory in Section~\ref{test conditions: subsec}, acting directly on the velocity parameters of $^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{x}_\text{t}(t)$. The resulting landing time, horizontal touchdown error, and relative speed at touchdown are collected in Fig.~\ref{speed envelope: figure}.

All $20$ runs are simultaneously \emph{precise} ($xy_\text{e}\le 0.05~\text{m}$) and \emph{soft} ($\|\boldsymbol{v}_\text{rel}\|\le 0.20~\text{m/s}$), establishing a $\pm 40\%$ guaranteed soft-precise envelope around every nominal trajectory without retuning. Worst-case across the sweep: $3.92~\text{cm}$ and $0.165~\text{m/s}$ (both on Sinusoidal, $\lambda=1.4$). Outside $\lambda\in[0.6,1.4]$ the failure mode is always $35^\circ$ attitude-cone saturation of $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{a}_\text{cd}$, confirming that the closed loop is limited by the platform's underactuation rather than by the adaptation law or the funnels.

\subsection{Comparison with State-of-the-Art Landing Controllers}
\label{comparison: subsec}
DF-ASMC is compared against four representative landing controllers: the performance-constrained PBVS of \cite{lin2022}, the PBVS with adaptive extended disturbance observer of \cite{zhang2026}, the IBVS with adaptive observer of \cite{chen2025}, and the feed-forward IBVS of \cite{cho2022}. All benchmarks use a common geometric SO(3) inner loop ($k_R=\text{diag}([0.8,0.8,0.4])$, $k_\Omega=\text{diag}([0.15,0.15,0.08])$) tuned to the X500 $10~\text{rad/s}$ roll/pitch bandwidth. The PBVS methods receive position/velocity noise ($\sigma_\text{pos}=0.01~\text{m}$, $\sigma_\text{vel}=0.02~\text{m/s}$), while the IBVS methods (DF-ASMC, \cite{chen2025}, \cite{cho2022}) receive pixel-level $50~\text{dB}$ AWGN. Ground effect, delay, and actuator constraints are identical for all five. IC is fixed at IC$4=[2,2,-5]~\text{m}$ and each trajectory is run for up to $40~\text{s}$. Each baseline's outer-loop gains were first initialised from the source paper and then retuned on the Sinusoidal trajectory under the full disturbance set, so the benchmarks compete from a tuning state at least as favourable as DF-ASMC, which uses the single locked set of Table~\ref{control params: table}. Two baselines require external signals absent in a vision-only harness --- the target-velocity feed-forward of \cite{lin2022} (Remark~2 therein) and the GPS/EKF ship-velocity channel of \cite{cho2022} --- and the resulting gap is discussed per-controller below. Table~\ref{comparison table: table} reports landing time, final horizontal error, and final altitude; runs that fail to touch down within $40~\text{s}$ are marked $\dagger$.

The comparison establishes the following observations.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The proposed DF-ASMC is the \emph{only} controller that achieves a sub-$0.05~\text{m}$ horizontal touchdown error on every trajectory. Every DF-ASMC cell in Table~\ref{comparison table: table} simultaneously satisfies the altitude, horizontal-error, and landing-budget thresholds, with the worst-case horizontal error ($0.023~\text{m}$ on the Sinusoidal) an order of magnitude below the $0.30~\text{m}$ prescribed tolerance.
    \item The four benchmarks each fail on one specific structural ground: \cite{lin2022} halts its descent to defend a monotonically shrinking funnel against a non-zero lateral drift, \cite{zhang2026} free-falls after its AEDO differentiates stationary measurement noise into a saturating force command, \cite{chen2025} violates its own constant-$z^\star$ gain condition as the altitude approaches zero, and \cite{cho2022} loses lateral authority once its GPS/EKF target-velocity feed-forward channel is denied in the vision-only harness. The per-baseline diagnoses, with the exact equations from each source that break under our disturbance profile, are collected in Section~S5 (and Fig.~S26) of the supplementary material.
    \item Collectively, the proposed DF-ASMC strictly dominates the four benchmarks on every trajectory in both the landing-time and horizontal-error metrics whenever the benchmark actually lands, and is the only controller that reliably touches down on all five trajectories. The simultaneous combination of scale independence, sub-decimeter horizontal precision, and guaranteed funnel adherence is not achieved by any competitor.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Summary of Numerical Observations}
The numerical study summarized in Sections~\ref{multi init: subsec}--\ref{comparison: subsec} supports the following conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The locked DF-ASMC achieves $25/25$ successful landings across five trajectories and five initial conditions under pixel noise, ground effect, computational delay, and actuator constraints, with mean landing time $\le 9.0~\text{s}$ and worst-case horizontal error $\le 3.1~\text{cm}$ — including ship-deck roll/pitch oscillation and~\cite{lin2022}-style heave on the Linear and Circular cases.
    \item A cross-trajectory deep sweep of $30$ gains $\times$ $4$ perturbations $\times$ $4$ trajectories $\times$ $5$ ICs identifies only two parameters ($\boldsymbol{p}_{2_0}(3)$, $K_{\zeta d}$) as real Pareto winners, whose combined lock-in cuts the aggregate landing time by $4.8\%$ and the aggregate worst-case horizontal error by $35\%$; all other knobs are either fragile, dead, or trade-offs. The locked gain set is therefore a justified design point on the true Pareto frontier of the closed loop.
    \item Across the symmetric speed-envelope sweep $\lambda\in[0.6,1.4]$ on all four moving trajectories, every run is simultaneously \emph{precise} ($xy_\text{e}\le 0.05~\text{m}$) and \emph{soft} ($\|\boldsymbol{v}_\text{rel}\|\le 0.20~\text{m/s}$), establishing a $\pm 40\%$ band of guaranteed soft-precise touchdown around every nominal trajectory; at the eventual envelope boundary the failure mode is always attitude-cone saturation rather than the adaptive law.
    \item Under identical disturbance conditions, the proposed DF-ASMC dominates four representative state-of-the-art controllers~\cite{lin2022,zhang2026,chen2025,cho2022} in both landing time and horizontal touchdown precision on all four moving trajectories.
\end{enumerate}
