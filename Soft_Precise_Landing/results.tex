
\section{NUMERICAL SIMULATION RESULTS}
\label{experimentation: section}
Simulations use an RK5 integrator at $\Delta t=0.01~\text{s}$ on the thirteen-state quadrotor model of Section~\ref{background: section}, with the image-based loop tracking $N=4$ feature points. Platform parameters (X500 class) are in Table~\ref{uav params: table}; the locked MDF-ASMC gains are in Table~S1 of the supplement. With landing-gear height $h_\text{lg}=0.20~\text{m}$, a run terminates at landing-gear contact when the UAV body altitude above the target first falls below $h_\text{lg}$. The landing gear also keeps $\,^\mathcal{V}z_\text{t}\ge h_\text{lg}$, structurally bounding $\beta=1/\,^\mathcal{V}z_\text{t}$ by $\beta_\text{max}=1/h_\text{lg}$, which provides a concrete value for the existence bound assumed in Assumption~1. Define the terminal horizontal error $r_{xy,\text{f}} \triangleq \|\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{r}_{\text{t/b},xy}(t_\text{f})\|$, terminal relative speed $v_\text{f} \triangleq \|\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{v}_{\text{t/b}}(t_\text{f})\|$, and terminal above-target gap $r_{z,\text{f}} \triangleq |\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{r}_{\text{t/b},z}(t_\text{f})|$. Per Table~\ref{uav params: table}, a touchdown is \emph{precise} when $r_{xy,\text{f}}$ is bounded, \emph{soft} when $v_\text{f}$ is bounded, and \emph{soft-precise} when both hold.

Each IC is evaluated against five target trajectories:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Case 1 (Static):}  $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{r}_\text{t} = [0,\,0,\,0]^\top$~m.
    \item \textit{Case 2 (Linear, ship deck):} $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{v}_{\text{t},xy} = [1.0,\,1.0]^\top$~m/s with heave $\,^\mathcal{I}z_\text{t}(t)=0.2\sin(0.5\,t)$~m~\cite{lin2022} and deck oscillation $(\phi_\text{t},\theta_\text{t})=(15^\circ\sin(0.9\,t),\,8^\circ\sin(0.6\,t+\pi/3))$, $\psi_\text{t}=0$.
    \item \textit{Case 3 (Sinusoidal):} $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{r}_\text{t} = [0.5\sin(0.8\,t),\,0.3\,t,\,0]^\top$~m.
    \item \textit{Case 4 (Lissajous):} $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{r}_\text{t} = [0.4\sin(-0.8\,t),\,0.8\sin(0.4\,t),\,0]^\top$~m.
    \item \textit{Case 5 (Circular, ship deck):} $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{r}_{\text{t},xy} = [-r(\cos\omega_z t -1),\,r\sin\omega_z t]^\top$~m with $r=0.5$~m, $\omega_z = 0.25$~rad/s, $\psi_\text{t}=\omega_z t$; same heave and deck oscillation as Case~2.
\end{enumerate}
Cases~2 and~5 bracket a harsher ship-deck envelope than the heave-only platform of~\cite{lin2022}.

The locked gain set (Table~S1) meets the precise and soft thresholds across all 33 axes of a cross-trajectory deep sweep ($3325$ deterministic runs over $\{\times 0.5,\times 0.75,\times 1.25,\times 1.5\}$ perturbations of the tunable MDF-ASMC gains and two closed-loop structural knobs). The per-axis methodology is in Section~S3-A.

MDF-ASMC achieves $25/25$ soft-precise landings under the disturbance model, with mean landing time $\le 18.2~\text{s}$ and worst-case horizontal error of $5.7~\text{cm}$. The funnel-margin cone clamp records zero FoV bound violations and is active for less than $1\%$ of control steps; worst-case physical-pixel excursions are $|\,^\mathcal{C}\hat{x}|_{\max}=134$~px and $|\,^\mathcal{C}\hat{y}|_{\max}=115$~px, well within the $\mathcal{R}/2=\pm[160,120]$~px FoV bound.

A target-speed sweep then applies a symmetric multiplicative scale $\lambda\in\{0.6,0.8,1.0,1.2,1.4\}$ to every moving trajectory (Cases~2--5) from IC$_1=[0,0,-5]~\text{m}$ with the same locked gains. The resulting $4\times 5=20$ runs all yield soft-precise touchdowns (Fig.~\ref{speed envelope: figure}), establishing a $\pm 40\%$ guaranteed soft-precise envelope \emph{without retuning}. Outside the $[0.6,1.4]$ band the $\theta_\text{cap}=60^\circ$ ceiling becomes the binding constraint and the airframe runs out of lateral acceleration; the dual-funnel certificates of Theorem~1 and Corollary~1 still hold, but the cone-clamped lateral acceleration is no longer enough to keep the chase converging. The per-axis breakdown is in Section~S3-C.

The robustness across both ICs and target speeds traces to four design features. Theorem~1 makes the optic-flow funnel a time-varying envelope that absorbs any $\boldsymbol{h}_\text{e}(0)$ inside $\boldsymbol{p}_{2_0}$ and contracts as $z\to h_\text{lg}$. Corollary~1 holds the features inside the FoV through the funnel-margin cone clamp regardless of IC or target speed. The depth-aware $\beta=1/\,^\mathcal{V}z_\text{t}$ scales the funnel sharpness with altitude, and the leakage-type adaptive gain (Remark~2) absorbs unknown disturbance bounds without \emph{a priori} knowledge; together with the resolution-invariant normalization of Remark~1 and the linearity established in Remark~3, these features remove the need for IC-specific or speed-specific retuning. The same sweep on the four baselines (Section~\ref{experimentation: section}-\ref{comparison: subsec}) yields $0/80$ soft-precise touchdowns.

The comparison establishes the following observations.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The proposed MDF-ASMC is the \emph{only} controller that reaches the target surface on every trajectory (Table~\ref{comparison: table}, Fig.~\ref{comparison combined: figure}). Every MDF-ASMC cell simultaneously satisfies the altitude, horizontal-error, and time-limit thresholds, with the worst-case horizontal error ($0.021~\text{m}$ on Case~4) well inside the $0.08~\text{m}$ \emph{precise} threshold.
    \item Each baseline fails on a \emph{structural} ground that within-framework retuning cannot close. \cite{lin2022} saturates its single PPC barrier under mean wind and mean-target motion, forcing image features outside the camera FoV within $3.5$--$16.4~\text{s}$ on every trajectory, whereas the funnel-margin cone clamp of MDF-ASMC averts the saturation kinematically. \cite{zhang2026} reaches the target surface but produces terminal lateral errors of $0.17$--$0.24~\text{m}$ on the four moving cases, exceeding the $0.08~\text{m}$ precise bound by $2$--$3\times$, and on Case~5 the same fixed-bandwidth descent also drives $v_\text{f}=0.65~\text{m/s}$ above the soft bound, whereas the depth-aware optic-flow funnel of MDF-ASMC accelerates convergence as $z\to h_\text{lg}$ and keeps terminal lateral errors within the precise bound. \cite{chen2025} amplifies depth-dependent pixel noise through a finite-difference image-moment derivative, stalling $\approx 1.08~\text{m}$ above the target surface on all five cases, whereas MDF-ASMC controls optic flow directly and avoids image-moment differentiation. \cite{cho2022} is unable to decouple its pseudo-inverse lateral velocity command from its adaptive altitude gate, triggering an immediate feature-visibility break on every trajectory, whereas MDF-ASMC's outer loop produces $\,^\mathcal{I}\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}$ and $\,^\mathcal{I}a_{\text{d},z}$ through independent feedback paths rather than pseudo-inverse decomposition. Per-trajectory 3-D plots and per-baseline diagnoses are in Sections~S3-D and S3-E of the supplement, with the per-baseline mechanism contrast against MDF-ASMC summarized in Table~S3.
    \item These pathologies persist across the same $\lambda$-sweep from IC$_1$. Across the $80$ baseline runs the soft-precise count is $0/80$ (Fig.~\ref{comparison multi speed circular: figure} for Case~5; Section~S3-D for Cases~2--4). \cite{zhang2026} reaches the target surface on every $(\text{traj},\lambda)$ pair but with terminal lateral errors of $0.05$--$0.47~\text{m}$, soft-precise on at most $1/20$ runs; on Case~5 the soft bound also breaks, with $v_\text{f}$ scaling from $0.23$ to $0.88$~m/s over $\lambda\in\{0.6,\ldots,1.4\}$. \cite{lin2022,chen2025,cho2022} each break feature visibility well above the target surface at every $\lambda$, with terminal altitudes of $1.3$--$5.1$~m. MDF-ASMC, by contrast, delivers $20/20$ soft-precise touchdowns over the same $\lambda$ range (Fig.~\ref{speed envelope: figure}), confirming that the speed-robustness gap is structural. The dual-funnel architecture supplies a unified visibility-and-depth-aware mechanism that no single baseline matches.
\end{enumerate}
