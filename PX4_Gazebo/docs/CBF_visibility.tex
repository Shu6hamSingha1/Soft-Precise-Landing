\noindent\emph{Scope and notation.} This note follows the notation of the manuscript's Visibility CBF
subsection and Implementation Appendix verbatim (frames $\Iframe$/$\Cframe$, feature points
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$, coupling $\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}=L_\omega M$, lean $\boldsymbol\theta$, cap
$\theta_\text{cap}$, drift $\boldsymbol{d}_i$, look-ahead $\tau$) and extends it with one additional
symbol, $A_\text{cap}$, the vehicle's measured maximum thrust-to-mass ratio. Visibility is fundamentally
an \emph{attitude} constraint (the body tilt shifts the FoV), but the attitude actually commanded is a
byproduct of the desired inertial acceleration $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$ the outer loop
produces. Rather than solving the tilt-only projection and separately deciding how much vertical thrust
to spend, this implementation solves one projection directly over $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$
that jointly enforces (i) the barrier condition of the manuscript's Visibility Forward Invariance
theorem and (ii) the deliverable-lateral-acceleration bound of the manuscript's Property~1, in a single
iteration rather than a tilt projection followed by a separate deliverability cap. The coupling itself is
unchanged: the \textbf{rotational part of the IBVS interaction matrix}, $L_\omega$, evaluated at the
measured image point --- exact, \emph{depth-independent}, no de-rotation or virtual frame required.

\section{Barrier on the real camera plane}
Per image axis $k$, for feature point $i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barrier}
\boxed{\,h_{i,k}=\varphi_{\text{max},k}-|\tilde r_{i,k}|\,}.
\end{equation}
$h_{i,k}\ge0\iff$ feature point $i$ is on the sensor on axis $k$. $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$ is the
\emph{measured} camera-frame feature point --- nothing is de-rotated. This implementation tracks a
single feature point (the target's decoded centroid), i.e.\ $N=1$ in the manuscript's general
per-feature barrier.

\section{Tilt$\to$feature coupling, expressed in acceleration units}
A camera rotation moves a feature point by the rotational part of the IBVS interaction matrix, a standard
and \textbf{depth-independent} object evaluated at the measured point:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lw}
\dot{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}_i=L_\omega(\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i)\,\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm rp}+\boldsymbol{d}_i,\qquad
L_\omega=\begin{bmatrix} xy & -(1+x^2)\\ 1+y^2 & -xy\end{bmatrix}_{(x,y)=\tilde{\boldsymbol r}_i}.
\end{equation}
$L_\omega$ contains \textbf{no depth}. The drift is measured directly by stripping the rotational term
from the observed feature velocity, $\boldsymbol{d}_i=\dot{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}_{i,\text{obs}}-L_\omega\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm rp}^{\rm curr}$
(using the true gyro rate), so it carries only genuine target motion, not our own tilting.

A commanded lean produces a rotation about the perpendicular axis, $\boldsymbol\omega=M\boldsymbol\theta$,
and the lean is itself the direction of the commanded lateral acceleration relative to the commanded
vertical acceleration, $\boldsymbol\theta=R_z(-\psi_\text{b})(\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}/\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z})$
(the manuscript's own definition, with $\psi_\text{b}$ composed here with the fixed camera-mount
alignment rotation this platform's downward-mounted camera requires; the composition reduces to
$R_z(-\psi_\text{b})$ alone for an ideally body-aligned camera). Writing $P$ for this composed rotation and
substituting both relations into \eqref{eq:Lw} gives the feature velocity directly as a function of the
acceleration command:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N-def}
\boldsymbol\omega=M\boldsymbol\theta,\qquad
\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}\boldsymbol\theta=\underbrace{\Big(\frac{\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}\,P}{a_z}\Big)}_{\textstyle N_i(a_z)}\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}.
\end{equation}
$N_i(a_z)=\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}P/a_z$ is the barrier's Jacobian with respect to the lateral acceleration
directly. It depends on $a_z$ --- the same quantity the joint projection below also solves for --- which
is exactly the coupling that projection is built to resolve, rather than treating $a_z$ as an external
given. For a finite acceleration change the exact map is the rotation homography of the measured point;
the first-order form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lin}
\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i(\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy})\approx\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i+N_i(a_z)\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}-\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}\boldsymbol\theta_\text{curr}
\end{equation}
is the local linearization, accurate over one control step. $\boldsymbol\theta_\text{curr}$ is read
directly from the realized attitude (not from $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$), so it needs no
rotation from acceleration units.

\section{The visibility--deliverability projection}\label{sec:qp}
Two physical constraints bind the same variable $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$: the barrier condition
of \eqref{eq:barrier} along the feature response \eqref{eq:Lw}--\eqref{eq:N-def}, and the
deliverable-lateral-acceleration bound of the manuscript's Property~1 (inner-loop deliverable-tilt
saturation bounds $\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}\|$ by a state-dependent fraction of
$|\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}|$). Writing $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$ for the conditioned
output (paralleling the manuscript's $\boldsymbol\theta^\star$ notation for its own tilt-only projection),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qp}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star=\arg\min_{\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}}\ &\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}-\,^\Iframe\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}_\text{d}\|^2 \\[2pt]
\text{s.t.}\ \
\big|\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i+N_i(a_z)\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}&-\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}\boldsymbol\theta_\text{curr}+\tau\boldsymbol{d}_i\big|\preceq\boldsymbol\varphi_\text{max},
\qquad \big\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}+g\boldsymbol{e}_3\big\|\le A_\text{cap}
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
where $\,^\Iframe\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}_\text{d}$ is the filtered command of the manuscript's low-pass
filter (Implementation Appendix), the unconstrained target this projection stays closest to. The first
constraint is the manuscript's per-feature FoV box (its Eq.~13, Implementation Appendix), now written directly
on $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}$; the second is the true thrust-deliverability sphere in
gravity-shifted acceleration space (the vehicle's total thrust vector is
$m(\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}+g\boldsymbol{e}_3)$, and
$\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}+g\boldsymbol{e}_3\|\le A_\text{cap}$ is exactly the statement that
this stays within the measured maximum). Both are convex for a fixed $a_z$; since $N_i(a_z)$ itself
depends on $a_z$, the two projections are iterated together (alternating projection onto the box at the
current $a_z$ estimate, then onto the sphere, updating $a_z$ for the next pass) until convergence. This
is a genuine joint solve for the lateral and vertical acceleration together, not a lateral solve followed
by a separate vertical adjustment.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Directional} --- only the outward-binding box row is active; inward (recovery) and
tangential accelerations are free. No strangling, matching the manuscript's own $\dot h_{i,k}$ argument.
\item \textbf{Envelope-aware} --- the deliverability side of \eqref{eq:qp} is the vehicle's actual measured
thrust ceiling $A_\text{cap}$, not $\theta_\text{cap}$ applied \emph{as if} $a_z$ were fixed at hover; it
tightens or loosens correctly as $a_z$ moves away from hover.
\item \textbf{Forward-invariant} --- the look-ahead shifts the box anchor by $\tau\boldsymbol{d}_i$, so the
projection accounts for centroid drift over the next $\tau$\,s in both phases (Sect.~\ref{sec:twophase}),
exactly as in the manuscript's realization.
\item \textbf{Bounded by construction} --- because $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$ always
satisfies the sphere constraint, the derived lean
$\boldsymbol\theta^\star=P\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}^\star/a_z^\star$ used to build attitude
(Sect.~\ref{sec:impl}) satisfies $\|\boldsymbol\theta^\star\|\le\tan\theta_\text{cap}$ at the solved
$a_z^\star$ automatically; no separate, hover-assumed cap application is needed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Two-phase $\delta$ (central-marker convention)}\label{sec:twophase}
The designated \emph{central} marker is the target. $\boldsymbol\varphi_\text{max}$ in \eqref{eq:qp} is
phase-dependent:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Phase 1 --- central marker decodes.}
$\delta_k=0$, so the box uses the full half-FoV $\boldsymbol\varphi_\text{max}=\mathcal{R}/(2f)$ (this
implementation does not apply the manuscript's inset margin $\delta_m=15$~px$/f$; see the note at the end
of this section). The barrier is centroid-only: the box spans the entire image plane, and only fires if
the \emph{centroid} drifts toward the edge. The central marker is deliberately allowed to grow and
overflow as the UAV closes in --- its visibility \emph{is} the guarantee; using its large half-extent
would fire the barrier early. $\dot\delta$ is tracked but excluded from $\boldsymbol\varphi_\text{max}$ in
Phase~1.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Phase 2 --- central marker overflows} (decode-fail, hysteresis-gated against flicker
$\Rightarrow$ touchdown range), matching the manuscript's ``on a marker decode failure the projection
falls back to a magnitude clamp $\|\,^\Iframe\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\text{d},xy}\|\le|\,^\Iframe\tilde a_{\text{d},z}|\tan\theta_\text{cap}$.''
$\delta_\text{eff}$ \textbf{ramps in} over a few frames from the visible-\emph{set} half-extent
($\tfrac12\mathrm{ptp}_k$ over ALL visible markers' corners, so $|\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i|+\delta_\text{eff}$
is the true outermost feature --- not one marker):
\[
  \boldsymbol\varphi_{\text{max},k} \to \varphi_{\text{max},k} - \delta_{\text{eff},k} - \tau\dot\delta_{\text{eff},k}.
\]
The ramp (not a step) keeps $h_{i,k}$ from dropping discontinuously at engagement, but the ramp gain is
clamped so $\delta_\text{eff}$ never \emph{lags} the measured $\tfrac12\mathrm{ptp}$ --- a ramp slower
than the fill under-protects. Within Phase~2 the visible markers themselves leave (a mild inner
sawtooth); $\dot\delta$ is reset across those switches. The sphere constraint of \eqref{eq:qp} applies
unchanged in both phases; the deliverability side of the joint solve does not depend on which phase the
visibility side is in.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Implementation:} hysteresis threshold~$=3$ consecutive decode-fails before Phase~2
activates; ramp completes over~$5$ control cycles (${\approx}120$\,ms at 42\,Hz).
Both $\delta_\text{eff}=\alpha\,\delta_\text{ref}$ and $\dot\delta_\text{eff}=\alpha\,\dot\delta_\text{ref}$
are scaled by the same ramp factor~$\alpha\in[0,1]$, where $\delta_\text{ref}$ and $\dot\delta_\text{ref}$
are the half-extent and loom rate measured at the last successful decode.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Note on $\delta_m$:} the manuscript's inset half-extent
$\boldsymbol\varphi_\text{max}=\mathcal{R}/(2f)-\delta_m$ absorbs the one-step linearization residual and
the attitude-tracking residual (the robustness margin $\delta_m\ge\bar{d}$ of the Visibility Forward
Invariance theorem). This deployed implementation currently uses the raw $\mathcal{R}/(2f)$ without
subtracting $\delta_m$ --- a known gap against the manuscript's stated margin, not yet closed in code.

\section{Feasibility floor and hand-off}
The box constraint alone is \textbf{infeasible} iff $\varphi_{\text{max},k}<0$ for some~$k$ under Phase~2
margins (the marker fills the frame within $\tau$). In Phase~1, $\boldsymbol\varphi_\text{max}=\mathcal{R}/(2f)>0$
always, so the box is \textbf{always feasible} on its own while the central marker decodes. The sphere
constraint is always feasible by construction (it is satisfied by
$\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}=\boldsymbol{0}$). Genuine joint infeasibility --- the box demanding more
lateral acceleration than $A_\text{cap}$ can deliver at any achievable $a_z$ --- is the acceleration-space
counterpart of the manuscript's feasibility condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feasibility}
\sigma_{\min}(\tilde{L}_{\omega,i})\,(\tan\theta_\text{cap}-\|\boldsymbol\theta_\text{curr}\|)\ \ge\ \max_{i,k}\big(|\tilde r_{i,k}|-\varphi_{\text{max},k}\big)_+ + \bar{d}\quad\forall i,
\end{equation}
evaluated here at the solved $a_z^\star$ rather than at a fixed hover-assumed $\theta_\text{cap}$; when it
fails, the iteration converges to the point on the sphere closest (in the box-projection sense) to
feasibility, the same graceful degradation the manuscript's alternating projection already provides for
the box alone.

The perception-floor hand-off is triggered by \emph{central-marker overflow} (Phase~2 activation,
hysteresis-gated), not by box infeasibility. On Phase~2 entry, or if $\varphi_{\text{max},k}<0$ under
Phase~2 margins, raise the perception-floor flag and hand off to the texture-free ring flow.

\section{Assumptions (and the practical challenge each carries)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The coupling $\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}=L_\omega M$ and its sign convention are calibrated.} The
fixed $90^\circ$ map $\boldsymbol\omega=M\boldsymbol\theta$ bridges the lean direction and the
rotation-axis rate that $L_\omega$ couples to. Any residual mount-dependent sign of $L_\omega$ is a
one-time check: command a small acceleration and confirm the resulting $\Delta\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$
matches $\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}$ in sign before trusting the barrier.
\item \textbf{The conditioned acceleration is realized} (the SO(3) loop tracks the attitude built from
$\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$), per Assumption on the timescale separation between the outer
and inner loops. \emph{Challenge:} attitude bandwidth $\Rightarrow$ lag; the sphere constraint bounds
magnitude, not delay.
\item \textbf{The drift $\boldsymbol{d}_i$ is the translation part}
($\dot{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}_{i,\text{obs}}-L_\omega\boldsymbol\omega^{\rm curr}$) and $\dot\delta$ the
loom. \emph{Challenge:} finite differences $\Rightarrow$ filter + binding-axis hysteresis; the disturbance
enters as \emph{measured} motion, matching the manuscript's own treatment.
\item \textbf{First-order $L_\omega$ over the cycle.} Exact is the rotation homography of
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$; the linearization \eqref{eq:lin} is accurate over one control step.
\item \textbf{$A_\text{cap}$ is the vehicle's true achievable thrust-to-mass ratio}, measured rather than
assumed --- this instantiates the manuscript's Property~1 exactly, using a direct, always-current physical
bound in place of the fixed $\theta_\text{cap}=60^\circ$ nominal margin.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scale-freeness and depth-freeness}
The barrier \eqref{eq:barrier}, the coupling \eqref{eq:Lw}, and the projection \eqref{eq:qp} contain only
the measured image point $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$, $\delta$, $\boldsymbol\varphi_\text{max}=\mathcal{R}/(2f)$,
the body rate, the commanded acceleration, and the vehicle's own $A_\text{cap}$ and $g$.
\textbf{$L_\omega$ is depth-independent by construction}, and $A_\text{cap}$, $g$ are fixed
vehicle/physical constants, not measurements of target depth or altitude --- the whole formulation
remains manifestly scale- and depth-free, matching the manuscript's own scale-free design throughout.

\section{Implementation (per control cycle)}\label{sec:impl}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(once)} Calibrate $L_\omega$'s sign / axis convention (Assumption 1); measure $A_\text{cap}$.
\item Measure $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$ (board centroid, homography). Advance the two-phase $\delta$
state: if the central marker decodes, reset Phase~2 counters and set $\delta_\text{eff}=0$; otherwise
increment the decode-fail counter, and once the hysteresis threshold is reached, ramp $\delta_\text{eff}$
toward the visible markers' $\tfrac12\mathrm{ptp}$.
\item Evaluate $L_\omega(\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i)$; form the drift
$\boldsymbol{d}_i=\dot{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}_{i,\text{obs}}-L_\omega\boldsymbol\omega^{\rm curr}$ and
$\dot\delta_\text{eff}$ (filtered; zero in Phase~1). Read $\boldsymbol\theta_\text{curr}$ from the current
realized attitude.
\item Two-phase margin per Sect.~\ref{sec:twophase}. If $\varphi_{\text{max},k}<0$ for any $k$, raise the
perception-floor flag and hand off to the ring estimator. Otherwise build the joint projection
\eqref{eq:qp}.
\item Starting from $\,^\Iframe\tilde{\boldsymbol{a}}_\text{d}$ (the filtered desired acceleration, per the
manuscript's low-pass filter), jointly project onto the FoV box (using $N_i(a_z)$ at the current $a_z$
estimate) and the deliverability sphere, iterating until convergence, to obtain
$\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$.
\item \textbf{Build the desired attitude directly from $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$,}
mirroring the manuscript's own direct-lean construction. Recover
$\boldsymbol\theta^\star=P\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}^\star/a_z^\star$ and set the desired
body-$z$ axis $\boldsymbol{r}_{d,3}=\big[-R_z(\psi_\text{b})\boldsymbol\theta^\star,\,1\big]^\top/\big\|[\,\cdot\,,1]\big\|$,
passing $R_\text{d}$ to the SO(3) inner loop and taking the thrust magnitude
$\,^\mathcal{B}T_u=m\,|\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}^\star|/R_{33}$ directly from
$\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$, exactly as the manuscript's own $R_\text{d}$/thrust-magnitude
construction. Building attitude from the un-filtered $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$ (rather
than from a separately low-pass-filtered command) keeps the barrier's guarantee landing exactly on the
commanded attitude, with no smearing from a downstream filter time constant.
\end{enumerate}
