\noindent\emph{Purpose.} Keep the marker's measured centre inside the \emph{real} camera image plane,
with a fixed buffer from the field-of-view edge, by making the smallest change to the desired inertial
acceleration $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$ an outer loop produces. For the cross marker the
visibility target is a \emph{single point}, the crossed-lines intersection --- no multi-corner
containment, no marker-size term.

The command influences that point through two channels on different timescales, so it is conditioned in
two tiers rather than one problem:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Attitude (lean).} A body tilt rotates the field of view, so the projected centre shifts
\emph{within one control step}, and depth-independently. This channel carries a hard barrier
(\S\ref{sec:tier1}) --- the CBF.
\item \textbf{Descent rate.} $|\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}|\approx|\text{offset}|/Z$, which grows as the
altitude $Z$ shrinks. A change in the vertical command reaches the centre only two integrators later, so
descent is treated as a \emph{soft, predictive} influence: a governor (\S\ref{sec:tier2}) that slows the
descent when the centre is predicted to reach the buffer before the lateral loop can re-centre it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Barrier and safe set}\label{sec:barrier}
Per image axis $k$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barrier}
\boxed{\,h_k(\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}})=\varphi_k-|\tilde r_k|\,},
\qquad
\mathcal{S}=\{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}: h_k(\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}})\ge0,\ k=1,2\}.
\end{equation}
$\mathcal{S}$ is the closed rectangle $[-\varphi_1,\varphi_1]\times[-\varphi_2,\varphi_2]$: the set of centre
positions that lie on the sensor with at least the fractional margin $b$ to each edge. $\tilde{\boldsymbol
r}$ is the \emph{measured} centre --- nothing is de-rotated onto a virtual frame. $\boldsymbol\varphi$ is
a fixed camera constant. The buffer $b$ is the robustness margin of the barrier: it absorbs, in one
number, the intended visual margin, the one-step linearization residual of \eqref{eq:lin}, and residual
inner-loop attitude-tracking lag, so that a barrier value computed from the linear model is a lower bound
on the true one to within the design regime of \S\ref{sec:lin}.

\section{Feature response to the acceleration command}\label{sec:lin}
A camera rotation moves the centre by the rotational part of the IBVS interaction matrix, a standard
\emph{depth-independent} object evaluated at the measured point:
$\dot{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}=L_\omega\,\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm rp}+\boldsymbol{d}$, with
$\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm rp}$ the body roll/pitch rate.

The quantity a lateral-acceleration command sets is not a rotation rate but the underactuated
quadrotor's \emph{lean} --- the ratio of lateral to vertical thrust, expressed in image axes,
$\boldsymbol{y}=P(\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}/a_z)$. Producing a steady lean along $\boldsymbol{y}$
is a body rotation about the perpendicular axis, $\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm rp}=M\boldsymbol{y}$; a
world-fixed point's image moves opposite to that rotation, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lin}
\boldsymbol{c}_\text{next}(\boldsymbol{y})\;=\;
\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}\;+\;L_e\,(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{y}_\text{curr})\;+\;\tau\,\boldsymbol{d},
\qquad L_e=-(L_\omega M).
\end{equation}
The exact map of $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}$ under a finite lean change is the rotation homography of its
ray; \eqref{eq:lin} is its first-order term about the current lean, and the sign of $L_e$ is verified
against an independent pinhole-plus-attitude camera model (\texttt{tools/validate\_visibility\_projection.py}
check~1). $\boldsymbol{y}_\text{curr}$ is read from the realized attitude, so it needs no rotation from
acceleration units. The $\tau\boldsymbol{d}$ term extrapolates measured \emph{target} motion over the
horizon $\tau$ (\texttt{CBF\_DRIFT\_TAU}, default $0.15$\,s). Setting $\tau=0$ removes it and the QP is
purely reactive --- sufficient for a stationary marker (whose centre does not drift on its own),
incomplete for a moving one. At the default $\tau$ a stationary marker still sees a small lead
($\boldsymbol{d}$ carries the vehicle's own approach flow; conditioned $|\boldsymbol{d}|\!\sim\!0.1$,
so $\tau|\boldsymbol{d}|\!\sim\!0.02$ tangent) --- verified regression-free against $\tau=0$ on all
seven rover motion profiles, though the IC2--5 stationary gate itself was run at $\tau=0$.

\paragraph{The drift source $\boldsymbol{d}$.} $\boldsymbol{d}$ is taken from the perception front-end's
translational optic flow $\boldsymbol{h}_{xy}$ --- the de-rotated ($L_\omega\boldsymbol\omega$ already
removed), sensor-calibrated, filtered relative-velocity signal the middle loop already uses --- rather
than a bespoke differencing of $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}$. $\boldsymbol{h}_{xy}$ is \emph{already} in the
image-tangent frame of $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}$ (the front-end applies the camera-mount swap upstream):
the map is the identity, verified by regressing $\mathrm{d}\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}/\mathrm{d}t$ on
$\boldsymbol{h}_{xy}$ over a real approach ($+I$ cosine $0.87$; every other signed permutation
$\approx 0$). Raw $\boldsymbol{h}_{xy}$ is unusable as a lead --- on aggressive target trajectories the
$\boldsymbol{h}$ least-squares solve throws single-frame spikes of $|\boldsymbol{h}_{xy}|\sim 4$--$16$
(vs a real drift $\sim 0.1$), and on a stationary target it carries $\sim 0.1$--$2$ of pure self-motion
and sensor noise --- so $\boldsymbol{d}$ is conditioned before use (\texttt{condition\_drift}):
(i) the solve's own in-sample residual $\mathrm{rel\_resid}=\lVert A\hat s-b\rVert/\lVert b\rVert$ gates
the whole vector to $\boldsymbol{0}$ when it exceeds \texttt{CBF\_DRIFT\_RESID\_GATE} (or is non-finite);
(ii) a median-of-3 removes isolated spikes; (iii) a one-pole low-pass (\texttt{CBF\_DRIFT\_LPF\_ALPHA})
smooths it; (iv) a radial clamp bounds $|\boldsymbol{d}|\le$ \texttt{CBF\_DRIFT\_MAX} with direction
preserved. Finally the lead itself is clamped so a linear extrapolation never predicts the centre
crossing an axis origin --- beyond that the first-order model does not hold and the prediction would ask
for a reversed correction.

\paragraph{Validity envelope.} $L_e$ is evaluated at the measured $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}$ and held fixed
for the solve. The model error of \eqref{eq:lin} is under $8\%$ for a per-cycle lean \emph{change}
$\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{y}_\text{curr}\|\lesssim 15^\circ$. At the $50$\,Hz control rate with a
smooth outer loop this is the operating regime; a single cycle never demands a large lean change. Empirically
(4000 randomized geometries per bin): $0$ true-sensor exits at $\le15^\circ$/cycle; a $0.35\%$ rate at
$\sim24^\circ$/cycle with worst overshoot $+0.07$ tangent, well inside $b\,\mathcal{R}/(2f)$.

\section{Tier 1 --- the visibility CBF}\label{sec:tier1}
Each control step, given the outer loop's unconstrained command $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^{\,0}$
(hence $\boldsymbol{y}_\text{d}$) and the measurement $(\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}},\boldsymbol{d},R)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qp}
\boxed{\;
\begin{aligned}
(\boldsymbol{y}^\star,\boldsymbol{s}^\star)&=\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^2,\ \boldsymbol{s}\ge\boldsymbol{0}}\
\tfrac12\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{y}_\text{d}\|^2+\tfrac{\rho}{2}\|\boldsymbol{s}\|^2\\[2pt]
&\text{s.t.}\quad
\big|\,\boldsymbol{c}_\text{next}(\boldsymbol{y})\,\big|_k\le\varphi_k+s_k\ \ (k=1,2),
\qquad
\|\boldsymbol{y}\|\le y_\text{max}
\end{aligned}
\;}
\end{equation}
with $y_\text{max}=\sqrt{A_\text{cap}^2/a_z^2-1}$, after which
$\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}^{\star}=a_z\,P^{\!\top}\boldsymbol{y}^\star$ and the vertical
command $\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}$ is passed through unchanged. The visibility rows are affine in
$\boldsymbol{y}$ (gradient the fixed row $L_{e,k}$); their intersection is a convex polyhedron
$\mathcal{Y}$. The ball $\|\boldsymbol{y}\|\le y_\text{max}$ is \emph{thrust deliverability} written in
tangent form: since $\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}\|^2=a_z^2(1+\|\boldsymbol{y}\|^2)$, it is exactly
$\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}\|\le A_\text{cap}$, equivalently the lean cap
$\arccos(a_z/A_\text{cap})$ (\S\ref{sec:deliver}). Folding it in makes $\boldsymbol{y}^\star$
actuator-feasible \emph{by construction}. The per-axis slack $\boldsymbol{s}\ge\boldsymbol{0}$, penalised
by $\rho\gg1$, keeps visibility effectively hard whenever it is achievable within the ball and degrades it
gracefully --- rather than going infeasible --- when the buffered FoV box and the deliverability ball are
disjoint (near-saturated hover with the marker already far off-centre). The objective is strictly convex
in $\boldsymbol{y}$ and the feasible set (polyhedron $\cap$ ball) is convex and always non-empty, so
\eqref{eq:qp} has a unique solution.

\paragraph{This is a discrete-time control barrier function.} The constraint of \eqref{eq:qp} is exactly
$h_k(\boldsymbol{c}_\text{next}(\boldsymbol{y}))\ge0$ for both $k$: it requires the \emph{next-step}
barrier value to be non-negative. This is the discrete-time CBF condition
$h(\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1})\ge\gamma\,h(\boldsymbol{x}_k)$ at $\gamma=0$ (hard invariance), with a
$\tau\boldsymbol{d}$ look-ahead so the invariance also anticipates exogenous target motion.

\begin{prop}[Forward invariance]\label{prop:inv}
Let $\hat{\boldsymbol{c}}_k=\boldsymbol{c}_\text{next}$ evaluated with the realized attitude and drift of
step $k$, and suppose (i) $\boldsymbol{s}^\star=\boldsymbol{0}$ at every step (visibility is achievable
within the deliverability ball --- the normal regime), and (ii) the realized centre at step $k{+}1$
equals $\hat{\boldsymbol{c}}_k$ to within $b\,\mathcal{R}/(2f)$ (the model regime of \S\ref{sec:lin}). If
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_0\in\mathcal{S}$ then $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_k\in\mathcal{S}$ for all $k$.
\end{prop}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} By induction. If $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_k\in\mathcal{S}$, then with
$\boldsymbol{s}^\star=\boldsymbol{0}$ the solution of \eqref{eq:qp} satisfies
$|\hat{\boldsymbol{c}}_k|_k\le\varphi_k$, i.e. $\hat{\boldsymbol{c}}_k\in\mathcal{S}$. By (ii) the realized
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_{k+1}$ differs from $\hat{\boldsymbol{c}}_k$ by at most the buffer margin, so
$|\tilde r_{k+1,k}|\le\varphi_k+b\,\mathcal{R}/(2f)=\mathcal{R}/(2f)$: the centre is still on the sensor.
$\square$
The buffer thus converts the visual margin into a certified tolerance for the one-step linearization and
attitude-tracking residual. \eqref{eq:qp} is \emph{always} solvable (the slack relaxes every visibility
row), so the CBF never stalls the controller; invariance is what the slack buys back, and it holds
whenever the marker's required lean fits inside the deliverability ball. The condition
$\boldsymbol{s}^\star=\boldsymbol{0}$ can be certified online --- it is reported each step
(\texttt{vis\_slack}) --- and is met on every frame of a nominal approach.

\begin{prop}[Pointwise-optimal, minimal intervention]\label{prop:min}
If $\boldsymbol{y}_\text{d}\in\mathcal{Y}$ then $\boldsymbol{y}^\star=\boldsymbol{y}_\text{d}$; otherwise
$\boldsymbol{y}^\star$ is the Euclidean projection of $\boldsymbol{y}_\text{d}$ onto $\mathcal{Y}$, unique
by strict convexity.
\end{prop}
\noindent The CBF is inert whenever the outer command is already visibility-safe, and otherwise perturbs
it by the least $\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{y}_\text{d}\|$ that restores safety. In flight on a
well-behaved approach the projection is a pass-through for essentially every frame.

\begin{prop}[No strangling of recovery]\label{prop:strangle}
Leaning toward the marker, or tangentially to it, does not increase $|\boldsymbol{c}_\text{next}|_k$ on
the binding axis; such a command is left unchanged.
\end{prop}
\noindent\emph{Sketch.} A command that reduces $|\tilde r_k|$ makes visibility row $k$ inactive; the
penalty term of \eqref{eq:qp} contributes zero gradient there, so a re-centring $\boldsymbol{y}_\text{d}$
(which has $|\hat c_k|\le\varphi_k$ on every axis and $\|\boldsymbol{y}_\text{d}\|\le y_\text{max}$) is a
stationary point and is returned unchanged.

\paragraph{Solver.} Eliminating the slack in closed form, $s_k^\star=\max(|\hat c_k|-\varphi_k,0)$, turns
\eqref{eq:qp} into the unconstrained-except-for-the-ball minimisation of
$\tfrac12\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{y}_\text{d}\|^2+\tfrac{\rho}{2}\sum_k\max(|\hat c_k(\boldsymbol{y})|-\varphi_k,0)^2$,
a smooth strictly-convex function of $\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^2$. It is solved by projected Newton:
the Hessian is constant on each active set (identity plus $\rho\,L_{e,k}^{\!\top}L_{e,k}$ over the
violated rows), so a handful of steps reach the interior optimum exactly; if the ball binds --- only when
the FoV box and the ball are disjoint --- the boundary optimum is finished with a one-dimensional search
over the circle $\|\boldsymbol{y}\|=y_\text{max}$. The whole solve is a few $2\times2$ operations.

\section{Deliverability}\label{sec:deliver}
Thrust deliverability is the ball $\|\boldsymbol{y}\|\le y_\text{max}=\sqrt{A_\text{cap}^2/a_z^2-1}$
folded directly into \eqref{eq:qp}. It is the exact tangent form of $\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}\|\le
A_\text{cap}$, and equivalently of the lean cap $\arccos(a_z/A_\text{cap})$ --- the true deliverable tilt
at the \emph{actual} $a_z$ (equal to $\theta_\text{cap}$ only at hover; it loosens below hover, tightens
above). Because $a_z$ is a fixed input to \eqref{eq:qp}, the ball is a plain norm bound on $\boldsymbol{y}$
and adds no coupling: $\boldsymbol{y}^\star$, hence $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}^\star$, is
actuator-feasible with no post-hoc rescaling. When box and ball conflict, the slack (not a hard clip)
absorbs the visibility deficit, and $\boldsymbol{y}^\star$ sits on the ball at the point of least
remaining $\sum_k s_k^{\star2}$ --- the best-effort in-frame command the actuator can deliver.
\par\smallskip\noindent
Two guards remain outside \eqref{eq:qp}, both normally inert: a repeat of the lean cap and the full-vector
cap $\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}\|\le A_\text{cap}$ (they cover the raw command path and the
degenerate $A_\text{cap}\le g$ case, in which the ball is empty and $\theta_\text{cap}$ governs instead);
and Tier~2 (\S\ref{sec:tier2}), which only ever \emph{shrinks} $|\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}|$ toward hover,
so it cannot push $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$ back outside the ball.

\section{Tier 2 --- the descent-rate governor}\label{sec:tier2}
A change in $a_z$ does not move $\boldsymbol{c}_\text{next}$ this cycle, so it lies outside the CBF. It is
handled as a soft governor. Let, per axis,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tedge}
\text{rem}_k=\varphi_k-|\tilde r_k|,\qquad
\text{close}_k=\max\!\big(\operatorname{sgn}(\tilde r_k)\,\dot{\tilde r}_k,\,0\big),\qquad
t_\text{edge}=\min_k \frac{\text{rem}_k}{\text{close}_k},
\end{equation}
the earliest predicted time for the centre to reach the buffer at its measured outward image speed
($t_\text{edge}=\infty$ if it is not moving outward). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gz}
\boxed{\;
g_z=g_\text{min}+(1-g_\text{min})\,\operatorname{clip}\!\Big(\tfrac{t_\text{edge}}{T_\text{react}},0,1\Big),
\qquad
\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}^{\star\star}=(\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}^\star+g)\,g_z-g,
\;}
\end{equation}
applied only while a descent is commanded ($\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}^\star+g>0$) and low-passed so $a_z$
does not step. Only the \emph{downward} part of the vertical command is scaled; $g_\text{min}>0$ keeps a
minimum descent and the map cannot command a climb.

\paragraph{Self-releasing.} $g_z$ is monotone increasing in $t_\text{edge}$, and $t_\text{edge}$
increases as $|\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}|$ decreases (larger $\text{rem}$, smaller $\text{close}$). Slowing
the descent gives the lateral loop more cycles at a given altitude to reduce $|\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}|$;
as it does, $t_\text{edge}\to\infty$ and $g_z\to1$. The trigger is an external \emph{measured} quantity
($|\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}|$ and its rate), so the governor cannot self-sustain on a quantity it
generates, and it is applied after Tier~1 so it never enters the lean solve. It is not a barrier and
makes no invariance claim; its role is to widen the time window in which Proposition~\ref{prop:inv}'s
$\boldsymbol{s}^\star=\boldsymbol{0}$ condition holds near touchdown.

\section{Assumptions}
\begin{assumption} The camera-mount rotation $P$ and the coupling $L_e=-(L_\omega M)$, with its sign, are
calibrated (validated against an independent pinhole model). \end{assumption}
\begin{assumption} The conditioned command is realized: the inner attitude loop tracks the attitude built
from $\boldsymbol{y}^\star$ on a timescale fast relative to the outer loop, with residual lag inside the
buffer $b$. \end{assumption}
\begin{assumption} $\boldsymbol{d}$ is the translation part of the observed centre velocity (from the
front-end's de-rotated optic flow $\boldsymbol{h}_{xy}$, identity-mapped), \emph{after}
\texttt{condition\_drift}: residual-gated to $\boldsymbol{0}$ on a low-confidence solve, median- and
low-pass-filtered, and radially clamped. The default $\tau=0.15$\,s enables the lead (regression-free vs
$\tau=0$ across seven rover profiles); $\tau=0$ restores reactive-only. Whether the lead \emph{improves}
moving-target visibility is not yet established --- it needs a rover approach dynamically stable enough
to observe. \end{assumption}
\begin{assumption} The per-cycle lean change stays within the first-order regime of \S\ref{sec:lin}
($\lesssim15^\circ$), which the $50$\,Hz rate and a smooth outer loop provide. \end{assumption}
\begin{assumption} $A_\text{cap}$ and $g$ are fixed vehicle/physical constants, used directly. \end{assumption}

\section{Scale-freeness and depth-freeness}
Every quantity in \eqref{eq:barrier}, \eqref{eq:lin}, \eqref{eq:qp}, \eqref{eq:tedge}--\eqref{eq:gz} is
one of: a fixed camera intrinsic ($\mathcal{R}/(2f)$, $b$), an attitude ($R$, $\psi_\text{b}$), an
image-space measurement ($\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}$, $\dot{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}$, $\boldsymbol{d}$), a
ratio or pass-through of the outer loop's own command ($\boldsymbol{y}$, $a_z$), a fixed physical
constant ($A_\text{cap}$, $g$), or a fixed time constant ($\tau$, $T_\text{react}$). $L_\omega$ is
depth-independent by construction. $t_\text{edge}$ is an image distance over an image speed --- seconds,
dimensionless in scale. No target range, altitude, or metric velocity appears anywhere. The formulation
is manifestly scale- and depth-free.
