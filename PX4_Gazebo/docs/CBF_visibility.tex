\noindent\emph{Scope.} Visibility is fundamentally an \emph{attitude} constraint (the body tilt shifts
the field of view), but the attitude actually commanded is a byproduct of the desired inertial
acceleration $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$ an outer control loop produces. Rather than constraining
a separately-defined tilt variable and then separately deciding how much vertical thrust to spend, this
note poses one projection directly over $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$ that jointly enforces
(i)~a field-of-view barrier condition and (ii)~a thrust-deliverability bound, as a single constrained
problem. The coupling between a commanded acceleration and the resulting feature motion is the
\textbf{rotational part of the IBVS interaction matrix}, $L_\omega$, evaluated at the measured image
point --- exact, \emph{depth-independent}, no de-rotation or virtual frame required.

\section{Barrier on the real camera plane}
Per image axis $k$, for feature point $i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barrier}
\boxed{\,h_{i,k}=\varphi_{\text{max},k}-|\tilde r_{i,k}|\,}.
\end{equation}
$h_{i,k}\ge0\iff$ feature point $i$ is on the sensor on axis $k$. $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$ is the
\emph{measured} camera-frame feature point --- nothing is de-rotated.

\section{Tilt$\to$feature coupling, expressed in acceleration units}
A camera rotation moves a feature point by the rotational part of the IBVS interaction matrix, a standard
and \textbf{depth-independent} object evaluated at the measured point:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lw}
\dot{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}_i=L_\omega(\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i)\,\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm rp}+\boldsymbol{d}_i,\qquad
L_\omega=\begin{bmatrix} xy & -(1+x^2)\\ 1+y^2 & -xy\end{bmatrix}_{(x,y)=\tilde{\boldsymbol r}_i}.
\end{equation}
$L_\omega$ contains \textbf{no depth}. The drift is measured directly by stripping the rotational term
from the observed feature velocity, $\boldsymbol{d}_i=\dot{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}_{i,\text{obs}}-L_\omega\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm rp}^{\rm curr}$
(using the true gyro rate), so it carries only genuine target motion, not our own tilting.

$L_\omega$ couples to a body \emph{rotation rate} $\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm rp}$, but the quantity a
lateral-acceleration command directly determines is not a rotation rate --- it is the underactuated
quadrotor's \emph{lean}, the ratio of lateral to vertical thrust acceleration,
$\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}/\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}$. Producing a steady lean in a given
direction requires a rotation about the perpendicular axis; writing $\boldsymbol{y}$ for the acceleration
ratio expressed in image-axis coordinates (a rotation by $R_z(-\psi_\text{b})$ aligns the inertial
$x$-$y$ axes with the image axes),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:y-def}
\boldsymbol{y}=R_z(-\psi_\text{b})\Big(\frac{\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}}{\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}}\Big),
\qquad
\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm rp}=M\boldsymbol{y},\quad M=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
$\boldsymbol{y}$ carries no information beyond the acceleration ratio itself --- it is not a separate
physical variable, only that ratio written in the coordinate frame $L_\omega$ is defined in. Substituting
\eqref{eq:y-def} into \eqref{eq:Lw} gives the feature velocity directly as a function of the acceleration
command:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N-def}
\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}\boldsymbol{y}=\underbrace{\Big(\frac{\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}\,R_z(-\psi_\text{b})}{a_z}\Big)}_{\textstyle N_i(a_z)}\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy},
\qquad \tilde{L}_{\omega,i}=L_\omega M.
\end{equation}
$N_i(a_z)=\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}R_z(-\psi_\text{b})/a_z$ is the barrier's Jacobian with respect to the
lateral acceleration directly. It depends on $a_z$ --- the same quantity the joint projection below also
solves for --- which is exactly the coupling that projection is built to resolve, rather than treating
$a_z$ as an external given. For a finite acceleration change the exact map is the rotation homography of
the measured point; the first-order form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lin}
\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i(\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy})\approx\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i+N_i(a_z)\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}-\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}\boldsymbol{y}_\text{curr}
\end{equation}
is the local linearization, accurate over one control step. $\boldsymbol{y}_\text{curr}$ is read directly
from the realized attitude (not from $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$), so it needs no rotation from
acceleration units.

\section{The visibility--deliverability projection}\label{sec:qp}
Two physical constraints bind the same variable $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$: the barrier condition
of \eqref{eq:barrier} along the feature response \eqref{eq:Lw}--\eqref{eq:N-def}, and the requirement that
the commanded thrust vector be one the vehicle can actually deliver. Writing
$\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$ for the conditioned output,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qp}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star=\arg\min_{\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}}\ &\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}-\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^{\,0}\|^2 \\[2pt]
\text{s.t.}\ \
\big|\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i+N_i(a_z)\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}&-\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}\boldsymbol{y}_\text{curr}+\tau\boldsymbol{d}_i\big|\preceq\boldsymbol\varphi_\text{max},
\qquad \big\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}+g\boldsymbol{e}_3\big\|\le A_\text{cap}
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
where $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^{\,0}$ is the unconstrained desired acceleration, the target this
projection stays closest to. The first constraint is the per-feature FoV box, written directly on
$\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}$; the second is the true thrust-deliverability sphere in
gravity-shifted acceleration space (the vehicle's total thrust vector is
$m(\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}+g\boldsymbol{e}_3)$, and
$\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}+g\boldsymbol{e}_3\|\le A_\text{cap}$ is exactly the statement that
this stays within the vehicle's maximum). Both are convex for a fixed $a_z$; since $N_i(a_z)$ itself
depends on $a_z$, the two constraints are resolved jointly rather than sequentially: the lateral
acceleration satisfying the box and the vertical acceleration satisfying the sphere are solved for
together, not one after the other. $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$ then defines the desired
attitude and thrust magnitude for the inner attitude-tracking loop.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Directional} --- only the outward-binding box row is active; inward (recovery) and
tangential accelerations are free. No strangling.
\item \textbf{Envelope-aware} --- the deliverability side of \eqref{eq:qp} is the vehicle's actual maximum
thrust-to-mass ratio $A_\text{cap}$, not a fixed angular cap applied \emph{as if} $a_z$ were fixed at
hover; it tightens or loosens correctly as $a_z$ moves away from hover.
\item \textbf{Forward-invariant} --- the look-ahead shifts the box anchor by $\tau\boldsymbol{d}_i$, so the
projection accounts for centroid drift over the next $\tau$\,s.
\item \textbf{Bounded by construction} --- because $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$ always
satisfies the sphere constraint, the resulting acceleration ratio
$\boldsymbol{y}^\star=R_z(-\psi_\text{b})\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}^\star/a_z^\star$ satisfies
$\|\boldsymbol{y}^\star\|\le\tan\theta_\text{cap}$ at the solved $a_z^\star$ automatically; no separate cap
application is needed.
\item \textbf{Descent-rate aware} (\S\ref{sec:relief}) --- while the box actively suppresses lateral
authority, the vertical component is relaxed toward hover, in proportion to that suppression and clamped so
it can only \emph{slow} a descent, never reverse it; the trade lives inside \eqref{eq:qp} so the output
stays box- and sphere-consistent.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Descent-rate relief under an active box}\label{sec:relief}
When the FoV box is binding it removes lateral acceleration the outer loop asked for: the realized
lean $\boldsymbol{y}^\star$ falls short of the desired $\boldsymbol{y}^{0}=R_z(-\psi_\text{b})(\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}^{0}/\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}^{0})$
by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dtheta}
\Delta_\theta \;=\; \big\|\boldsymbol{y}^{0}-\boldsymbol{y}^\star\big\|,
\end{equation}
the magnitude of lean the box could not grant this step. Pressing on at the full commanded closing rate
while $\Delta_\theta>0$ spends the remaining descent budget without the lateral error being corrected ---
the marker leaves the FoV, or touchdown arrives, before convergence. The projection therefore spends part
of that budget on time instead: the vertical component is pulled toward the hover value in proportion to
$\Delta_\theta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relief}
\boxed{\;\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}^{\star}
\;=\;\min\!\Big(\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z},\;
\max\big(\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}^{0}-\kappa_{az}\,\Delta_\theta,\;-g\big)\Big)
\quad\text{when }\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}^{0}>-g\;}
\end{equation}
and left unchanged otherwise. The interpretation is exact per axis of \eqref{eq:qp}: an $A_\text{cap}$
budget spent laterally is not available vertically and vice versa; \eqref{eq:relief} makes the vertical
side of that trade explicit whenever the box has forced the lateral side. Two bounds make it safe as a
one-way relief:
\begin{itemize}
\item the inner $\max(\cdot,-g)$ clamps the result at the hover acceleration $-g$, so this term alone can
bring a commanded descent toward zero closing rate but \emph{never} past it into a commanded climb;
\item the outer $\min(\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z},\cdot)$ keeps any lift the deliverability sphere or the
outer loop already commanded ($\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}<-g$): the relief only ever adds toward-hover, it
never removes lift.
\end{itemize}
The relief is recomputed from the unconstrained $\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}^{0}$ each outer iterate, so it
does not accumulate across iterates, and it re-enters $N_i(a_z)$ on the next iterate --- the lateral box
and the vertical relief converge together, not one after the other. $\kappa_{az}=0$ disables it and
recovers \eqref{eq:qp} unchanged.

\section{Feasibility}
The box constraint alone is \textbf{infeasible} iff $\varphi_{\text{max},k}<0$ for some $k$ (the marker
subtends the full FoV). The sphere constraint is always feasible by construction (it is satisfied by
$\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}=\boldsymbol{0}$). Genuine joint infeasibility --- the box demanding more
lateral acceleration than $A_\text{cap}$ can deliver at any achievable $a_z$ --- reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feasibility}
\sigma_{\min}(\tilde{L}_{\omega,i})\,(\tan\theta_\text{cap}-\|\boldsymbol{y}_\text{curr}\|)\ \ge\ \max_{i,k}\big(|\tilde r_{i,k}|-\varphi_{\text{max},k}\big)_+ + \bar{d}\quad\forall i,
\end{equation}
evaluated at the solved $a_z^\star$ rather than at a fixed hover-assumed $\theta_\text{cap}$: joint
feasibility holds whenever some achievable $a_z$ satisfies \eqref{eq:feasibility}, a weaker requirement
than demanding it hold at the hover value of $a_z$ alone.

\section{Assumptions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The coupling $\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}=L_\omega M$ and its sign convention are calibrated.} The
fixed $90^\circ$ map $\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm rp}=M\boldsymbol{y}$ bridges the acceleration-ratio direction
and the rotation-axis rate that $L_\omega$ couples to.
\item \textbf{The conditioned acceleration is realized}: the inner attitude loop tracks the attitude built
from $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$ on a timescale fast relative to the outer loop.
\item \textbf{The drift $\boldsymbol{d}_i$ is the translation part} of the observed feature velocity,
$\dot{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}_{i,\text{obs}}-L_\omega\boldsymbol\omega^{\rm curr}$, entering as
\emph{measured} motion --- no separate disturbance term is introduced.
\item \textbf{First-order $L_\omega$ over the cycle.} Exact is the rotation homography of
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$; the linearization \eqref{eq:lin} is accurate over one control step.
\item \textbf{$A_\text{cap}$ is the vehicle's true achievable thrust-to-mass ratio}, used directly rather
than a fixed angular margin computed from it at hover.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scale-freeness and depth-freeness}
The barrier \eqref{eq:barrier}, the coupling \eqref{eq:Lw}, and the projection \eqref{eq:qp} contain only
the measured image point $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$, $\boldsymbol\varphi_\text{max}=\mathcal{R}/(2f)$, the
body rate, the commanded acceleration, and the vehicle's own $A_\text{cap}$ and $g$. \textbf{$L_\omega$ is
depth-independent by construction}, and $A_\text{cap}$, $g$ are fixed vehicle/physical constants, not
measurements of target depth or altitude --- the whole formulation remains manifestly scale- and
depth-free.
