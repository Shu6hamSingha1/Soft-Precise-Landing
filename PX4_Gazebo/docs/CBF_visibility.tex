\noindent\emph{Scope and notation.} This note follows the notation of the manuscript's Visibility CBF
subsection verbatim (frames $\Iframe$/$\Cframe$, feature points $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$, coupling
$\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}=L_\omega M$, lean $\boldsymbol\theta$, cap $\theta_\text{cap}$, drift
$\boldsymbol{d}_i$, look-ahead $\tau$) and extends it with one additional symbol, $A_\text{cap}$, the
vehicle's maximum achievable thrust-to-mass ratio. Visibility is fundamentally an \emph{attitude}
constraint (the body tilt shifts the FoV), but the attitude actually commanded is a byproduct of the
desired inertial acceleration $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$. Rather than solving the tilt-only
projection of the manuscript and separately deciding how much vertical thrust to spend, this note poses
one projection directly over $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$ that jointly enforces (i) the barrier
condition of the manuscript's Visibility Forward Invariance theorem and (ii) the
deliverable-lateral-acceleration bound of the manuscript's Property~1, as a single constrained problem
rather than a tilt projection followed by a separate deliverability cap. The coupling itself is
unchanged: the \textbf{rotational part of the IBVS interaction matrix}, $L_\omega$, evaluated at the
measured image point --- exact, \emph{depth-independent}, no de-rotation or virtual frame required.

\section{Barrier on the real camera plane}
Per image axis $k$, for feature point $i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barrier}
\boxed{\,h_{i,k}=\varphi_{\text{max},k}-|\tilde r_{i,k}|\,}.
\end{equation}
$h_{i,k}\ge0\iff$ feature point $i$ is on the sensor on axis $k$. $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$ is the
\emph{measured} camera-frame feature point --- nothing is de-rotated. This is the manuscript's own
barrier, unchanged.

\section{Tilt$\to$feature coupling, expressed in acceleration units}
A camera rotation moves a feature point by the rotational part of the IBVS interaction matrix, a standard
and \textbf{depth-independent} object evaluated at the measured point:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lw}
\dot{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}_i=L_\omega(\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i)\,\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm rp}+\boldsymbol{d}_i,\qquad
L_\omega=\begin{bmatrix} xy & -(1+x^2)\\ 1+y^2 & -xy\end{bmatrix}_{(x,y)=\tilde{\boldsymbol r}_i}.
\end{equation}
$L_\omega$ contains \textbf{no depth}. The drift is measured directly by stripping the rotational term
from the observed feature velocity, $\boldsymbol{d}_i=\dot{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}_{i,\text{obs}}-L_\omega\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm rp}^{\rm curr}$
(using the true gyro rate), so it carries only genuine target motion, not our own tilting.

A commanded lean produces a rotation about the perpendicular axis, $\boldsymbol\omega=M\boldsymbol\theta$,
and the lean is itself the direction of the commanded lateral acceleration relative to the commanded
vertical acceleration, $\boldsymbol\theta=R_z(-\psi_\text{b})(\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}/\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z})$
--- the manuscript's own definition. Substituting both relations into \eqref{eq:Lw} gives the feature
velocity directly as a function of the acceleration command:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N-def}
\boldsymbol\omega=M\boldsymbol\theta,\qquad
\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}\boldsymbol\theta=\underbrace{\Big(\frac{\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}\,R_z(-\psi_\text{b})}{a_z}\Big)}_{\textstyle N_i(a_z)}\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}.
\end{equation}
$N_i(a_z)=\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}R_z(-\psi_\text{b})/a_z$ is the barrier's Jacobian with respect to the
lateral acceleration directly. It depends on $a_z$ --- the same quantity the joint projection below also
solves for --- which is exactly the coupling that projection is built to resolve, rather than treating
$a_z$ as an external given. For a finite acceleration change the exact map is the rotation homography of
the measured point; the first-order form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lin}
\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i(\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy})\approx\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i+N_i(a_z)\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}-\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}\boldsymbol\theta_\text{curr}
\end{equation}
is the local linearization, accurate over one control step, exactly matching the manuscript's own
linearization of $\dot h_{i,k}$ along \eqref{eq:Lw}. $\boldsymbol\theta_\text{curr}$ is read directly from
the realized attitude (not from $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$), so it needs no rotation from
acceleration units.

\section{The visibility--deliverability projection}\label{sec:qp}
Two physical constraints bind the same variable $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$: the barrier condition
of \eqref{eq:barrier} along the feature response \eqref{eq:Lw}--\eqref{eq:N-def}, and the
deliverable-lateral-acceleration bound of the manuscript's Property~1 (inner-loop deliverable-tilt
saturation bounds $\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}\|$ by a state-dependent fraction of
$|\,^\Iframe a_{\text{d},z}|$). Writing $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$ for the conditioned
output (paralleling the manuscript's $\boldsymbol\theta^\star$ notation for its own tilt-only projection),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qp}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star=\arg\min_{\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}}\ &\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}-\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^{\,0}\|^2 \\[2pt]
\text{s.t.}\ \
\big|\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i+N_i(a_z)\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}&-\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}\boldsymbol\theta_\text{curr}+\tau\boldsymbol{d}_i\big|\preceq\boldsymbol\varphi_\text{max},
\qquad \big\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}+g\boldsymbol{e}_3\big\|\le A_\text{cap}
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
where $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^{\,0}$ is the unconstrained desired acceleration --- the
manuscript's own $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}$ before conditioning, the target this projection
stays closest to. The first constraint is the manuscript's per-feature FoV box, now written directly on
$\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}$; the second is the true thrust-deliverability sphere in
gravity-shifted acceleration space (the vehicle's total thrust vector is
$m(\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}+g\boldsymbol{e}_3)$, and
$\|\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}+g\boldsymbol{e}_3\|\le A_\text{cap}$ is exactly the statement that
this stays within the vehicle's maximum). Both are convex for a fixed $a_z$; since $N_i(a_z)$ itself
depends on $a_z$, the two constraints are resolved jointly rather than sequentially: the lateral
acceleration satisfying the box and the vertical acceleration satisfying the sphere are solved for
together, not one after the other. $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$ then defines the desired
attitude and thrust magnitude through the inner loop, exactly as the manuscript's own $\boldsymbol\theta^\star$
does.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Directional} --- only the outward-binding box row is active; inward (recovery) and
tangential accelerations are free. No strangling, matching the manuscript's own $\dot h_{i,k}$ argument.
\item \textbf{Envelope-aware} --- the deliverability side of \eqref{eq:qp} is the vehicle's actual maximum
thrust-to-mass ratio $A_\text{cap}$, not $\theta_\text{cap}$ applied \emph{as if} $a_z$ were fixed at
hover; it tightens or loosens correctly as $a_z$ moves away from hover.
\item \textbf{Forward-invariant} --- the look-ahead shifts the box anchor by $\tau\boldsymbol{d}_i$, so the
projection accounts for centroid drift over the next $\tau$\,s, exactly as in the manuscript.
\item \textbf{Bounded by construction} --- because $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$ always
satisfies the sphere constraint, the derived lean
$\boldsymbol\theta^\star=R_z(-\psi_\text{b})\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_{\text{d},xy}^\star/a_z^\star$
satisfies $\|\boldsymbol\theta^\star\|\le\tan\theta_\text{cap}$ at the solved $a_z^\star$ automatically; no
separate, hover-assumed cap application is needed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Feasibility}
The box constraint alone is \textbf{infeasible} iff $\varphi_{\text{max},k}<0$ for some $k$ (the marker
subtends the full FoV). The sphere constraint is always feasible by construction (it is satisfied by
$\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}=\boldsymbol{0}$). Genuine joint infeasibility --- the box demanding more
lateral acceleration than $A_\text{cap}$ can deliver at any achievable $a_z$ --- is the acceleration-space
counterpart of the manuscript's feasibility condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feasibility}
\sigma_{\min}(\tilde{L}_{\omega,i})\,(\tan\theta_\text{cap}-\|\boldsymbol\theta_\text{curr}\|)\ \ge\ \max_{i,k}\big(|\tilde r_{i,k}|-\varphi_{\text{max},k}\big)_+ + \bar{d}\quad\forall i,
\end{equation}
evaluated here at the solved $a_z^\star$ rather than at a fixed hover-assumed $\theta_\text{cap}$: joint
feasibility holds whenever some achievable $a_z$ satisfies \eqref{eq:feasibility}, a weaker requirement
than demanding it hold at the hover value of $a_z$ alone.

\section{Assumptions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The coupling $\tilde{L}_{\omega,i}=L_\omega M$ and its sign convention are calibrated.} The
fixed $90^\circ$ map $\boldsymbol\omega=M\boldsymbol\theta$ bridges the lean direction and the
rotation-axis rate that $L_\omega$ couples to.
\item \textbf{The conditioned acceleration is realized}: the inner attitude loop tracks the attitude built
from $\,^\Iframe\boldsymbol{a}_\text{d}^\star$ on a timescale fast relative to the outer loop, the
manuscript's own timescale-separation assumption.
\item \textbf{The drift $\boldsymbol{d}_i$ is the translation part} of the observed feature velocity,
$\dot{\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}}_{i,\text{obs}}-L_\omega\boldsymbol\omega^{\rm curr}$, entering as
\emph{measured} motion, matching the manuscript's own treatment --- no separate disturbance term is
introduced.
\item \textbf{First-order $L_\omega$ over the cycle.} Exact is the rotation homography of
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$; the linearization \eqref{eq:lin} is accurate over one control step, the same
approximation the manuscript's own barrier derivative relies on.
\item \textbf{$A_\text{cap}$ is the vehicle's true achievable thrust-to-mass ratio}, the physical quantity
the manuscript's own $\theta_\text{cap}=60^\circ$ (a $2\times$-hover-thrust margin) is derived from ---
this projection uses that quantity directly rather than the fixed angular margin computed from it at
hover.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scale-freeness and depth-freeness}
The barrier \eqref{eq:barrier}, the coupling \eqref{eq:Lw}, and the projection \eqref{eq:qp} contain only
the measured image point $\tilde{\boldsymbol{r}}_i$, $\boldsymbol\varphi_\text{max}=\mathcal{R}/(2f)$, the
body rate, the commanded acceleration, and the vehicle's own $A_\text{cap}$ and $g$. \textbf{$L_\omega$ is
depth-independent by construction}, and $A_\text{cap}$, $g$ are fixed vehicle/physical constants, not
measurements of target depth or altitude --- the whole formulation remains manifestly scale- and
depth-free, matching the manuscript's own scale-free design throughout.
