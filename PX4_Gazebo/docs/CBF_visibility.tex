\noindent\emph{Scope.} Visibility is an \emph{attitude} constraint --- the body tilt moves the field of
view --- but the attitude actually commanded is a byproduct of the desired inertial acceleration
$\bm I_a=({}^\mathcal I a_x,{}^\mathcal I a_y,{}^\mathcal I a_z)$ that the outer control loop produces.
Rather than solving a tilt-only QP and separately deciding how much vertical thrust to spend, we solve
one QP directly over $\bm I_a$ that jointly enforces (i)~the target stays in the field of view and
(ii)~the commanded thrust stays within what the vehicle can actually deliver. Both constraints are
naturally expressed in acceleration units, so a single variable and a single feasible set serve both.
The coupling between a candidate acceleration and the resulting feature motion is still the
\textbf{rotational part of the IBVS interaction matrix}, $\Lw$, evaluated at the measured image point ---
exact, \emph{depth-independent}, no de-rotation or virtual frame required. The barrier uses image
features only --- scale- and depth-free (no $\beta$, no optic flow, no disturbance term).

\section{Barrier on the real camera plane}
Per image axis $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barrier}
\boxed{\,h_k=\varphi_{\max,k}-|\,^\Cframe\hat r_k|-\delta_k\,}.
\end{equation}
$h_k\ge0\iff$ the marker (outermost feature point) is on the sensor. $\,^\Cframe\rhat$ is the
\emph{measured} camera image point --- nothing is de-rotated.

\section{Tilt$\to$feature coupling, expressed in acceleration units}
A camera rotation moves a feature point by the rotational part of the IBVS interaction matrix, a standard
and \textbf{depth-independent} object evaluated at the measured point $\,^\Cframe\rhat=(x,y)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lw}
\,^\Cframe\dot{\rhat}=\Lw(\,^\Cframe\rhat)\,\bm\omega_{\rm rp}+\bm d,\qquad
\Lw=\begin{bmatrix} xy & -(1+x^2)\\ 1+y^2 & -xy\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
$\Lw$ contains \textbf{no depth}. The drift is measured directly by stripping the rotational term from
the observed feature velocity, $\bm d=\,^\Cframe\dot{\rhat}_{\rm obs}-\Lw\bm\omega_{\rm rp}^{\rm curr}$
(using the true gyro rate), so it carries only genuine target motion, not our own tilting.

A commanded lean produces a rotation about the perpendicular axis, $\bm\omega=M\bm\theta$
\eqref{eq:M-def}, and a lean is itself the direction of the commanded lateral acceleration relative to
the commanded vertical acceleration: $\bm\theta=P\,\bm I_{a,xy}/a_z$, where $P$ folds together the yaw
alignment and the fixed camera-mount rotation. Substituting both into \eqref{eq:Lw} gives the feature
velocity directly as a function of the acceleration command:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-def}
\bm\omega=M\bm\theta,\quad M=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\Lw M\bm\theta=\underbrace{\Big(\frac{\Lw M\,P}{a_z}\Big)}_{\textstyle N(a_z)}\bm I_{a,xy}.
\end{equation}
$N(a_z)=\widetilde\Lw P/a_z$ is the box constraint's Jacobian with respect to the lateral acceleration
directly. It depends on $a_z$ --- the same acceleration this QP also solves for --- which is exactly the
coupling the joint formulation below is built to resolve, rather than treating $a_z$ as an external given.
For a finite acceleration change the exact map is the rotation homography of the measured point; the
first-order form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lin}
\,^\Cframe\rhat(\bm I_{a,xy})\approx\,^\Cframe\rhat+N(a_z)\,\bm I_{a,xy}-\widetilde\Lw\,\bm\theta_{\rm curr}
\end{equation}
is the local linearization, accurate over one control step. $\bm\theta_{\rm curr}$ is read directly from
the \emph{realized} attitude (not from $\bm I_a$), so it needs no rotation from acceleration units.

\section{The visibility--deliverability QP}\label{sec:qp}
Two physical constraints bind the same variable $\bm I_a$: the predicted feature position must stay
inside the field-of-view box, and the commanded thrust vector must stay inside the vehicle's real
envelope. Writing $\bm I_a^d$ for the unconstrained desired acceleration (the outer loop's own command,
before any visibility consideration) and $A_{\rm cap}$ for the maximum achievable thrust-to-mass ratio,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qp}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\bm I_a^\star=\arg\min_{\bm I_a}\ &\|\bm I_a-\bm I_a^d\|^2 \\[2pt]
\text{s.t.}\ \
\big|\,^\Cframe\hat r_k+[N(a_z)\bm I_{a,xy}&-\widetilde\Lw\bm\theta_{\rm curr}]_k+\tau d_k\big|\le m_k\ \ \forall k,
\qquad \big\|\bm I_a+g\bm e_3\big\|\le A_{\rm cap}
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
The first constraint is the same FoV box as before, now written directly on $\bm I_{a,xy}$; the second is
the true thrust-deliverability sphere in gravity-shifted acceleration space (the vehicle's total thrust
vector is $m(\bm I_a+g\bm e_3)$, and $\|\bm I_a+g\bm e_3\|\le A_{\rm cap}$ is exactly the statement that
this stays within the measured maximum). Both are convex for a fixed $a_z$; since $N(a_z)$ itself depends
on $a_z$, the two projections are iterated together (alternating projection onto the box at the current
$a_z$ estimate, then onto the sphere, updating $a_z$ for the next pass) until convergence. This is a
genuine joint solve for the lateral and vertical acceleration together, not a lateral solve followed by a
separate vertical adjustment: the vertical component the visibility constraint can ``spend'' and the
vertical component actually available from the thrust ceiling are reconciled inside the same iteration.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Directional} --- only the outward-binding box row is active; inward (recovery) and
tangential accelerations are free. No strangling.
\item \textbf{Envelope-aware} --- the deliverability constraint is the vehicle's actual measured thrust
ceiling, not an angle assumed at hover; it tightens or loosens correctly as $a_z$ moves away from hover,
because it is expressed on the full acceleration vector rather than on a lean angle computed \emph{as if}
$a_z$ were fixed.
\item \textbf{Forward-invariant} --- the look-ahead shifts the box anchor by $\tau\bm d$, so the QP
accounts for centroid drift over the next $\tau$\,s in both phases (Sect.~\ref{sec:twophase}).
\item \textbf{Bounded by construction} --- because $\bm I_a^\star$ always satisfies the sphere constraint,
the derived lean $\bm\theta^\star=P\,\bm I_{a,xy}^\star/a_z^\star$ used to build attitude (Sect.~\ref{sec:impl})
is automatically bounded by the true deliverable cone at the solved $a_z^\star$; no separate,
hover-assumed angular cap is needed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Two-phase $\delta$ (central-marker convention)}\label{sec:twophase}
The designated \emph{central} marker is the target. The predicted margin $m_k$ is phase-dependent:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Phase 1 --- central marker decodes.}
$\delta_k=0$, so $m_k=\varphi_{\max,k}$ (full half-FoV).
The barrier is centroid-only: the QP box spans the entire image plane, and only fires if the
\emph{centroid} drifts toward the edge. The central marker is deliberately allowed to grow and
overflow as the UAV closes in --- its visibility \emph{is} the guarantee; using its large half-extent
would fire the barrier early. $\dot\delta$ is tracked but excluded from $m_k$ in Phase~1.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Phase 2 --- central marker overflows} (decode-fail, hysteresis-gated against flicker
$\Rightarrow$ touchdown range). $\delta_{\rm eff}$ \textbf{ramps in} over a few frames from the
visible-\emph{set} half-extent ($\tfrac12\mathrm{ptp}_k$ over ALL visible markers' corners, so
$|\,^\Cframe\rhat|+\delta_{\rm eff}$ is the true outermost feature --- not one marker):
\[
  m_k = \varphi_{\max,k} - \delta_{{\rm eff},k} - \tau\dot\delta_{{\rm eff},k}.
\]
The ramp (not a step) keeps $h$ from dropping discontinuously at engagement, but clamp the ramp
gain so $\delta_{\rm eff}$ never \emph{lags} the measured $\tfrac12\mathrm{ptp}$ --- a ramp slower
than the fill under-protects. Within Phase~2 the visible markers themselves leave (a mild inner
sawtooth); $\dot\delta$ is reset across those switches. In Phase~2 the sphere constraint of
\eqref{eq:qp} still applies unchanged; the deliverability side of the joint solve does not depend on
which phase the visibility side is in.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Implementation:} hysteresis threshold~$=3$ consecutive decode-fails before Phase~2
activates; ramp completes over~$5$ control cycles (${\approx}120$\,ms at 42\,Hz).
Both $\delta_{\rm eff}=\alpha\,\delta_{\rm ref}$ and $\dot\delta_{\rm eff}=\alpha\,\dot\delta_{\rm ref}$
are scaled by the same ramp factor~$\alpha\in[0,1]$, where $\delta_{\rm ref}$ and $\dot\delta_{\rm ref}$
are the half-extent and loom rate measured at the last successful decode.

\section{Feasibility floor and hand-off}
The box constraint alone is \textbf{infeasible} iff $m_k<0$ for some~$k$ (the marker fills the frame
within $\tau$). In Phase~1, $m_k=\varphi_{\max,k}>0$ always, so the box is \textbf{always feasible} on
its own while the central marker decodes. The sphere constraint is always feasible by construction (it
is satisfied by $\bm I_a=\bm 0$, i.e.\ zero net commanded acceleration). Genuine joint infeasibility ---
the visibility box demanding more lateral acceleration than the thrust ceiling can deliver at any
achievable $a_z$ --- is handled by the iteration converging to the point on the sphere closest (in the
box-projection sense) to feasibility, rather than by declaring failure; this is the same graceful
degradation the alternating-projection method already provides for the box alone.

The perception-floor hand-off is triggered by \emph{central-marker overflow} (Phase~2 activation,
hysteresis-gated), not by box infeasibility. On Phase~2 entry, or if $m_k<0$ under Phase~2 margins,
raise the perception-floor flag and hand off to the texture-free ring flow.

\section{Assumptions (and the practical challenge each carries)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The coupling $\widetilde\Lw=\Lw M$ and its sign convention are calibrated.} The fixed
$90^\circ$ map $\bm\omega=M\bm\theta$ \eqref{eq:M-def} bridges the lean direction and the rotation-axis
rate that $\Lw$ couples to. Any residual mount-dependent sign of $\Lw$ is a one-time check: command a
small acceleration and confirm the resulting $\Delta\,^\Cframe\rhat$ matches $\widetilde\Lw$ in sign
before trusting the barrier.
\item \textbf{The filtered acceleration is realized} (the SO(3) loop tracks the attitude built from
$\bm I_a^\star$). \emph{Challenge:} attitude bandwidth $\Rightarrow$ lag; the sphere constraint bounds
magnitude, not delay.
\item \textbf{The drift $\bm d$ is the translation part} ($\,^\Cframe\dot{\rhat}_{\rm obs}-\Lw\bm\omega^{\rm
curr}$) and $\dot\delta$ the loom. \emph{Challenge:} finite differences $\Rightarrow$ filter + binding-axis
hysteresis; the disturbance enters as \emph{measured} motion --- no separate $\bm d_h$ term. The homography
centroid (hence $\,^\Cframe\dot{\rhat}_{\rm obs}$) must stay \emph{smooth across marker-set changes} (the
homography re-fits on a different visible subset) or $\bm d$ spikes --- filter / hold a stable subset across a switch.
\item \textbf{First-order $\Lw$ over the cycle.} Exact is the rotation homography of $\,^\Cframe\rhat$; the
linearization \eqref{eq:lin} is accurate over one control step.
\item \textbf{$A_{\rm cap}$ is the vehicle's true achievable thrust-to-mass ratio}, measured rather than
assumed --- this replaces an earlier fixed-angle deliverability assumption with a direct, always-current
physical bound.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scale-freeness and depth-freeness}
The barrier \eqref{eq:barrier}, the coupling \eqref{eq:Lw}, and the QP \eqref{eq:qp} contain only the
measured image point $\,^\Cframe\rhat$, $\delta$, $\bm\varphi_{\max}=\mathcal R/(2f)$, the body rate, the
commanded acceleration, and the vehicle's own thrust-to-mass ratio $A_{\rm cap}$ and gravitational
constant $g$. \textbf{$\Lw$ is depth-independent by construction} (no $\mathcal V z_\mathrm{t}$, no
$\beta$), and $A_{\rm cap}$, $g$ are fixed vehicle/physical constants, not measurements of target depth or
altitude --- the whole formulation remains manifestly scale- and depth-free. The only metric quantity
upstream is the geometric tracker's $R_d$ (a controller output).

\section{Implementation (per control cycle)}\label{sec:impl}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(once)} Calibrate $\Lw$'s sign / axis convention (Assumption 1); measure $A_{\rm cap}$.
\item Measure $\,^\Cframe\rhat$ (board centroid, homography). Advance the two-phase $\delta$ state:
if central marker decodes, reset Phase~2 counters and set $\delta_{\rm eff}=0$; otherwise increment
the decode-fail counter, and once the hysteresis threshold is reached, ramp $\delta_{\rm eff}$ toward
the visible markers' $\tfrac12\mathrm{ptp}$.
\item Evaluate $\Lw(\,^\Cframe\rhat)$; form the drift $\bm d=\,^\Cframe\dot{\rhat}_{\rm obs}-\Lw\bm\omega^{\rm
curr}$ and $\dot\delta_{\rm eff}$ (filtered; zero in Phase~1). Read $\bm\theta_{\rm curr}$ from the
current realized attitude.
\item Two-phase predicted margin: Phase~1: $m_k=\varphi_{\max,k}$; Phase~2:
$m_k=\varphi_{\max,k}-\delta_{{\rm eff},k}-\tau\dot\delta_{{\rm eff},k}$.
If $m_k\le0$ for any $k$, raise the perception-floor flag and hand off to the ring estimator.
Otherwise build the joint QP \eqref{eq:qp}.
\item Starting from $\bm I_a^d$ (the outer loop's unconstrained desired acceleration), jointly project
onto the FoV box (using $N(a_z)$ at the current $a_z$ estimate) and the deliverability sphere, iterating
until convergence, to obtain $\bm I_a^\star$.
\item \textbf{Build the desired attitude directly from $\bm I_a^\star$.} Recover
$\bm\theta^\star=P\,\bm I_{a,xy}^\star/a_z^\star$ and set the desired body-$z$ axis
$\bm r_{d3}=\big[-R_z(\psi)\bm\theta^\star,\,1\big]/\big\|[\,\cdot\,,1]\big\|$, passing $R_d$ to the
SO(3) inner loop and taking the thrust \emph{magnitude} from $\bm I_a^\star$ directly. Building attitude
from the un-filtered $\bm I_a^\star$ (rather than from a separately low-pass-filtered acceleration
command) keeps the barrier's guarantee landing exactly on the commanded attitude, with no smearing from
a downstream filter time constant.
\end{enumerate}
