Image-Based Visual Servoing of Micro Aerial
Vehicles With Robust Observation and
Output Feedback
Yongcong Chen , Baoquan Li , Senior Member, IEEE, Wuxi Shi , and Xuebo Zhang , Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—An output feedback control method is proposed in
the article with state observers for robust image-based visual
servoing (IBVS) regarding micro aerial vehicles (MAVs). Specif-
ically, image features are ﬁrst selected from the perspective
image moments and are projected onto a suitably deﬁned image
plane so as to provide decoupled kinematics for translational
motion. In order to address the problem of low quality and
even unavailability of linear velocity information, the image
moment error observer is designed as a foundation based on
an image ﬁlter, and then the MAV body velocities are estimated
by designing a robust observer to obtain translational optical ﬂow
of virtual image features. An adaptive law is proposed for the
unknown depth information of the visual target and is combined
with observers in output feedback controller design to enhance
system robustness. Stability analysis shows that all states of the
system are uniformly ultimately bounded (UUB) and converge to
zero asymptotically. Comparative simulation and experimental
results demonstrate the performance of the proposed method.
Index Terms—Depth adaptation, image-based visual servoing
(IBVS), robust velocity observer, translational optical ﬂow, micro
aerial vehicles (MAVs).

have continued to design new models and advanced controllers
to enhance the reliability of MAVs in various potential applica-
tions. MAVs can be used for aerial vision, such as providing a
wide variety of aerial footage, and can perform tasks in danger-
ous and hard-to-reach areas, such as infrastructure inspections.
In addition, MAVs play a signiﬁcant role in reconnaissance and
rescue missions, enabling rapid response to emergencies and
providing important information and support [1], [2]. MAV
researches generally include developing eﬃcient actuators,

sensor system of a MAV usually includes an inertial measure-
ment unit (IMU) and a global positioning system (GPS). While
the IMU system can provide reliable angular velocity and
attitude information for the MAV, it cannot accurately estimate
translational position and linear velocity [3], [4]. Additionally,
GPS provides relatively high-accuracy positioning information
but does not operate eﬀectively in indoor and urban canyon
environments, and obtaining linear velocity in GPS-free areas
is a challenging problem [5], [6].
Much research has been conducted recently aiming at
providing environmental and motion information to MAVs,
especially in the case of workspaces without GPS sig-
nals. In this context, solutions have emerged for MAV
systems by suﬃciently using visual sensors. The vision sen-
sor is a reliable and low-cost device that can be fused
with IMU data to provide eﬀective translational velocity
information, and it can also be eﬀectively used for MAV
localization relative to the environment [7], [8]. Application
of MAV vision systems includes obstacle avoidance [9],
[10], attitude estimation [11], [12], simultaneous localiza-
tion and mapping (SLAM) [13], [14], path planning [15],
and so on.
MAV control based on vision systems mainly consists
of two patterns: position-based visual servoing (PBVS) and
image-based visual servoing (IBVS). PBVS estimates the
3-D Cartesian space information of a target object through
the visual sensor and determines MAV motion based on
this information [16]. IBVS uses image information of target
features to form closed-loop control directly, and it is robust
for camera models and without 3-D reconstruction. Compared
with PBVS, IBVS does not need to calculate the position
of the target under 3-D coordinates; instead, it relies on the
projection relationship between the camera and the target
for calculation and control. IBVS can better adapt to fast
motion in the scene, uncertainty of target motion direction, and
distance change [17]. Considering the underactuated traits of
quadrotor MAVs and coupling dynamics on the image plane,
the IBVS approach for quadrotor MAVs is, moreover, more
challenging than that for fully actuated systems [18]. Rotation
and translation degrees of freedom need to be decoupled and
independently controlled under IBVS frameworks. In [19]
and [20], IBVS frameworks are designed by selecting image
features with passive traits for decoupling MAV kinematics,

The IBVS control pattern based on the virtual camera
has gradually become a research hotspot in recent years. In
order to solve the problem that the visual object leaves the
camera ﬁeld of view (FOV), Xie and Lynch [21] propose
an input saturation control law to ensure that roll, pitch,
and thrust are within speciﬁed ranges. In order to handle
the visibility constraint, Zhang et al. [22] developed a robust
nonlinear model predictive control scheme for MAV IBVS
control such that the visual target can stay within camera FOV.
Huang et al. [23] designed a visual servoing scheme based on
hierarchical homography, which is used for pose extraction to
solve the under-actuation problem of the MAV. In order to
solve the problem of manually operating for observing visual
targets, Shi et al. [24] design an integrated IBVS ﬂight system
including target search and IBVS stages.
MAV linear velocities are usually measured by auxiliary
devices such as IMUs in the IBVS framework, but mea-
surement accuracy is easily aﬀected by error accumulation
and large vibrations. In order to dispose the limitation, Asl
and Yoon [25] designed a controller according to the optical
ﬂow on the image plane that does not require linear velocity
information of the MAV. Fink et al. [26] designed a virtual-
camera-based visual servoing approach by using simpliﬁed
models, where the linear velocity of the MAV is provided by
an external motion capture system. Hashim [27] designed a Lie
group-based full-state observer for controlling MAV motion,
which does not need angular and linear velocity measurements.
Depth information needs to be considered in IBVS con-
troller design, and it is an unknown parameter when the
geometric model of the target is not available. Asl et al. [28]
proposed an adaptive IBVS scheme to compensate for depth
uncertainty, where a linear observer is designed to estimate the
velocity of image features regarding the stationary target. Gao
et al. [29] proposed an IBVS strategy based on hierarchical
images, using a distance sensor to estimate object depth. Zheng
et al. [30] designed a visual servoing controller by using the
backstepping pattern, and the depth as part of the control gain
is set in a certain range, eliminating the need to know the depth
information of feature points. It is, however, still challenging to
eﬃciently improve the robustness of MAVs in unknown depth
environments and address low quality and even unavailability
of linear velocities obtained by IMUs.
In the article, the image moment error observer is ﬁrst
constructed based on an image ﬁlter. Then, for MAV body
velocity estimation, a robust observer is designed to obtain
the translational optical ﬂow of virtual image features. An
adaptive control law is proposed for the uncertainty of image
feature depth, which makes the controller robust to unknown
depth information. Stability analysis proves that system states
are bounded and the error signals converge to zero. Compar-
ative simulation and experiment are conducted to verify the
performance of the proposed method. The main contribution
of the article is to design an output feedback control strategy
regarding unknown linear velocity and unknown depth infor-
mation, where velocity observer and depth adaptive law are
constructed for robust MAV control.

III. IMAGE FEATURE DYNAMICS
Perspective projection patterns are widely discussed to con-
duct visual servoing of MAVs, where appropriately deﬁned
image moments are mainly adopted as visual features. By
using MAV roll and pitch angles, image data are reprojected
to a virtual image plane, which is parallel to the object. The
image moments can avoid the inﬂuence of single-point fea-
tures from environmental noise and dynamic interference and
provide stable image feature measurement. The mathematical
form of image moments can be directly used in control design.
Based on image moments selected in the virtual image plane,
image-based full-actuation controllers can be designed for the
MAV to maintain good behavior in Cartesian space.
The horizontal virtual camera model is introduced with
the same optical center and parameters as the downward
camera, whose frame is denoted as Fv. Considering there are

VII. CONCLUSION
In the article, an output feedback IBVS method is proposed
with state observers for controlling quadrotor MAV motion.
By deﬁning appropriate image moment features on the virtual
Fig. 19.
Experiment 2: Observation for MAV velocities by [25] (solid lines:
estimated values, dashed lines: truth values).
image plane, it aims to obtain satisﬁed regulation trajectories
in both image and Cartesian space. IMUs commonly used in
MAV motion do not provide accurate linear velocity informa-
tion and suﬀer from accumulated errors. The ﬂow of image
features is, thus, used as the linear velocity cue in the article,
which is estimated from selected visual features by designing
nonlinear observers. An adaptive law is designed for unknown
image depth information, which is combined with observers
in output feedback IBVS controller design to enhance system
robustness. Stability analysis shows that all states of the
system are bounded, and the error signals converge to zero.
Simulation and experimental results show that the proposed
method responds well in both image space and Cartesian space
and provides accurate estimation for image moment errors
and MAV velocities. In the future, linear velocity observers
will be designed regarding both MAV and dynamic targets
so as to achieve image-based visual servo tracking tasks with
robustness.

sity, Tianjin, China, in 2006, and the Ph.D. degree in
control theory and control engineering from Nankai
University, Tianjin, in 2011.
From 2014 to 2015, he was a Visiting Scholar
with the Department of Electrical and Computer
Engineering, University of Windsor, Windsor, ON,
Canada. He was a Visiting Scholar with the Depart-
ment of Mechanical and Biomedical Engineering,
City University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong, in 2017.
He is currently a Professor with the Institute of Robotics and Automatic
Information System, and Tianjin Key Laboratory of Intelligent Robotics,
Nankai University. His research interests include planning and control of
autonomous robotics and mechatronic system with focus on time optimal
planning and visual servo control, intelligent perception including robot vision,
visual servo networks, simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM),
reinforcement learning, and game theory.
Dr. Zhang is a Technical Editor of IEEE/ASME TRANSACTIONS ON
MECHATRONICS and an Associate Editor of ASME Journal of Dynamic
Systems, Measurement, and Control.
