Physics-Informed Koopman Neural Operator for

Archit Krishna Kamath , Member, IEEE, Bing Yan , Member, IEEE, Peng Shi , Life Fellow, IEEE,
and Mir Feroskhan , Member, IEEE
Abstract—This paper introduces a Physics-Informed Koopman
Neural Operator (PI-KNO) for augmented dynamics visual
servoing of multirotors that integrates Koopman operator the-
ory with neural networks. The proposed method establishes a
structured learning framework that eﬀectively captures complex
system dynamics while embedding physics-based priors. Unlike
fully data-driven approaches, PI-KNO improves generalization
and minimizes reliance on extensive real-world training data
by employing a hybrid loss function that combines physics-
informed constraints with real-time observations. The learned
model is incorporated into a monotonically weighted nonlinear
model predictive control (NMPC) framework, ensuring precise
trajectory tracking while adhering to state and input constraints.
Experimental results demonstrate that PI-KNO reduces training
time by 16.81% and enhances tracking accuracy by 19.56%
compared to conventional Data-Driven Koopman Neural Opera-
tors (DD-KNO) and Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINN).
Additionally, under 70.83% uncertainty in camera parameters,
PI-KNO achieves 10.6% and 22.2% lower tracking errors than
PINN and DD-KNO, respectively. These ﬁndings underscore the
robustness and eﬃciency of the proposed approach for real-time
multirotor visual servoing applications.
Note to Practitioners—This work presents an implementation
oriented view of a learning based predictive controller for aerial
robots designed to operate within the limits of onboard com-
putation and sensing. The proposed PI-KNO approach combines
physical modeling and data driven learning to create an accurate
and stable dynamic predictor suitable for real time deployment.
The architecture is distributed between an NVIDIA Jetson and
a Pixhawk. The Jetson runs the vision front end, state estimator,
PI-KNO predictor, and NMPC optimizer. The estimator fuses
visual and inertial data to provide position, velocity, and attitude
states, while the NMPC computes high level motion commands
at about 30Hz using the PI-KNO rollout. The Pixhawk executes

I. INTRODUCTION
A
CHIEVING high precision control in robotic systems
remains a fundamental challenge across a wide range
of applications, including industrial automation, autonomous
aerial systems, and medical robotics [1]. Attaining this level of
precision requires a thorough understanding of the underlying
system dynamics, which are frequently nonlinear and complex
in nature [2], [3]. For instance, in the case of multirotor
platforms performing visual servoing tasks [4], the system
behavior is inﬂuenced by a combination of aerodynamic
forces, sensor uncertainties, and time varying environmental
conditions. Visual servoing refers to the use of visual feedback,
typically from onboard cameras, to guide the motion of a
robot in real time [5]. For multirotors, this involves estimating
the position and orientation of objects or features directly
from image data, which is often noisy, delayed, and sensitive
to environmental variations such as lighting, occlusions, and
motion blur [6]. These limitations can severely degrade the
accuracy of state estimation and, consequently, the quality
of control. As a result, visual servoing presents a uniquely
challenging control problem that demands models capable of
capturing complex interactions between ﬂight dynamics and
perceptual feedback. The motion of the multirotor is deter-
mined not only by its inherent ﬂight dynamics but also by the
quality of the visual feedback, which introduces complexity.

such

control
[7],
linear

and sliding mode control [10], often depend on simpliﬁed
assumptions. While eﬀective in structured environments,

these approaches typically fail to account for unmodeled
uncertainties and dynamic changes, leading to performance
degradation in unstructured or unpredictable scenarios [11].
Furthermore, ensuring robustness and stability under such
conditions remains a signiﬁcant obstacle, particularly when
some system states are only partially observable [12].
Nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) has emerged as
a powerful framework for managing system constraints and
optimizing control actions over a ﬁnite horizon [13]. Despite
its advantages, NMPC is sensitive to inaccuracies in system
modeling and real world uncertainties, which can lead to
increased computational demands. To preserve performance,
NMPC often requires frequent reoptimization and high ﬁdelity
models that necessitate solving complex nonlinear programs
at each control step. Eﬀorts to improve robustness, such as
the use of chance constraints, min-max optimization [14],
tube based methods [15], and adaptive learning mechanisms
[16], [17], typically add further computational overhead,
making real time deployment increasingly challenging.
Recent progress in machine learning, particularly in deep
learning and fuzzy logic [18], [19], has demonstrated strong
potential in modeling complex nonlinear dynamics [20], [21].
These methods capture system uncertainties and modeling
errors by learning directly from data, rather than relying
entirely on ﬁrst principles. Neural networks have been eﬀec-
tively used to approximate unknown system dynamics [22],
compensate for model discrepancies [23], and adapt to time
varying environments in real time [24], [25]. When used
in conjunction with NMPC, such models improve prediction
accuracy and reduce the need for explicit uncertainty handling,
thereby easing computational burdens. However, conventional
deep learning techniques often lack interpretability, require
large volumes of training data, and may exhibit poor general-
ization outside their training distribution. These issues present
signiﬁcant barriers to real time deployment in safety critical
robotic systems [26].
To address these limitations, physics-informed neural net-
works (PINNs) have been proposed as a hybrid learning
approach that incorporates prior physical knowledge into the
training process. This reduces data dependency and enforces
consistency with governing dynamics [27]. Recent studies
have demonstrated the integration of PINNs with NMPC
frameworks for controlling quadrotors [26], [28]. In particular,
[29] applied PINNs for UAV system estimation and demon-
strated their potential to generate physics-consistent models
suitable for model-based predictive control. However, despite
their promise, PINNs often face challenges related to training
ineﬃciency and hyperparameter sensitivity. These issues limit
their scalability to high-dimensional systems exhibiting rapidly
evolving dynamics [30].
Koopman neural operators (KNOs) provide a promising
alternative by utilizing Koopman operator theory, which trans-
forms nonlinear dynamics into a higher dimensional space
where their evolution becomes linear [31]. This approach
enables structured and data driven representations of system
dynamics, improving both interpretability and computational
tractability. KNOs have been applied in modeling ﬂuid ﬂows
[32], robotic systems [33], and control tasks. However,
conventional KNOs do not enforce physical consistency and
are fully reliant on data, making them vulnerable to inac-
curacies under sparse or noisy conditions. In light of these
challenges, this paper presents the following contributions:
1) This paper introduces PI-KNO, a structured learning
framework that integrates Koopman operator theory with
neural networks to model multirotor dynamics in visual
servoing. By incorporating physics informed constraints,
PI-KNO improves generalization and reduces reliance
on extensive real world data. Experiments demonstrate
a 16.81% reduction in training time and a 19.56%
improvement in tracking accuracy relative to data driven
KNO and PINN, resulting in improved eﬃciency and
robustness for multirotor control in real time.
2) The learned PI-KNO model is integrated into a monoton-
ically weighted NMPC framework. This ensures robust
trajectory tracking while enforcing state and input con-
straints. The adaptive weighting mechanism dynamically
adjusts the prediction horizon, which enhances closed
loop stability and reduces computational complexity.
3) The proposed framework is evaluated through exten-
sive simulation and hardware experiments that include
benchmarking, ablation, and cross method compar-
isons. In addition to physics-informed and data driven
baselines such as PINN [26] and data driven KNO
(DD-KNO) [31], the evaluation encompasses state of the
art learning based controller designs KNODE-MPC [34]
and SINDy+MPC [35], [36]. The results consistently
show that the proposed approach achieves the most accu-
rate trajectory alignment across all evaluated scenarios,
yielding a mean percentage soft dynamic-time wrapping
(%SDTW) improvement of approximately 14.7% over
SINDy+MPC, 24.2% over KNODE-MPC, 40.0% over
PINN, and 52.4% over DD-KNO. The framework also
achieves a 16.81% reduction in training time and a
19.56% improvement in tracking accuracy relative to
existing Koopman and PINN based formulations. Hard-
ware experiments on multirotor visual servoing tasks
conﬁrm that the method maintains stable and precise
tracking under up to 70.83% uncertainty in camera
calibration parameters, consistently outperforming all
learning based controllers in convergence speed, spatial
temporal coherence and robustness to uncertainties.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
Section II presents the mathematical preliminaries on Koop-
man operator theory and physics-informed learning. Section III
describes the nominal visual servoing multirotor dynamics and
the PI-KNO modeling process. The control strategy, based
on monotonically weighted NMPC, is detailed in Section IV
followed by the overall framework in Section V. Section VI
discusses the experimental results, and Section VII concludes
the paper.

B. Physics-Informed Learning for KNO
The training of the conventional KNO is currently based
on estimating Γ using (12) and w using (13), which heav-
ily relies on the availability of observation samples of the
system states and control inputs. This approach entirely dis-

commonly referred to as the nominal dynamics, which rep-

IV. CONTROL DESIGN
The motivation for employing NMPC to control the aug-
mented dynamics visual servoing multirotor is not only to
ensure system stability but also to enforce adherence to
prescribed input and state constraints. Input constraints in the
control design help mitigate the risk of actuator saturation,
while state constraints prevent issues such as target loss.
This is particularly important as the image pixel-related term,
denoted as r, is included among the states in ηADVS.
However, conventional NMPC formulations often demand
signiﬁcant computational resources, making them impracti-
cal for real-time implementation on multirotors with limited
onboard computing capabilities, especially when accounting
for parametric uncertainties and modeling inaccuracies. The
proposed PI-KNO model addresses these challenges by pro-
viding a reliable estimate that eﬀectively compensates for
uncertainties and inaccuracies, improving the model’s pre-
dictive accuracy. Nevertheless, the challenge of incorporating
terminal constraints within the NMPC formulation to guar-
antee stability persists, still contributing to the computational
burden. A solution for this is to formulate a monotonically
weighted NMPC based on the PI-KNO dynamics as follows:

A. Training and Benchmarking Studies
The training of the PI-KNO, along with the benchmarking

and 432 Tensor cores, enabling mixed-precision training with
FP16 and TF32 to enhance computational eﬃciency. Its high
memory bandwidth of 1.6 TB/s facilitates the eﬃcient pro-

performance during both training and benchmarking.

VII. CONCLUSION
This paper presented a Physics-Informed Koopman Neural
Operator (PI-KNO) for augmented dynamics visual servo-
ing of multirotors. By integrating Koopman operator theory
with physics-informed neural learning, the framework pro-
vides a structured and data-eﬃcient means to model complex
nonlinear dynamics while retaining physical interpretability.
The learned Koopman representation is embedded within
a monotonically weighted NMPC scheme that adaptively
adjusts the prediction horizon and enforces input and state
constraints for stable real-time trajectory tracking. Extensive
simulation and hardware experiments benchmarked the pro-
posed approach against DD-KNO, PINN, KNODE-MPC [34],
and SINDy+MPC [35], [36]. Across circular, cardioid, and
lemniscate trajectories, PI-KNO achieved the lowest %SDTW,
outperforming SINDy+MPC by 14.7%, KNODE-MPC by
24.2%, PINN by 40.0%, and DD-KNO by 52.4%, with a
16.81% reduction in training time and a 19.56% improvement
in tracking accuracy. Real-world experiments on a Jetson-
Pixhawk platform conﬁrmed robust performance, maintaining
accurate trajectory alignment even under 70.83% uncertainty
in camera calibration. These results indicate that physics
embedding in PI-KNO improves convergence robustness by
reducing error propagation compared to data-driven and sparse
models. Future work will investigate online adaptive Koopman
learning and cooperative multi-agent control using shared
representations.
