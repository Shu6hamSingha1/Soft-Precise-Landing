Autonomous Flight Control of a Nano Quadrotor
Helicopter in a GPS-Denied Environment
Using On-Board Vision
Xu Zhang, Bin Xian, Senior Member, IEEE, Bo Zhao, and Yao Zhang
Abstract—In this paper, a nano quadrotor helicopter
which weighs about only 45 grams and has a diameter
less than 0.15 m is employed for the autonomous ﬂight
control development in GPS-denied environments. Due to
the very limited payload ability of the helicopter, a micro
onboard vision system is designed to provide visual pose
measurement. Then the attitude data obtained from a low
cost micro inertial measurement unit (IMU) are fused with
the visual measurement to provide accurate estimation of
the helicopter’s translational position and velocity. Non-
linear ﬂight controller is designed to hold the quadrotor
in a certain position and altitude without external ground
motion capture system. Moreover, trajectory tracking ability
is achieved with the help of a modiﬁed visual simultaneous
localization and mapping (SLAM) algorithm. Experimental
results are included to demonstrate the good hovering and
tracking control performance of the proposed design in
GPS-denied indoor and outdoor environments. To our best
knowledge, few of previous works have demonstrated au-
tonomous control ability of a quadrotor helicopter weighs
less than 50 grams without the help of external ground
motion capture system in GPS-denied indoor and outdoor
environments.
Index Terms—Nano quadrotor, robust control, UAV, vi-
sual control.

navigation system (INS) sensors can now be integrated into a
tiny chip weighing only several milligrams. Based on the above

them have been performed on quadrotor UAVs that typically
weigh more than 500 grams and few studies have been done
for the control of nano quadrotor UAVs which have a mass less
than 50 grams. In this paper, our interest is the stable hovering
and trajectory tracking control design for a nano quadrotor UAV
which has a net weight about 45 grams with a payload ability

The limited payload ability and size of the nano quadrotor
UAV bring great challenges to the ﬂight control development.

vices are not suitable for the nano quadrotor UAV, such as
the Global Position System (GPS) unit and laser range ﬁnders.

In the early approaches to the localization and navigation
problems in GPS denied environments, many researchers have
taken advantage of optical ﬂow based control methods [8]. This
biomimetic method does not need a great number of computa-
tions or artiﬁcial land marks placed in the environment [9]. In

make onboard optical ﬂow calculation possible for practical
ﬂight control. In [10], an open source high speed optical ﬂow

which is relatively tiny comparing to regular size quadrotor, but
it is still too heavy to be mounted on our 45 grams quadrotor
as shown in Fig. 1. The major drawback of optical ﬂow based
approach is that the integration of the translational velocity will
lead to an unacceptable position estimation drift.
Compared to the vision-based algorithms mentioned above,
visual simultaneous localization and mapping (VSLAM) is a
more promising solution to GPS-denied navigation [11], [12].
This VSLAM approach can provide almost drift-free pose
estimation in an unknown environment without the aid of any
external localization system, beacons or artiﬁcial features.
The quadrotor helicopter is a typical underactuated non-
linear system. Recently, the control design for underactuated
mechatronic system has drawn great attention. In [13], an
energy-based approach was employed to design a regulation
controller for an underactuated overhead crane system. In [14],
the dynamics of an underactuated offshore boom crane was
analyzed and a nonlinear tracking controller was proposed.
In [15], robust compensator approaches were combined with
linear feedback control design to formulate robust regulation
and tracking controller for a class of underactuated laboratory
helicopter setup.
Our nano quadrotor helicopter is equipped with a micro
wireless monocular camera, a low-cost IMU, a micro ﬂight
control board as the main avionic devices. The images are
transferred from the onboard camera to the ground station
through wireless communication, and estimation for the pose
of the helicopter is obtained via a modiﬁed visual SLAM
algorithm. To improve the accuracy of position estimation, the
IMU data are fused with the raw visual measurement using a
Kalman ﬁlter [16]. To increase the robustness with respect to
external disturbance and modeling uncertainties, a nonlinear
robust feedback methodology is used to formulate the ﬂight
controller. Real-time ﬂight tests have been carried out to verify
the hovering and tracking control performance of the proposed
design in GPS-denied indoor and outdoor environments. To
our best knowledge, few of previous works have reported the
autonomous control ability of a nano UAV which weighs less
than 50 grams in GPS-denied indoor and outdoor environments,
and does not require any help from an external ground motion
capture system.
This paper is organized as follows. The overview of our
nano quadrotor helicopter setup and control development is
presented in Section II. The motion estimation based on the
onboard vision system is presented in Section III. Experimental
results for the validation of the proposed design are given in
Section IV. Finally, some conclusion remarks are presented in
Section V.
II. FLIGHT CONTROL DEVELOPMENT
In this section, the position and attitude control algorithm
is designed for the nano quadrotor helicopter. Inspired by
the work in [17], an inner-loop-outer-loop control structure is
utilized for the ﬂight control development. Based on the rigid
body dynamic model of the nano quadrotor, a PID controller is
designed for the inner-loop (or the attitude loop) control, and a
robust nonlinear position controller is developed for the outer-
loop (or the position loop) control. A linear attitude dynamics
model is obtained via system identiﬁcation technique to provide
a base for the numerical simulation and control gains tuning.
A. Nano Quadrotor Helicopter Setup
For our research work, a self-built nano quadrotor helicopter
is used. Fig. 1 shows the setup of the vehicle. Its diameter
is 0.15 m and weighs about 45 grams. The maximum ﬂight
endurance is approximately 8 minutes with a 3.7 V 500 mAh
Li-Po battery which weighs 10 grams. Based on its lightweight
carbon ﬁber body frame and efﬁcient propelling system, sufﬁ-
cient payload capacity is obtained for onboard avionic devices.
Despite its tiny size, this vehicle contains a full suite of onboard
avionic devices. A customized 32-bits microcontroller board
which is integrated with low cost IMU is used as the onboard
autopilot board. The onboard low cost IMU consists of linear
acceleration, angular velocity and magnetic sensors. A wireless
monocular video camera which weighs about 3 grams is se-
lected as the onboard vision sensor. A telemetry radio device
which weighs about 1.9 grams is utilized to provide communi-
cation between the quadrotor helicopter and the ground station
(Table I).
The host-based system conﬁguration of our testbed is illus-
trated in Fig. 2. The attitude data of the quadrotor helicopter is
measured by the onboard IMU, the altitude of the quadrotor is
measured via a micro onboard barometer. The micro wireless
camera points directly down and transmits video information
to a laptop computer which is employed as the ground station

C. Design of the Controller
In this paper, the system output is selected as the nano
helicopter’s position and yaw angle, denoted by η(t) =
[x(t) y(t) z(t) ψ(t)]T , and the control objective is to let the

by ηd(t) = [xd(t) yd(t) zd(t) ψd(t)]T . The translational posi-

D. System Identiﬁcation
Due to the light structure and small body size, the ﬂight
properties associated with the low Reynolds number will bring
strong uncertainties to the nano quadrotor’s dynamic model. It
is very difﬁcult to obtain accurate values for the dynamic sys-
tem parameters in (5). For the consideration of the experimental
implementation, a linear attitude dynamic model of the nano
quadrotor helicopter is obtained via the system identiﬁcation
method which is based on the least square parameter estimation
algorithm in [27].
It is reasonable to assume that the dynamics for the roll
channel, pitch channel, and the yaw channel are some single-
input-and-single-output systems (SISO) [28]. Also, due to the
symmetric properties of the quadrotor’s structure, the roll chan-
nel and the pitch channel have nearly the same dynamics, i.e.,
the same dynamic model can be used to denote the dynamics
for these two channels.
The data-collection experiments are performed by the human
pilot in PID controller based closed loop. To an inherently un-
stable system like the nano quadrotor, closed-loop experiment
is the best choice, since the designed PID controller is known,
the closed-loop identiﬁcation result can be transferred to open-
loop identiﬁcation result [27]. The human pilot controlled the
quadrotor helicopter’s motion in the roll, pitch, and yaw sepa-
rately, and the ﬂight data is recorded by the ground station for
analyzing the dynamics. The quadrotor’s attitude response is
recorded at a frequency of 50 Hz.
Using normalized inputs from pilot as model input, and
actual attitude angles as model output, the Black-box transfer
function models are chosen to represent the SISO roll chan-
nel, pitch channel, and yaw channel dynamics. By employing
the least square parameter estimation algorithm, the identiﬁed
transfer function for the pitch and roll dynamics are obtained as
follows:

is proved to be with high robustness. However, in most cases,
tracking landmarks cannot be maintained for a long time, and
then the optical ﬂow tracker could not provide drift-free posi-
tion estimation, thus long duration on-spot hovering was very
difﬁcult to achieve with only optical ﬂow based methodology.
To eliminate drift in the ﬂight control, the visual frame-
work based on Parallel Tracking and Mapping (PTAM) [30]
is employed in this paper, this approach is highly capable
of tracking and mapping from a single camera, and its long-
term performance has been proved in [12]. Being divided into
tracking and mapping threads, the PTAM algorithm can obtain
real-time performances and 3-D maps of the environment. The
tracking thread estimates the pose of the nano helicopter based
on tracking and registering features, the parallel mapping thread
ﬁnds 3-D points of the world, builds and maintains the map.
Both tracking and mapping threads require an existing map to
operate on, so an initialization procedure is required to generate
an initial map. These three components of PTAM algorithm is
illustrated in Fig. 6.
The mainly drawback of the PTAM algorithm lies in the
ineffectiveness when it works in a self-similar scenario, it can
be easily confused by repetitive stripes or patterns. Although
this drawback can be compensated to some extent by using
wide-angle lens, the PTAM algorithm still might be lost in
tracking due to the fact that ground tiles often ﬁll all the ﬁeld of
view in the case that the UAV is ﬂying in low altitude.
The VSLAM framework in Robot Operating System pro-
vided by ETH [29] is chosen as our starting point, and several
major modiﬁcations are made to the VSLAM software for the
ﬂight control of our nano quadrotor helicopter. These modiﬁca-
tions are listed as follows.
(1) The VSLAM framework alone cannot provide the mag-
nitude or absolute scale of translational motion. In the
original software, manually initialization is needed [30],
and this initialization step is kind of tricky and inac-
curate. In order to transfer proper position information
to the ﬁlter for data fusion, we use the data from
the onboard barometer sensor to trim the initial scale
estimation according to the absolute measurement of
the helicopter’s altitude. This revision brings automatic
initialization for the VSLAM procedure.

using the stored sensor measurements. By recalculating the last
cycles of the Kalman ﬁlter, delay compensated measurements
can be obtained for real-time control.

V. CONCLUSION
In this paper, a nano quadrotor helicopter which has a mass
about only 45 grams was developed. Given the limited payload
ability, the tiny body size, and the vulnerability to external dis-
turbance, the control design for this kind of helicopter is a very
difﬁcult one. By using the modiﬁed visual SLAM algorithm
with the visual data from an onboard monocular camera, ac-
curate hovering and trajectory tracking control are achieved in
GPS-denied indoor and outdoor environments without the help
of an external ground motion capture system, such as a VICON
system. The PID control approach is combined with a nonlinear
robust feedback design to formulate the ﬂight controller for
the nano quadrotor helicopter. Experimental results in different
scenarios show that the proposed design has achieved good
hovering and trajectory tracking control performance. Future
work will be extended to autonomous ﬂight control in a more
conﬁned non-constructed environment.
