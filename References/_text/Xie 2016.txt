Input Saturated Visual Servoing for Unmanned
Aerial Vehicles
Hui Xie and Alan F. Lynch, Member, IEEE
Abstract—This paper presents an input saturated visual
servoing controller for a quadrotor unmanned aerial vehicle
(UAV). The controller regulates relative pose between the
vehicle and a planar horizontal visual target. In order to sim-
plify the control design, the method uses a virtual camera to
deﬁne a set of image moment features. The image feature
kinematics is independent of roll and pitch motion. Since
the image feature error is stabilized in the virtual camera,
the visual target potentially leaves the real camera’s ﬁeld
of view (FoV). To keep the visual target in the camera FoV,
an input saturated law is proposed to sufﬁciently bound
the roll and pitch of the vehicle. Furthermore, an adaptive
input saturated control law is proposed to ensure positive
thrust. Experimental and simulation results are provided to
demonstrate controller performance.

I. INTRODUCTION
V
ISUAL servoing of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) in-
volves using feedback from computer vision to perform
motion control. Visual servoing techniques allow a UAV to ﬂy
in GPS-denied environments [1] or can be applied to control rel-
ative position to a visual target as in autonomous landing [2]. In
this paper, we consider visual servoing of a quadrotor UAV with
a single downward facing camera and the proposed visual ser-
voing law regulates the quadrotor’s relative pose to a stationary
planar horizontal target. Visual servoing laws usually are cate-
gorized into two approaches: 1) position-based visual servoing
(PBVS); and 2) image-based visual servoing (IBVS) [3]. In this
paper, we focus on IBVS methods where the error signal to be
stabilized is directly deﬁned in the image plane. Unlike PBVS,
IBVS avoids the need for pose reconstruction which requires
accurate camera calibration and a prior three-dimensional (3-D)
model of the target. In general, IBVS is simple to implement
and is relatively insensitive to camera calibration [4].
Previous work on IBVS for UAVs can be classiﬁed into four
categories: 1) the spherical image moment-based design [5],

A summary of these approaches can be found in our previous
work [15]. In this paper, we follow [11]–[14] and employ a
virtual camera that has the same position and yaw as the real
camera but with zero roll and pitch. The image features for
visual servoing are deﬁned in the virtual camera image plane.
Hence, a transformation is required to convert the image fea-
tures in the real camera image plane into the image feature in the
virtual camera plane. The expression for this transformation can
be derived from geometry as mentioned in [12] and [13], or by
solving a system of ﬁrst-order homogeneous partial differential
equations [15]. The transformation depends on an attitude mea-
surement that is commonly available from an onboard attitude
and heading reference system (AHRS). Since the virtual camera
has zero roll and pitch motion, the image feature kinematics is
independent of roll and pitch rates. This independence is crucial
for simplifying the image feature kinematics and facilitating the
control design.
Although the image feature error deﬁned in the virtual camera
image can be stabilized, there is no guarantee that the visual tar-
get remains in the real camera’s ﬁeld of view (FoV). Hence, the
control gains in our previous work [13], [15], [16] were chosen
small to avoid aggressive motion that makes the visual target
leave the camera FoV. The problem of keeping the visual target
in the camera FoV has been considered in the literature. In [17],
an output constraint method from [18] is used for landing a UAV
with parallel line image features. An alternative approach is to
constrain the vehicle’s attitude (e.g., [19] and [20]). Although
this approach has no rigorous proof that the visual targets re-
main in the camera FoV, it is simpler to implement relative to
[17]. In this paper, we propose a new control law based on an
input constraint method.
The proposed approach uses an inner–outer loop control
structure. The inner loop tracks an attitude reference from the
outer IBVS loop that regulates the image feature error in the
virtual camera image. We assume the inner loop has been ap-
propriately designed and focus on the outer loop IBVS design.
The main contribution of this paper is a new nested saturation
control for the outer loop to ensure the roll, pitch, and thrust
inputs lie within speciﬁed bounds. Bounds on roll and pitch
keep the visual target in the camera FoV, and positive thrust is a
constraint for typical quadrotors. Compared with the commonly
used nested saturation approach mentioned in [21] and its vari-
ations such as [22] and [23], the proposed method beneﬁts from
being able to use less conservative (i.e., lower) input bounds

V. CONCLUSION
An input saturation visual servoing law is proposed to con-
trol the height, lateral, and yaw motion of a quadrotor UAV.
The control law is proven to asymptotically stabilize the closed-
loop. The control law for the height subsystem restricts thrust
to a predeﬁned compact set. The positive thrust is guaranteed
by setting a positive lower bound for the compact set. The con-
trol law for the lateral subsystem bounds the reference roll and
pitch to a compact set. This avoids large roll and pitch when the
image feature error is large, and this helps to keep the visual
target in the camera FoV. The saturation controller proposed
in this paper provides high gain when the image feature error
approaches zero and therefore reduces the steady-state error of
the image feature. Both simulation and experimental work val-
idate the performance of the proposed input saturation control
on the ANCL quadrotor test stand. Future work includes gen-
eralizing the method to moving and nonhorizontal targets. We
plan to develop the theory to explicitly account for attitude bias
input disturbances to the lateral subsystem. Improvements to
the onboard vision computer are planned which should improve
the performance of the outer loop. For example, reduced image
processing delay leads to improved outer loop bandwidth.
