Adaptive Extended Disturbance
Observer-Based Control for Quadrotor Landing
on Fast Platform
Yufei Zhang
and Zhong Wu
, Member, IEEE
Abstract—In order to land quadrotor uncrewed aerial
vehicles precisely and reliably on fast platforms, complex
aerodynamic disturbances can be estimated by disturbance
observers with proper bandwidth and then compensated by
feedforward. However, the observer bandwidth is difﬁcult to
design since both aerodynamic disturbances and measure-
ment noises vary signiﬁcantly in the landing process. In this
article, an adaptive extended disturbance observer-based
control scheme is proposed to improve disturbance esti-
mation accuracy and suppress aerodynamic disturbances
in the landing of quadrotors. First, the time-varying proper-
ties of aerodynamic disturbances and measurement noises
are analyzed in the landing process. Then, two adaptive
extended disturbance observers (AEDOs) are designed for
translational and rotational subsystems, respectively with
their bandwidths adaptively tuned according to the power
ratio of disturbances to measurement noises. Theoretical
analysis indicates AEDO has higher accuracy in distur-
bance estimation as well as robustness to measurement
noises compared with other EDOs. Finally, block backstep-
ping controllers are designed for the subsystems with the
outputs of the AEDOs as feedforward compensation. Land-
ing experiments on a platform of 8.3 m/s demonstrate the ef-
fectiveness of the proposed scheme in tuning the observer
bandwidth adaptively according to disturbance and noise
information and improving the precision of landing points.
Index Terms—Adaptive observer, autonomous landing,
disturbance observer, quadrotor, uncrewed aerial vehicles
(UAVs).

To suppress aerodynamic disturbances in the entire landing
process, they can be estimated by disturbance observers ﬁrst
and then compensated by feedforward. In this strategy, the key
to disturbance suppression is accurate estimation [11], [12],
[13], [14], [15]. Although both disturbance observer (DO) [16],
[17], [18], [19] and extended state observer (ESO) [20], [21]
have been employed to estimate aerodynamic disturbances in
the landing on moving platforms, they are designed based on
the assumption of slowly-varying disturbances. Therefore, it is
difﬁcult for them to accurately estimate complex aerodynamic
disturbances in the landing on fast platforms.
To accurately estimate complex aerodynamic disturbances,
extended disturbance observer (EDO) and noise reduction ex-
tended disturbance observer (NREDO) can be utilized [22],
[23]. Although NREDO has better noise suppression perfor-
mance compared with EDO, both of the observers typically
use ﬁxed bandwidth to tune gains. To ensure accurate esti-
mation, the bandwidth design should consider the effects of
aerodynamic disturbances and measurement noises in a bal-
anced manner. However, aerodynamic disturbances vary with
the relative height between the propellers and platform when
the quadrotor is close to the platform. In fact, the measurement
noises from the measurement of the relative distance between
the quadrotor and platform varies with the relative distance
due to the resolution limitations of the vision camera. There-
fore, it is difﬁcult to ensure accurate estimation with ﬁxed
bandwidth in the entire process of quadrotors landing on fast
platforms.
For disturbance estimation in the presence of time-varying
disturbances and measurement noises, many studies have been
reported in other ﬁelds. For example, Kalman ﬁlter [24] and
α–β–γ ﬁlter [25] have been used in object tracking to cope with
time-varying disturbances and measurement noises, and their
bandwidths are tuned automatically according to the statistical
characteristics of disturbances and the noises. It should be noted
that their estimation accuracy relies on the Gaussian distribution
assumption of disturbances and noises. However, aerodynamic
disturbances do not satisfy the assumption.
In summary, it remains a critical issue to suppress com-
plex aerodynamic disturbances for quadrotor UAVs landing
on fast platforms. The motivation of this article is to han-
dle the variation of disturbances and measurement noises
so as to improve estimation accuracy and suppress distur-
bances in the landing scenario. Inspired by the idea of adap-
tive bandwidth, an adaptive EDO (AEDO) based control
scheme is proposed in the landing of quadrotors on fast
platforms.
The main contributions of this article are listed as follows.
1) According to the characteristics of aerodynamic dis-
turbances and measurement noises in the landing pro-
cess, two AEDOs are designed for the translational
and rotational subsystems of a quadrotor to estimate
force and torque disturbances. With the bandwidth tuned
automatically based on the power ratio of disturbances

a sensor fusion system to measure its own pose, but also a
camera positioning system to measure the platform motion [31].
As the measurement noises in both systems affect the landing
performance, their characteristics should be analyzed.
Deﬁne the heading of the platform and quadrotor as β and
ψ, and denote the acceleration and angular acceleration of the

D. Problem Statement
The aforementioned analysis indicates that the intensity of
aerodynamic disturbances increases and that of measurement
noises decreases when the quadrotor descends. Moreover, the
dynamics of aerodynamic disturbances become faster in the
landing on fast platforms. In this case, it is difﬁcult for dis-
turbance observers to ensure accurate estimation with ﬁxed
bandwidth in the entire landing process. As a result, complex
aerodynamic disturbances will reduce the precision of landing
points and make the landing unreliable. Inspired by the charac-
teristics of aerodynamic disturbances and measurement noises
in the landing scenario, it is necessary to design disturbance
observers with variable bandwidths to adaptively handle the
changes in disturbances and measurement noises and design
controllers with the outputs of the observers as feedforward
compensation to suppress complex aerodynamic disturbances
in the landing scenario.

be designed for the translational and rotational subsystems,
respectively to estimate force and torque disturbances, and their
performance will be analyzed.

landing experiments on a fast platform were conducted on our
self-assembled quadrotor. The landing platform is shown in

on the safe landing zone with the maximum error of landing
points less than 8.5 cm. Moreover, the vision system should
have sufﬁcient bandwidth to ensure continuous tracking of the
landing platform.

procedures are arranged. In the preparatory stage, the platform
travels at 8.3 m/s and the quadrotor ﬂies with the platform

quadrotor descends vertically at an expected speed of 0.4 m/s.

VI. CONCLUSION
This article proposes AEDO to estimate complex aerody-
namic disturbances when quadrotor UAVs land on fast plat-
forms. Two AEDOs are designed to estimate force and torque
disturbances for translational and rotational channels of a
quadrotor UAV. In AEDO, the bandwidth is designed to be
tuned adaptively according to the power ratio between distur-
bances and measurement noises. The comparison with other
EDOs shows that AEDO exhibits higher estimation accuracy
and robustness to measurement noises. Repeated experiments
on a 30 km/h platform have validated the effectiveness of
the proposed strategy. In the experiments, the quadrotor UAV
consistently lands on the safe landing zone with an error of
less than 6.3 cm. As the increased computational complexity
of the AEDO may affect its application in low-cost quadrotor
UAVs, future work will focus on the reduction of the algorithm
complexity.
