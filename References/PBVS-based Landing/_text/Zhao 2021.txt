Abstract 
In this paper, a robust visual servoing control approach is proposed to address the landing problem for 
quadrotors on a moving platform. A vision system is implemented to estimate the position and velocity of 
the quadrotor. A robust cascade controller is proposed by following backstepping-like fundamentals and 
robust compensating theory. The effects of time-varying uncertainties, including parameter uncertainties 
and external disturbances, and time-varying delays resulted from image acquisition, image processing, 
and sensor measurement delays can be restrained. Experimental results using a quadrotor to land on a 
ground moving target illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach.

1. Introduction 
Quadrotors have attracted increased interest due to their capabilities of vertical take-off 
and landing with a simple structure, and thereby offer advantages when used for routine 
surveillance, environmental exploration, and target tracking (see, [1–3] ), to mention but a few 
applications. Accurate measurements of the unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) states is an open 
issue on designing controllers. Typical measurement sensors include GPS, Inertial Measure- 
ment Unit (IMU), and vision systems. GPS has limitations as it does not operate efﬁciently

indoors, nor in cluttered urban areas. An IMU is commonly used to provide UAV attitude 
and angular velocity data. However, such sensor systems are also shown to be unreliable to 
provide quadrotor position and velocity data over long periods of time, because of open-loop 
integration errors; this limitation is crucial during the vehicle landing process. An alternative 
way is to integrate a vision system along with common sensors such as IMU sensors (see, 
e.g. , [4–6] . The resulting vision system can provide accurate estimation of quadrotor position 
and attitude and it is also developed by using low-cost lightweight hardware (due to pay- 
load limitation). Therefore, it is advantageous on missions such as obstacle avoidance, target 
tracking, and autonomous landing. 
Visual servoing regulates the pose of the UAV with respect to a target, using image features 
extracted from cameras. Visual servoing may be classiﬁed into two branches: position-based 
visual servoing (PBVS) as illustrated in [7–9] , and image-based visual servoing (IBVS) in 
[10,11] . PBVS constructs 3D information from 2D image data, then, the constructed 3D 
information is used in the control law design. The IBVS directly uses image features to 
design the control law. The PBVS approach has the advantage of separating the vision-based 
quadrotor state estimation from the quadrotor control law design. But, this approach often 
depends on knowledge of the geometry of the target; thus, it is computationally expensive in 
reconstructing the 3D information. Furthermore, In practical landing process of the quadrotor 
on a ground target using a monocular camera, the ground target in the image is either small 
in the beginning or partially visible in the end of the landing task. Therefore the geometry 
of the ground target is not always detectable in the whole landing process of the quadrotor 
using a monocular camera and thus the typical PBVS methods in [7–9] are not suitable 
in the landing on a ground moving target task. In contrast, the IBVS approach is robust 
against camera calibration errors, and it is computationally efﬁcient. But the IBVS approach 
introduces highly nonlinear and coupled image dynamics, and thereby complicates quadrotor 
controller design. Besides, the IBVS approach cannot operate in complex environments as 
shown in [12] . 
When designing a visual servoing controller for a quadrotor, three challenges should be 
addressed and overcome: quadrotor highly nonlinear and coupled dynamics; quadrotor param- 
eter uncertainties and external disturbances (for example, the wind disturbances) related to the 
translational and rotational dynamics; state and input time delays due to image acquisition and 
processing [13] , measurement delays from sensors, and the controller operating rate. In fact, 
the state and input time delays, especially the time delays resulted from the image acquisition 
and processing, are non-ignorable in the visual servoing controller design. It should be noted 
that the acquisition and processing of images are computationally expensive and the compu- 
tational resource of the quadrotor embedded computer is limited, which is the main source 
of the state time delays in the quadrotor dynamics. The state time delays are unavoidable in 
practical implementations and can result in the failure of ﬂight mission for quadrotors using 
the traditional control strategies, as illustrated in [14] . 
To tackle these challenges, several control approaches were developed following the PBVS 
approach. In [8] and [15] , vision-based control methods including feedback linearization, 
backstepping, sliding mode, and nested saturation structure were studied to restrain nonlinear 
and coupled dynamics. In [16] , the visual servoing control of quadrotors and the tracking of 
moving targets was achieved in a simulation. However, a simple PD controller was designed 
to achieve the motion of the quadrotor and the uncertainties and time delays in the quadrotor 
dynamics were not discussed in [16] . In [9] , the time delay was assumed to be a measurable 
constant and a RISE controller was designed to restrain nonlinearities and uncertainties in

In [18] , the uncertainties including parameter uncertainties, nonlinear and coupling dynamics,

the landing of a quadrotor on a ground target using the robust visual servoing controller 
without knowledge of the position information of the quadrotor or the ground target in the 
earth-ﬁxed frame was not discussed in [17–20] . 
In addition, nonlinear controllers were developed following the IBVS approach. In [21] and 
[22] , integral backstepping was applied in the design of the visual servoing controller to limit 
nonlinearities impact in the control system. In [23] , a nonlinear controller was developed to 
tackle time-varying disturbances in the quadrotor dynamics. A robust visual servoing control 
method was studied in the path-planning task and the uncertainties in the image model were 
considered in [24] . In [25] , the adaptive backstepping method was followed where quadro- 
tor uncertainties were treated as unknown system parameters. In [26] , an adaptive control 
scheme was developed via using dynamic surface control and backstepping to limit external 
disturbances in the translational and rotational dynamics of the quadrotor. However, the un- 
certainties impact and time delays on the closed-loop control system was not fully analyzed 
in [8,9,15,21–26] . In [27] , the time delay of image acquisition and image processing was 
modeled as a positive constant in the translational dynamics. Then, a nonlinear controller 
developed in [28] was provided in [27] to compensate for time delay and external distur- 
bances; the effectiveness of the developed controller was demonstrated in simulations, and 
the nonlinearities and parameter uncertainties were not further discussed in [27] . In [29] , the 
ground target tracking was achieved for quadrotors under the effects of nonlinearities, cou- 
plings, and uncertainties. However, the impacts of time-varying delays resulted from image 
acquisition, image processing, and sensor measurement delays on the quadrotor dynamics 
were not discussed and the landing task of quadrotors on a moving ground target was not 
further investigated in [29] . 
Although multiple studies have been published on visual servoing control of UAVs, to 
the best of the authors’ knowledge, quadrotor robust visual servoing control subject to mul- 
tiple uncertainties and time delays is still an open issue. This paper proposes and applies 
to a quadrotor a robust visual servoing controller to solve the landing maneuver problem 
under uncertainties and time delays in the translational and rotational dynamics, based on a 
backstepping-like method and the robust compensation theory. Uncertainties include parameter 
uncertainties and external disturbances. Time delays are mainly related to image acquisition, 
image processing, the controller operating rate, and sensor measurement delays. In the current 
paper, image features are fused with the IMU sensor data and the sonar sensor to provide 
quadrotor position and velocity information with respect to the ground target. Similarly to [6] , 
the sonar sensor is used in our proposed controller to estimate the image depth of the ground

Compared to previous research on the same topic, obtained results include: (i) A vision- 
based estimation system is developed for the quadrotors to land on the moving target. The 
estimation procedure is simple and the prior knowledge of the geometry of the target is not re- 
quired compared to the traditional PBVS control methods as shown in [8,9,15] . The proposed 
estimation system avoids introducing highly nonlinear image dynamics for the IBVS control 
method as depicted in [21–23,25–28] . (ii) Nonlinear and coupled terms are fully included in 
the dynamics of the quadrotor, as opposed to reported research in [9,25,26] , where quadrotor 
models were simpliﬁed to facilitate the design process of the visual servoing controllers. (iii) 
Parameter uncertainties, external disturbances, and time delays in the translational and rota- 
tional quadrotor dynamic are considered in the robust visual servoing controller design process. 
The robustness properties of the proposed robust visual servoing controller for quadrotor un- 
der uncertainties and time delays are analyzed and practical landing experimental test of the 
quadrotor is provided. Note that the impact of uncertainties including parameter uncertainties 
and external disturbances on quadrotor dynamics was not fully analyzed in [8,15,21,22] and 
the time delays on the vision-based control system were ignored in [8,9,15,21–23,25,26,29] .

design process of the robust cascade control system is depicted, and the robustness properties 
of the proposed controller are analyzed. Experimental results are given in Section 5 and 
conclusions are drawn in Section 6 .

5. Experiment results 
To validate the effectiveness of the proposed robust visual servoing controller, a Tarot 650 
unmanned quadrotor is constructed. An open-source Pixhawk autopilot is applied to control 
the quadrotor. The proposed robust visual servoing control algorithm is implemented in the 
Pixhawk to achieve the desired ﬂight performance. An open-source ground station software 
named Mission Planner is applied to monitor the quadrotor and generate the control com- 
mand for the quadrotor. To improve the identiﬁcation capability of the vision system, the 
ground target is marked with infrared markers. The onboard sensors of the Pixhawk hardware 
include a 3-D accelerometer, a 3-D gyroscope, a 3-D magnetometer and a pressure sensor. 
The attitude of the quadrotor is estimated by fusing the data of the IMU and magnetome- 
ter sensor. The onboard sensors include a vision sensor named CMUcam5 Pixy and a sonar 
sensor. The camera of CMUcam5 Pixy and the sonar sensor are ﬁxed below the quadrotor, 
facing downward. The focus length of the camera lens is 3 . 6 × 10 −3 m and the horizontal 
and vertical ﬁeld of views of the camera is approximately 60 degrees and 35 degrees. The

Case 2: Landing on a Ground Moving Target 
An outdoor ground moving target landing task for the quadrotor is achieved to illustrate 
the behavior of the closed-loop control system using the proposed control algorithm. The 
quadrotor in the landing task is required to track the center of the ground target in the 
horizontal direction and approach to the ground target in the vertical direction. To guarantee 
the safety of the quadrotor control system in practical landing task, the vertical landing velocity 
of the quadrotor is limited. The ground target is manually controlled with the velocity being

6. Conclusion 
This paper proposes a robust visual servoing control method for quadrotors to land on 
a moving target, subject to model nonlinearities, coupling, external disturbances, parameter 
uncertainties, and time delays. The quadrotor position and velocity with respect to the ground 
target is obtained by using a combination of vision-based sensors. The proof of convergence 
of the control system is provided. The proposed robust visual servoing controller is compared 
with a standard visual servoing controller and the obtained experimental results illustrate the 
advantages of the proposed robust visual servoing algorithm.
